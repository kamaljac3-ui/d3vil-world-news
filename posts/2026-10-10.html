Americas

Magnitude-7.7 earthquake shakes Panama, triggers tsunami warnings across the region

A magnitude-7.7 earthquake struck southern Panama on Friday, centered roughly 200 kilometers from the capital near the rural community of Pitaloza Arriba, followed hours later by a 6.6 aftershock, authorities said. The quake collapsed a cathedral tower and part of a hotel facade in Penonomé and damaged a government building, injuring several people with debris, while the U.S. National Weather Service issued tsunami warnings for Panama, Colombia, Ecuador, Nicaragua and Guatemala that were later lifted without a destructive wave. President José Raúl Mulino said the capital escaped damage and that Panama was not yet seeking international assistance.

Sources: The Washington Times (AP), Al Jazeera

US prosecutors add torture-conspiracy charge against Maduro and his wife

Federal prosecutors in Manhattan filed a new charge accusing former Venezuelan president Nicolás Maduro and his wife, Cilia Flores, of conspiring to torture political opponents and detainees, including more than ten Americans held in Venezuela during his rule. The indictment adds to the narcoterrorism and drug-trafficking charges filed after Maduro's capture earlier this year, and prosecutors say he personally ordered abuse that included electric shocks, beatings and simulated drowning. Maduro's lawyer says the couple have diplomatic immunity and intend to contest the case; a trial is scheduled for June 2027.

Sources: NBC News (AP), CBS News

Europe

Russian strike on Zaporizhzhia kills at least eight, including children

A Russian strike hit a residential area of Zaporizhzhia early Saturday, killing at least eight people, including three children, and wounding about ten others, Ukrainian officials told Reuters and AFP. Regional authorities said the toll could climb further, with some reports putting it as high as 12 as rescue crews searched the rubble for residents still unaccounted for. The strike came as Russia kept up a broader bombardment of Ukrainian towns even as diplomats worked on a new push to end the war.

Sources: France 24 (AFP), Al Jazeera

US hosts Ukraine, European allies and NATO in Miami for talks on ending the war

Representatives of the United States, Ukraine, the European Commission, NATO and Britain, France and Germany met in Miami on Friday for talks aimed at ending the war with Russia before winter, with U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff calling the discussions "constructive." The two-day meeting was meant to produce a unified proposal, covering issues such as strikes on energy infrastructure and grain shipments, that Washington could then put to Moscow, though no agreement was announced. President Vladimir Putin told President Trump by phone that he saw little prospect of resuming broader talks soon, citing continued Ukrainian drone strikes.

Sources: MarketScreener (Reuters), Al Jazeera

Middle East

Gaza ceasefire marks grim first anniversary as rights groups say truce exists "in name only"

A year after the US-brokered Gaza ceasefire took hold, more than 100 humanitarian and rights organizations said this week that the truce has delivered little beyond the initial hostage release, citing Gaza health ministry figures showing more than 1,400 Palestinians killed by Israeli fire since the deal began. The UN says Israeli forces now hold more than 60% of Gaza, up from roughly half earlier this year, and reconstruction has not begun despite estimated rebuilding costs near $71 billion. Hamas says it will not disarm until Israeli forces fully withdraw, while Israel says its strikes target militants who violate the agreement.

Sources: Arab News (AP), Al Jazeera

Yemeni forces say they drove Houthis from key Red Sea strait; claim disputed

Yemen's Saudi-backed government said its forces, backed by an intensified Saudi-led air campaign, pushed the Iran-aligned Houthis out of most of the territory they had seized near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, including the port city of Mokha, this week. Reuters reported that four government sources described troops reaching only the city's outskirts as Houthi fighters retreated into the highlands, and the Houthis denied the city had fallen; Reuters said it could not independently verify the extent of the government's gains. Turkey and Pakistan have agreed to send troops to Saudi Arabia under a defense pact after Houthi retaliatory strikes hit Saudi airports, including one that killed three people in Riyadh on Thursday.

Sources: The Canberra Times (Reuters), Arab News

Africa

Sudanese paramilitary drone strike kills dozens of women and children in Blue Nile

A drone strike by Sudan's paramilitary Rapid Support Forces hit a displacement camp and informal gold-mining site in Wad al-Mahi, in Blue Nile state near the Ethiopian border, on Friday, killing at least 26 women and four children, an official and eyewitnesses told AFP. Blue Nile's regional government said the drone was launched from inside Ethiopia and called for an international investigation; the UN's preliminary information suggested the toll could be as high as 40, mostly women and children. Sudan has repeatedly accused Ethiopia of harboring RSF fighters along the border, which Addis Ababa denies.

Sources: Asharq Al-Awsat (AFP), Al Jazeera

Kenya's first Ebola case comes as Congo outbreak death toll tops 4,200

The World Health Organization's latest situation report counts 8,728 confirmed Ebola cases and 4,205 confirmed deaths in the Democratic Republic of Congo as of October 6, making the outbreak that began in May the second-largest on record. The report also confirmed Kenya's first case of the outbreak: a man who had lived in Congo for years and died in a Nairobi hospital after traveling home through Uganda, becoming the fourth country to record a case. Kenyan President William Ruto ordered an enhanced response after officials quarantined at least 29 contacts, including family members and health workers who treated him.

Sources: ABC News (AP), Al Jazeera

Asia

South Korea recalls ambassador to Ukraine in rare diplomatic protest over POW disclosure

South Korea's ambassador to Ukraine returned home Saturday after being recalled in protest over Kyiv's disclosure of the transfer of two captured North Korean soldiers to Seoul, Yonhap reported. Seoul says Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy broke a confidentiality agreement when he revealed the transfer at the UN General Assembly on September 23, while Kyiv denies any such agreement existed; South Korea's foreign ministry said an apology from its Ukrainian counterpart fell short of expectations. It is the first time Seoul has recalled an ambassador in protest since 2008, though the foreign ministry said the embassy will keep operating and consultations with Kyiv will continue.

Sources: The Korea Times (Yonhap), Al Jazeera

Pakistan's PTI long march presses toward Swabi as government weighs emergency powers

A march by supporters of jailed former prime minister Imran Khan continued toward the town of Swabi in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa on Saturday, its sixth day, after talks between his party and the federal government broke down and the government warned it would not allow the convoy into Islamabad. Pakistani media reported, citing unnamed government sources, that the federal government is weighing a two-month emergency, or governor's rule, across 12 Khyber Pakhtunkhwa districts in response to the march — a step PTI calls a violation of the constitution's 18th Amendment; neither the emergency plan nor its timing has been officially confirmed. Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Chief Minister Sohail Afridi, who is leading the march, said he would announce its next phase after reaching Swabi.

Sources: NPR, Dawn
