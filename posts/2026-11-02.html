Americas

Trump calls US-Canada trade deal "very close" as Carney reports progress days before midterms

President Trump said Saturday a trade deal with Canada was "very close," repeating his claim that the US has "been losing money for many years" in trade with its northern neighbor, after a call with Prime Minister Mark Carney. Carney described the conversation as building on "recent constructive discussions" toward a framework but said Canada would not rush into terms against its interest, with both governments now aiming to outline a plan within three to four weeks; talks had collapsed in August over a dispute on tariff relief for medium and heavy-duty vehicles, and Washington's 50% tariffs on roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods remain in place.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), Al Jazeera

Europe

Bulgaria's Iotova wins presidential runoff, becomes country's first elected woman head of state

Acting President Iliana Iotova won Bulgaria's presidential runoff with near-final results showing about 57.6% of the vote against conservative rival Andrey Gyurov's 39.5%, making her the country's first elected female head of state. Iotova became acting president in January after Rumen Radev resigned the post to run for parliament, and both she and Radev have called for a diplomatic end to Russia's war in Ukraine; European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen congratulated her on the win.

Sources: Reuters (via Internazionale), Kiprinform

Tusk blames Nawrocki for a "forced confrontation" as Poland's tribunal standoff drags into a new week

Prime Minister Donald Tusk said Monday that President Karol Nawrocki had chosen a "forced confrontation" over control of Poland's Constitutional Tribunal that could "cost Poland very dearly," days after a tribunal faction aligned with Tusk's coalition suspended the court's sitting president and installed its own judge over the weekend. Nawrocki, who cut short a trip abroad and was filmed in a scuffle with judges outside the tribunal, calls the move a "forcible takeover"; French far-right leader Marine Le Pen condemned the crisis on Monday, prompting Poland's foreign minister, Radosław Sikorski, to tell her not to interfere, while the European Commission has urged Nawrocki to accept the disputed judges.

Sources: AP (via U.S. News), Euronews

Middle East

Israeli strikes kill three more Palestinians in Gaza as year-old ceasefire keeps fraying

Israeli strikes killed at least three Palestinians and wounded several others in Gaza on Monday, medics said, with two killed in separate strikes in Khan Younis and a third in Al-Mussadar, a day after separate strikes killed eight people including five police officers. Gaza's health ministry says at least 1,583 Palestinians have been killed since the US-brokered ceasefire took hold roughly a year ago, while Israel says four of its soldiers have died in the same period; Israel says Hamas has been rearming and attacking in violation of the deal, which Hamas denies.

Sources: Reuters (via ABC News Australia), Arab News

Iranian negotiators leave Doha as US draft proposal circulates after third day of talks

Iranian negotiators met Qatari mediators in Doha for a third straight day on Sunday before departing, with a senior official saying "we have not reached a final deal" but that a draft proposal aimed at ending the US-Israel-Iran war is now circulating ahead of a possible follow-up meeting. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi called the talks "ongoing and progressing" and said the Strait of Hormuz could reopen within two days of any agreement with Washington, while President Trump has said the US will not resume strikes before Tuesday's US midterm elections.

Sources: Reuters (via The Jerusalem Post), Arab News

Africa

Ethnic clashes in southern Ethiopia push more than 50,000 people into Kenya

Fighting between armed groups from Ethiopia's Somali and Oromia regions around the border town of Moyale has displaced more than 50,000 people into northern Kenya over more than a week, the UN refugee agency and Kenya Red Cross say, with wire reporting putting the confirmed death toll at nine and rising. The violence has halted cross-border transport and trade, closed schools and disrupted national exams in the area, and the UN's humanitarian office says food, water, shelter and sanitation are now the most urgent unmet needs for those who have fled; Ethiopia's federal government has yet to issue an official public statement on the crisis.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), UN News

Asia

Newborn among at least 15 killed as Myanmar's military strikes a clinic and a school

Myanmar's military killed at least 15 people over the weekend in strikes on multiple sites in Rakhine and Kayah states, including a week-old baby girl killed when a maternity ward run by the Arakan Army collapsed in Mrauk-U, and three students killed in a separate airstrike on a school in Loikaw. Thailand's prime minister called the school strike a war crime, and UN investigators have accused Myanmar's military of deliberately attacking civilian buildings as part of an escalating campaign to retake territory from resistance groups that now control large parts of the country; a military government spokesperson did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

Sources: Reuters (via Free Malaysia Today), Manila Times

Bangladesh garment workers clash with police as wage protests shut more than 500 factories

Thousands of Bangladeshi garment workers blocked a highway and clashed with police in the Ashulia industrial hub on Monday, demanding a monthly minimum wage of 30,000 taka ($245) — more than double the current 12,500 taka — as protests over pay entered a third day. Police fired tear gas and stun grenades and workers set fire to a police vehicle, while the manufacturers' association says the unrest has disrupted operations at 529 factories in an industry that employs about four million people and generates more than 80% of Bangladesh's export earnings; the government formed a wage board Sunday in an attempt to end the protests, which continued anyway.

Sources: AFP (via Dawn), Reuters (via Bangkok Post)
