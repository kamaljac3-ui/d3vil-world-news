Americas

Brazil votes in tightest presidential race in a generation as Lula seeks a fourth term

Brazilians went to the polls Sunday to choose between President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, seeking an unprecedented fourth term, and Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, son of imprisoned former president Jair Bolsonaro. Final pre-election polling from Datafolha and Quaest showed the two separated by one to three points, with neither projected to clear the 50% threshold needed to avoid an October 25 runoff; the vote also decides two-thirds of the Senate, the full Chamber of Deputies and governorships nationwide.

Sources: Reuters (via Investing.com), Al Jazeera

UN says Haiti gang violence has killed or wounded 5,700 people this year

UN human rights chief Volker Türk told the Human Rights Council that gang violence in Haiti has killed or wounded at least 5,700 people so far this year, including more than 1,000 women and girls raped, with the toll — compiled through September 11 — likely an undercount. Türk said gangs continue to expand into new areas despite the deployment of a UN-backed Gang Suppression Force that remains far below its planned strength of 5,500 personnel, with roughly 1,500 deployed so far.

Sources: Associated Press (via Yahoo News), Rio Times Online

Europe

Hundreds of thousands march across Spain after parliament rejects Sánchez's housing decrees

Spain's parliament voted down two emergency housing decrees from Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez's minority government on Friday, and nationwide protests two days later drew tens of thousands into the streets — organizers claimed as many as 500,000 in Madrid alone, while police put the citywide count at 70,000. The demonstrations, partly sparked by the eviction of an elderly disabled woman, have fueled speculation that Sánchez could call a snap election as early as November.

Sources: Reuters (via U.S. News), Al Jazeera

Bosnia holds complex multiethnic election for its tripartite presidency

About 3.4 million registered voters in Bosnia and Herzegovina cast ballots Sunday to fill the country's three-member, power-sharing presidency along with national and regional parliaments, in the first nationwide use of biometric fingerprint identification and ballot scanners meant to combat fraud. Preliminary results were expected from the Central Election Commission late Sunday, though official results are not due until November 20.

Sources: Reuters (via Internazionale), Al Jazeera

Middle East

Yemeni forces press offensive around Taiz as Houthis claim strike on Saudi oil facility

Yemeni government forces carried out dozens of airstrikes and reported retaking positions from Houthi fighters around Taiz this week in what officials called the fiercest fighting over the city in years, as Saudi Arabia reportedly weighs a broader offensive against the Iran-backed group to secure the Red Sea shipping route. Houthi forces separately said they struck a Saudi Aramco facility near Riyadh on Saturday; a Saudi-led coalition spokesman called the claim misleading. The Aramco strike claim has not been independently confirmed. The renewed fighting has displaced more than 133,000 people from Taiz and Lahij provinces.

Sources: The National (citing Reuters), Al Jazeera

Israeli strike targeting senior Hamas official kills five in Gaza City

An Israeli airstrike on an apartment in Gaza City's Rimal neighborhood before dawn Saturday killed five people, including four women, Gaza's civil defense agency and a hospital said. The Israeli military said it had targeted Ali al-Amoudi, a senior Hamas official described as a former aide to the late Yahya Sinwar; as of Sunday, neither Israel nor Hamas had confirmed whether he was among the dead. Amoudi's fate has not been independently confirmed.

Sources: AFP (via France 24), Arab News

Africa

Ethiopian government forces seize Tigray's capital as rebel coalition withdraws

Federal troops and allied fighters took control of Mekelle, the Tigray regional capital, on Sunday after the Tigray People's Liberation Front said in a statement that it had “temporarily moved out” to “operate from somewhere else,” a day after government forces captured the city's airport. The fighting is the worst in the region since the 2022 Pretoria peace deal collapsed, and Washington has urged Ethiopia and Eritrea — which severed diplomatic ties last week amid mutual accusations of backing hostile forces — to exercise restraint.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Associated Press (via Yahoo News)

Sudan's army chief rejects US ceasefire plan as RSF says it accepts a truce

An adviser to Sudanese army chief Abdel Fattah al-Burhan told Reuters that a US-brokered 90-day ceasefire proposal from envoy Massad Boulos amounted to pressure to accept “a de facto partitioning of the country similar to the Libyan model,” after Washington largely ignored the government's counter-demand for an internationally monitored withdrawal of the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces from cities. The RSF, which seized the Darfur city of El Fasher in a campaign UN investigators say bore hallmarks of genocide, has separately said it accepts the US proposal, leaving the three-month truce without the army's buy-in.

Sources: Reuters (via Internazionale), Sudan Tribune

Asia

Myanmar junta airstrike on Rakhine market toll rises as ASEAN voices concern

The death toll from a Myanmar military airstrike on a market in Kyauktaw township, Rakhine state, has risen to roughly 49 people with dozens more wounded, after a senior rescue official confirmed the casualties to the Associated Press; the UN Human Rights Office called it one of the deadliest airstrikes in Rakhine since the 2021 coup. The Philippines, as this year's ASEAN chair, voiced concern on October 3 over the strike's toll, part of a pattern that has killed roughly 540 Myanmar civilians this year, mostly in Rakhine.

Sources: Associated Press (via Korea Times), Al-Monitor

China presses Japan to retract Taiwan remarks as diplomatic freeze continues

Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi told a visiting Japanese delegation led by former foreign minister Takeshi Iwaya that relations are “once again facing a severe situation” and demanded Tokyo “correct” Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi's November remarks that a Chinese attack on Taiwan could be an existential threat to Japan, reporting on the Beijing meeting noted. Beijing has maintained export controls on rare earths, tourism restrictions and tariffs on Japanese chemical exports since the remarks, with its Foreign Ministry spokesperson recently dropping Takaichi's title in briefings as a further diplomatic snub.

Sources: Nippon.com, Al Jazeera
