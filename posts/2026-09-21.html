Americas

US strike kills four in latest Caribbean drug-boat operation, pushing the campaign's toll past 230

US Southern Command said it struck a "go-fast" vessel in the Caribbean on Saturday, killing four people it described as narco-terrorists, without publicly detailing evidence that the boat was carrying drugs. The strike was the latest in a campaign that began roughly a year ago and has now killed at least 231 people in 69 strikes, a toll that lawmakers and foreign governments, including Colombia and Venezuela, have questioned as several of those killed may not have been trafficking narcotics.

Sources: ABC News (AP), Euronews

Peruvian journalist and regional governor candidate shot dead at campaign event

Susy Aponte Polo, a journalist running for governor of Peru's Áncash region, was shot dead Saturday at a market in Caraz while handing out campaign material, days after she said publicly that she had received death threats. Peruvian prosecutors are investigating the killing as a contract hit; three people have been detained, and five bystanders were wounded in the attack, which comes two weeks before Peru's October 4 regional and municipal elections.

Sources: Infobae (EFE), La República

Europe

Germany's CDU crashes out of state parliament as AfD wins Mecklenburg-Vorpommern

The far-right Alternative for Germany won Sunday's state election in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern with about 38% of the vote, becoming the strongest party there for the first time, while Chancellor Friedrich Merz's Christian Democrats collapsed to roughly 5%, their worst result in any state election since 1949. Merz called the outcome a "disaster" but said he would stay on as chancellor, as the same day's Berlin election handed the strongest result to the left-wing Die Linke.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Euronews

Denmark says NATO, not just Washington, will share responsibility for Greenland's security

Ahead of a security agreement due to be signed by the US, Denmark and Greenland at the United Nations General Assembly on Tuesday, Danish officials said the deal will place Arctic security under NATO's wider umbrella rather than leaving it solely to Washington and Copenhagen. The two sides still disagree publicly on what the pact actually grants: President Trump has said it gives the US "permanent control" over Greenland's security, while Denmark and Greenland say it does not compromise the island's sovereignty.

Sources: US News (AP), Al Jazeera

Middle East

Israeli settler killed in West Bank shooting; Netanyahu orders expanded military operations

A gunman opened fire on Israelis gathered at a spring near the West Bank settlement of Neve Tzuf on Saturday, killing 36-year-old Netanel Shakron; soldiers arrested a suspect at a nearby hospital hours later. Separately the same day, Israeli forces shot dead a Palestinian man near Jenin whom the military said had attempted a car-ramming attack, and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu instructed security forces to expand operations across the West Bank in response to the shooting.

Sources: KSAT (AP), Al Jazeera

Netanyahu confirms UN General Assembly trip despite active ICC arrest warrant

Israeli officials confirmed Netanyahu will fly to New York this week to address the UN General Assembly on September 24, more than a year after the International Criminal Court issued a warrant for his arrest over alleged war crimes in Gaza. The visit is occurring amid a public dispute with New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani, who has said the city should enforce the ICC warrant, while the Trump administration has said Netanyahu will not be arrested; the US is not a party to the court that issued the warrant.

Sources: NPR, Al Jazeera

Africa

Seven Ethiopian armed groups, including former Tigray-war foes, form alliance to oust Abiy Ahmed

Seven Ethiopian armed factions — including the Tigray People's Liberation Front and the Amhara Fano movement, which fought on opposite sides of the 2020-2022 Tigray war, plus the Oromo Liberation Army and four smaller regional groups — announced Sunday they had formed the "Alliance for Survival" to try to remove Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's government and install a transitional administration. The groups accused Abiy of authoritarian rule and said they want a national dialogue, though analysts note the federal government's drone-heavy military still holds a decisive advantage; Addis Ababa has not yet publicly responded.

Sources: US News (AP), Al Jazeera

Asia

Xi Jinping confirms first US state visit since 2015

China's foreign ministry said Monday that President Xi Jinping will make a state visit to Washington from September 23 to 25 at Donald Trump's invitation, his first trip to the US presidential residence in over a decade. The visit, which will include a state dinner and bilateral talks, follows Trump's own visit to China earlier this year and comes as the two governments try to stabilize a relationship strained by trade, technology and security disputes.

Sources: Investing.com (Reuters), US News

Tens of thousands rally in Manila on martial law anniversary, demanding action on flood-corruption scandal

Thousands of protesters marched in Manila and other Philippine cities on Monday, marking the 54th anniversary of Ferdinand Marcos Sr.'s 1972 martial law declaration while demanding accountability over a scandal involving billions of dollars in fraudulent flood-control projects. More than 17,000 police officers were deployed nationwide and the presidential palace complex was locked down; two people were arrested on vandalism charges and tensions briefly rose between police and demonstrators, but authorities said the rallies were largely peaceful, a year after a similar march turned deadly.

Sources: The Star (AP), Philstar
