Americas

Alberta voters reject first step toward leaving Canada

Albertans rejected, by roughly a three-to-one margin, a referendum asking whether the province should begin the constitutionally required process toward a future binding vote on separation, with early counts showing about 76% favoring remaining in Canada. Premier Danielle Smith called the result a mandate for "a stronger Alberta in a united Canada," while Prime Minister Mark Carney said Albertans had made "a clear choice" for unity. Separatist organizers said the roughly quarter of voters who backed starting the process showed real grievance remains, and vowed to keep pressing their case.

Sources: NPR, CTV News (Canadian Press)

Gangs attack international force in Haiti as elections postponed indefinitely

Gang gunmen attacked an armored vehicle carrying foreign personnel near Port-au-Prince on Sunday and set it ablaze, in what officials called the largest assault yet on the international force deployed to fight Haiti's gangs; the fate of four missing crew members remained unclear. The attack came as the United Nations told the Security Council that gangs, who now control roughly 70% of the capital, were adapting to the security crackdown and expanding into rural areas, prompting Haiti to indefinitely postpone presidential and legislative elections planned for December 13. The UN says gang violence killed more than 1,100 people and displaced nearly 1.5 million people between July and early October.

Sources: AP (via Manila Times), Al Jazeera

Europe

France imposes overnight curfews after a weekend of rioting

France's government rolled out nighttime curfews in Paris, Lyon, Bordeaux and Nice after a weekend of nationwide unrest over living costs and school conditions turned violent, with Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez saying 2,513 people had been taken into custody and 67 jailed. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu said the curfews had brought "a measure of calm," even as he acknowledged police had briefly lost control of parts of Paris, Marseille and Lyon despite roughly 60,000 officers deployed nationwide. President Emmanuel Macron rejected Nuñez's offer to resign after the minister admitted the lapses.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), CBS News

Flash floods kill at least four in eastern Spain

Torrential rain from Storm Adam triggered flash floods across Spain's Valencia and Alicante regions and the Balearic Islands, killing at least four people and forcing emergency crews to rescue more than 450 others stranded by rising water. Shelter-in-place orders initially covered roughly 800,000 people before being scaled back to about 87,000 people across eight municipalities by Monday evening, and Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez said military emergency units had joined the rescue effort. Officials said the toll remained far below the more than 220 deaths in the region's 2024 floods, crediting a now-functioning telephone alert system.

Sources: AFP (via Tempo.co), NL Times

Middle East

Houthi missile strike on Yemeni army camp kills dozens of soldiers

Iran-aligned Houthi rebels fired ballistic missiles into a government training camp in Yemen's Shabwa province on Monday, in what Yemeni officials called the deadliest attack since fighting escalated three months ago. Shabwa's governor, Awad al-Wazir, told Reuters at least 58 soldiers were killed and more than 200 wounded, while a separate AP count put the toll at 60 dead with roughly a dozen still missing; the Houthis said the camp was run by Saudi officers. Yemen's presidential council chairman, Rashad al-Alimi, condemned the strike and pledged a "firm response."

Sources: Reuters (via Saudi Gazette), AP (via The Korea Times)

UN food agency cuts Gaza aid for half a million people

The World Food Programme said Monday it will stop monthly food assistance to 500,000 people in Gaza because of a funding shortfall, shrinking its caseload there from about 1.6 million to 1.1 million people. The agency said remaining recipients will get a single food parcel instead of two and fewer cash payments, while daily hot meals will fall from 250,000 to 150,000; it warned it needs a further $450 million to sustain operations over the next six months. The WFP said 2026 funding for its Palestinian territories operation has come in at less than half of prior years' levels.

Sources: AFP (via France 24), Al Jazeera

Africa

Drone strike on Sudanese market town kills dozens as UN condemns attack wave

A drone strike hit a transport hub in Al-Dibab, a market town in West Kordofan, on Sunday evening; witnesses and medical sources told AFP at least 80 people were killed, mostly commuters, while rights groups and the Rapid Support Forces-aligned administration put the toll far higher, near 150. Sudan's army, which the RSF-aligned side blamed for the strike, told AP it does not target civilian infrastructure and denied responsibility. A UN spokesperson said drone attacks have killed more than 160 people across Sudan's Darfur and Kordofan regions in recent days, including children, and condemned the continuing toll on civilians. The exact death toll remains disputed between sources.

Sources: AFP (via France 24), Al Jazeera

Exiled critic of Rwanda's Kagame found beheaded in Uganda

Robert Kabera, a former Rwandan army officer who ran a YouTube channel critical of President Paul Kagame, was found beheaded in a rural district outside Kampala, Ugandan police said, with his head not yet recovered. Rwanda's opposition Rwanda National Congress called for a transparent investigation, and Kabera's brother rejected past Rwandan military allegations against him as a "witch-hunt." Ugandan authorities said no motive has been established and an investigation is ongoing. No link between the killing and Kabera's political activities has been officially confirmed.

Sources: CTV News (Canadian Press), Al Jazeera

Asia

Kolkata stampede toll rises to 19 as arrests follow investigation

The death toll from a crush at a Durga Puja pavilion in Kolkata climbed to 19, with about 25 more injured, after a crowd sheltering from sudden rain surged and fell against each other outside the venue on October 17. West Bengal police arrested seven people, including two senior organizers of the puja committee, and a judge-led inquiry has been ordered after autopsies pointed to suffocation and crush injuries as the cause of death. India's prime minister's office announced compensation of 200,000 rupees for each victim's family.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), The Hans India

Philippines summons Chinese envoy over "threat" of force remark

The Philippines' foreign department summoned China's ambassador, Jing Quan, after he said Beijing could "easily win" a military confrontation with Manila but preferred not to use force — a remark the department called a "thinly veiled threat." President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. said the comment sounded "very much like" a threat and arranged to discuss it with Jing directly, but his office said this week he does not intend to declare the ambassador persona non grata. China's embassy accused Philippine officials of "selective quotation and overinterpretation" of Jing's remarks.

Sources: Reuters (via Internazionale), Philstar.com
