Americas

Ecuador declares a 60-day state of emergency and coastal curfews as gang violence surges

President Daniel Noboa declared a 60-day state of emergency across eight provinces on September 15, suspending several constitutional protections and authorizing warrantless raids to confront organized-crime violence. The decree followed 442 homicides recorded in the four weeks before it was signed, with Guayas province accounting for nearly half; a midnight-to-5-a.m. curfew now runs through September 30 in the hardest-hit coastal provinces.

Sources: BERNAMA, Rio Times Online

US drops Venezuela from failed-narcotics list, keeps Colombia decertified a second year

The Trump administration removed Venezuela from its annual list of countries deemed to have failed to combat drug trafficking, the first time in more than two decades, crediting cooperation from Caracas's interim government since Nicolás Maduro's removal. Colombia was decertified for a second consecutive year over record coca cultivation under outgoing President Gustavo Petro, though Washington issued Bogotá and Bolivia, which also stayed on the failing list, waivers preserving US assistance.

Sources: Al Jazeera, US News (AP)

Europe

Sweden's Kristersson resigns as a center-left bloc narrowly wins the general election

Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson said he would resign after his right-wing governing bloc was narrowly defeated by opposition parties led by Magdalena Andersson's Social Democrats, with preliminary results showing the two blocs separated by roughly 50,000 votes. Kristersson wished Andersson “good luck” forming a government, though Sweden's parliament cannot vote on a new prime minister before September 29.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Washington Post

Russian strikes hit Kyiv, Zaporizhzhia and Odesa regions, killing one

Russian missiles and drones struck an industrial park west of Kyiv and hit a steel mill and an electronics plant in Zaporizhzhia on Thursday, killing at least one person and wounding several others, Ukrainian officials said. Ukraine's air force said it shot down or jammed 131 of the incoming aerial targets overnight, while a separate Ukrainian drone strike set fire to an oil refinery in Russia's Yaroslavl region.

Sources: US News (AP), Al Jazeera

Middle East

Israeli strikes continue in Gaza despite the ceasefire; toll since the truce nears 1,400

Israeli strikes killed at least two Palestinians in Gaza City on Saturday, including a man on a motorcycle in the Sheikh Radwan neighborhood, in the latest of near-daily violations of the year-old ceasefire, according to Gaza officials. Gaza's health ministry says at least 1,386 Palestinians have been killed since the truce took effect on October 10, 2025, though the UN human rights office says it has independently verified a smaller portion of that toll so far.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Middle East Monitor

Iran executes man convicted of spying for Israel on missile sites

Iran's judiciary said it hanged Hossein Pedaran after the Supreme Court upheld his death sentence for passing Israel's Mossad intelligence on military sites in Isfahan province, with officials saying some of the identified sites were later struck during the February war. Reuters reported that Iran has sharply increased its use of the death penalty for alleged espionage since that conflict began.

Sources: Al Arabiya, US News (Reuters)

Africa

Tinubu orders prosecution over deaths of 37 miners in Nigerian custody as protests grow

Nigerian President Bola Tinubu ordered that any official found responsible for the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners in Niger State security custody be arrested and prosecuted, saying the earlier suspension of the state's NSCDC commandant was “only an administrative step.” Reuters reported clashes between protesters and security personnel in Minna this week as anger grew over the deaths, which an intelligence report shared with the AP attributed to overcrowding and poor ventilation in the holding cell.

Sources: Channels Television, Rio Times Online

Asia

20th Asian Games open in Nagoya with 11,000 athletes from 45 nations

The Asian Games opened Saturday in Japan's Aichi Prefecture, launching a 16-day competition billed as the world's second-largest multi-sport event after the Olympics. Host Japan is fielding its largest delegation, about 900 athletes, in the country's third time hosting the Games, which run through October 4.

Sources: WRAL (AP), Al Jazeera

China threatens to cancel Trump-Xi summit over new Taiwan arms sales

China warned Washington it would cancel a planned September 24 summit between President Trump and President Xi Jinping if the US approves new arms transfers to Taiwan, Kyodo News reported, reiterating that Taiwan remains a “red line” in bilateral relations. Trump has floated the prospective arms sales as a bargaining chip and is said to want the summit ahead of November's midterm elections.

Sources: Kyiv Post (Kyodo News), Taipei Times
