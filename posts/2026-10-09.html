Americas

Riot at Mexican prison in cartel-plagued Sinaloa state kills 10

A brawl that broke out during visiting hours Thursday at the El Castillo penitentiary in Mazatlán, in Mexico's Sinaloa state, left nine inmates and one visitor dead and 16 people injured, state security officials said. Army, navy, National Guard and state police units were sent in to restore order at the facility, which holds roughly 1,800 inmates, and officials said it was back under control within hours. Authorities have not said what triggered the violence and have suspended family visits while the state attorney general's office investigates.

Sources: Al Jazeera, KSAT (AP)

Brazilian Supreme Court orders arrest of Eduardo Bolsonaro days before runoff

Justice Alexandre de Moraes ordered federal police to arrest Eduardo Bolsonaro, cancel his passport and begin enforcing his four-year prison sentence, after his conviction for lobbying the Trump administration to pressure Brazil's Supreme Court became final on October 3. Eduardo, the brother of presidential candidate Flávio Bolsonaro, has lived in the United States since 2025 and is unlikely to be extradited; a law professor cited by AP said the order appears designed to pressure him rather than secure his return. It lands weeks before Brazil's presidential runoff between Flávio Bolsonaro and incumbent Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, after neither won a majority in the first round.

Sources: Click2Houston (AP), Al Jazeera

Europe

Russian glide bomb kills at least 30 at Ukrainian bus stop in Kramatorsk

A Russian glide bomb struck two minibuses waiting at a stop in the eastern Ukrainian city of Kramatorsk on Thursday, killing at least 30 people and injuring 18, Ukrainian officials said. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the attack would "not go unanswered," while Donetsk's regional governor accused Russia of deliberately targeting civilians and ordered public transport suspended in Kramatorsk and the neighboring city of Sloviansk. The strike came a day after a separate Russian missile attack killed 22 people, including five children, in the northern town of Pryluky.

Sources: Al Jazeera, The Washington Post

Ukrainian drones hit Russian tech giant Yandex's data center for the first time

Ukrainian drones struck a Yandex data center in Sasovo, in Russia's Ryazan region, late Wednesday, starting a fire that forced the company to take the facility offline, Russian officials and Yandex said. The Moscow Times reported it was the first confirmed Ukrainian strike on Russian data infrastructure since the 2022 invasion began; the site is one of five major Yandex data centers and reportedly houses two of the three supercomputers the company uses to develop AI models. No casualties were reported, and Yandex said it could not yet confirm whether the damaged equipment could be repaired.

Sources: France 24 (AFP), The Moscow Times

Middle East

Houthi attack on Riyadh airport kills three, deadliest yet on Saudi capital

Saudi Arabia's civil aviation authority said Houthi-claimed strikes hit King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on Thursday, killing three Saudi nationals, including an airline pilot, and wounding several people of other nationalities. Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthi movement said it struck the airport in retaliation for the kingdom's support of Yemeni government forces, who launched a major offensive last weekend to retake Houthi-held territory along the Red Sea coast. Hundreds of flights were canceled before the airport said operations had returned to normal.

Sources: France 24 (AFP), US News (AP)

Iran steps up tanker attacks around Strait of Hormuz, pushing oil above $104

Iran has intensified attacks on commercial tankers in and around the Strait of Hormuz, with UK Maritime Trade Operations logging nine incidents in the first week of October alone — about half of September's total for the waterway and the wider Persian Gulf. Brent crude settled near $104 a barrel this week as roughly 30% less oil moved through the strait than before the war between Iran and the US and Israel began, even as Iranian strikes this week reached tankers off Qatar for the first time in six months. President Trump said the US would not resume strikes on Iran before November's midterm elections, but that its naval blockade of Iranian ports would continue.

Sources: Euronews, CNBC

Africa

South Africa withdraws asylum directive after anti-migrant violence in Soweto, Durban

South Africa's Home Affairs department withdrew a directive letting asylum seekers who entered the country unlawfully apply for protection, a day after anti-migrant protests turned violent in the Johannesburg township of Soweto and the port city of Durban. Rioters torched roughly two dozen vehicles combined and looted foreign-owned shops on Thursday, and three foreign nationals were arrested on accusations of assaulting a South African protester. Officials said the directive, meant to carry out a July Constitutional Court ruling, had overwhelmed refugee offices and put staff at risk amid false claims that the ruling granted undocumented migrants automatic legal status.

Sources: ThePrint (Reuters), Daily Maverick

Ethiopia accuses Eritrea of "blatant aggression" as airstrikes kill 17 near Tigray front

Ethiopia's government accused Eritrea of "blatant aggression" in a letter to the UN Security Council, after residents and local officials told AFP that soldiers in Eritrean uniforms have been seen moving toward the Tigray regional capital, Mekelle, through the towns of Adigrat and Idaga Hamus; Eritrea has dismissed the troop reports as an Ethiopian pretext for renewed war. Airstrikes of disputed origin killed at least 17 people in the Tigray towns of Adwa and Sheraro this week, days after Ethiopian drones reportedly targeted Eritrean positions near Adigrat. Mekelle remains without a new administration more than a week after Tigray People's Liberation Front fighters withdrew from the city, and local radio and television have stayed off the air.

Sources: Al Jazeera, ClickOnDetroit (AP)

Navi Pillay, who led UN panel that found Israel committed genocide, wins Nobel Peace Prize

The Norwegian Nobel Committee awarded the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize to Navi Pillay, an 85-year-old South African jurist and former UN human rights chief, for what it called her decades of work upholding international law and human rights. Pillay served as a judge on the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda and the International Criminal Court and, more recently, led the UN commission that concluded Israel had committed genocide against Palestinians in Gaza. Committee chair Jørgen Watne Frydnes said judges and international courts are under political attack; Pillay was chosen from 287 nominees, among them Donald Trump.

Sources: The Philadelphia Inquirer (AP), Al Jazeera

Asia

Pakistan's PTI long march reaches Peshawar as government blocks path to Islamabad

A convoy led by Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Chief Minister Sohail Afridi reached Peshawar on Thursday, the fifth day of a march by supporters of jailed former prime minister Imran Khan demanding his release, after two rounds of talks between his party and the federal government broke down. The government has said the march will not be allowed into Islamabad and has placed shipping containers and other roadblocks around the capital, while Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi warned authorities "will not spare anyone who violates the law." Khan has been imprisoned since August 2023 on charges his party calls politically motivated, and PTI has not yet announced the march's next stop beyond Peshawar.

Sources: US News (Reuters), Dawn

South Korea says North Korea planted DMZ mines, prepares to demand apology

A joint investigation by South Korea's military and the UN Command concluded that North Korean-made anti-personnel mines caused a blast that wounded three South Korean soldiers inside the Demilitarized Zone on September 21, violating the 1953 armistice. Defense Minister Kang Shin-chul told Yonhap that Seoul's response would be "decisive" and "unprecedented," though officials have not detailed what steps they will take, while North Korea's Kim Yo Jong dismissed the findings as fabricated and warned of retaliation if South Korean troops fire on North Korean soldiers at the border. South Korean officials said North Korean troops crossed the Military Demarcation Line more than 20 times in August alone, compared with 17 crossings in all of 2025.

Sources: The Korea Times (Yonhap), Al Jazeera
