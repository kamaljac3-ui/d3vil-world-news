Americas

Paraguay's ousted president held in military custody as investigators find an empty vault in his mansion

Former Paraguayan President Santiago Peña remains jailed at a military facility outside Asunción after a judge ruled him a flight risk in the collapse of Ueno Bank, which froze deposits for roughly half the country's adults, according to the AP and Reuters. Prosecutors who raided Peña's summer home in San Bernardino last weekend found a hidden, armor-plated vault behind a false wall; after obtaining the combination from his lawyer, investigators opened it this week and found it empty, Al Jazeera and MercoPress reported. Peña, impeached and replaced by President Pedro Alliana on November 6, faces breach-of-trust charges carrying up to ten years each and denies wrongdoing.

Sources: AP (via US News), Al Jazeera, MercoPress

Salvadoran-backed raid destroys Haitian gang headquarters, killing 20

Haitian police backed by Salvadoran troops raided the headquarters of the 400 Mawozo gang over the weekend, an operation President Nayib Bukele said destroyed the home of a senior gang leader and left 20 people dead, according to AFP and Al Jazeera. The raid follows weeks of tension after four Salvadoran personnel attached to a UN-backed anti-gang mission went missing in an October ambush, prompting Bukele to deploy roughly 2,500 troops to Haiti, most operating outside the UN framework. Haitian Prime Minister Alix Didier Fils-Aimé praised the operation and is due in El Salvador next week to discuss the troops' future, even as Amnesty International presses both governments to disclose the deployment's terms.

Sources: AFP (via France 24), Al Jazeera

Europe

Russia and Ukraine end second day of Abu Dhabi talks without a territorial breakthrough

US-mediated talks between Russian and Ukrainian negotiators wrapped a second day in Abu Dhabi on Wednesday with no agreement on territory, according to the AP and Reuters. Moscow continues to demand Ukraine withdraw from eastern areas still under Kyiv's control, while President Volodymyr Zelensky said such questions can only be settled by national leaders, not negotiators, and Ukraine's Rustem Umerov said security guarantees were also discussed. US envoys are expected to travel next to Moscow to meet President Vladimir Putin and then to Dublin to meet Zelensky, while Russia has said it sees no reason to include European officials directly in the talks.

Sources: AP (via WRAL), Reuters (via Investing.com)

World leaders urged to "get serious" as COP31's leaders' summit opens in Antalya

Dozens of heads of state and government gathered in Antalya, Turkey, on Wednesday for the two-day leaders' segment of the COP31 climate conference, with UN Secretary-General António Guterres warning the world has entered what he called a "new age of climate extremes," according to Reuters. Turkey's presidency is pushing a pledge to electrify more than a third of the global economy by 2035, while poorer nations continue to argue that existing climate finance falls well short of their needs. Most G7 leaders, including France's Emmanuel Macron, Germany's Friedrich Merz and Italy's Giorgia Meloni, skipped the summit, and the United States sent no official delegation.

Sources: Reuters (via GMA News), Hurriyet Daily News

Middle East

Israeli fire kills seven Palestinians in Gaza in a single day, including a father and son

Israeli strikes and gunfire killed at least seven Palestinians across Gaza on Tuesday, according to Reuters, with medics saying a father and his 13-year-old son died when a strike hit the roof of a displacement shelter in Gaza City's Zeitoun neighborhood. Separate incidents killed a man in Khan Younis and three more people in a strike on a metal foundry in Gaza City's Sabra neighborhood; the Israeli military said it had targeted militants in both areas without elaborating. Health officials in Gaza say more than 1,600 Palestinians have been killed since the ceasefire took hold, while Israel says four of its soldiers have died over the same period, according to Arab News.

Sources: Reuters (via ThePrint), Arab News

G7 ministers warn of a Lebanese "security void" as UN peacekeepers' withdrawal nears

G7 foreign ministers meeting in Paris agreed on the need to avoid a security vacuum in southern Lebanon once UN peacekeepers leave, but reached no agreement on what would replace them, French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot said, according to Al Jazeera and Arab News. The UN Security Council voted under US pressure to end UNIFIL's mandate by December 31, with full withdrawal expected by the end of 2027; Secretary-General Guterres has floated a transitional uniformed UN presence, though that could require a new resolution facing a possible US veto. A UNIFIL patrol came under fire in southern Lebanon days earlier without casualties, while Israeli forces remain in a roughly 10-kilometer zone inside the country.

Sources: Al Jazeera, AFP (via Asharq Al-Awsat)

Africa

Nearly 64,000 Nigerians flee to Niger as violence deepens a regional crisis

The UN refugee agency said this week that almost 64,000 people have fled violence in northern Nigeria into Niger since the start of the year, with hundreds more crossing into Benin, according to UN News and allAfrica. UNHCR is appealing for $11.4 million to respond, including $2 million for immediate needs such as water, shelter and health care, as most new arrivals settle in Niger's already-strained Diffa and Tahoua regions, which were hosting more than half a million refugees before this year. The agency said violence from jihadist groups, criminal gangs and farmer-herder clashes is driving the displacement, and roughly 34,000 of the new arrivals remain unregistered and at heightened risk.

Sources: UN News, Vanguard

Somalia's president pushes reconciliation talks as court standoff over his term drags on

Somali President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud has proposed national reconciliation talks for November 14-18, even as his government continues to reject a Supreme Court ruling that his term expired in May and that his dismissal of the sitting chief justice was unlawful, according to the AP and allAfrica. The Attorney General's office insists the ruling has no legal effect, while opposition figures say security forces pushed out the dismissed chief justice, Bashe Yusuf Ahmed — a claim that has not been independently verified. Rival political factions have questioned whether Mohamud has standing to host the proposed talks given the dispute over his own legitimacy in office.

Sources: AP (via The Japan Times), allAfrica

Asia

Indonesia's vice president offers to resign amid a probe into his 2024 eligibility

Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka told President Prabowo Subianto this week that he intends to resign as lawmakers weigh an inquiry into whether he was eligible to run for the office in 2024, according to Reuters and the AP. Presidential spokesperson Prasetyo Hadi said the offer followed developments at the Constitutional Court and Supreme Court, though the palace says Gibran remains in office and has not formally stepped down; Gibran has asked to contest the eligibility questions before the State Administrative Court. A forum of retired military officers has separately urged the national assembly to consider removing him, a process that would have Prabowo nominate two replacement candidates within 60 days of any vacancy.

Sources: Reuters (via Yahoo News), AP (via The Washington Post)

Philippine Vice President Sara Duterte's impeachment trial resumes with defense witnesses

The Philippine Senate's impeachment trial of Vice President Sara Duterte resumed this week on its sole remaining charge — allegations of unexplained wealth and incomplete asset disclosures — after prosecutors withdrew three other articles, according to the AP's ongoing coverage of the case and Philippine outlets including GMA News and the Philippine Star. The defense has called several witnesses tied to Duterte's property and business holdings, with a corporate lawyer testifying this week about companies linked to her, and presiding officer Senate President Pro Tempore Chiz Escudero has said he cannot predict when the trial will conclude. Speculation has grown that Duterte could resign before a possible conviction, though her defense team says no such decision has been made.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), GMA News
