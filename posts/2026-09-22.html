Americas

World leaders open UN General Assembly as Trump set to address body for first time since starting war with Iran

The UN General Assembly's high-level week opened in New York on Tuesday, with President Trump scheduled to address the body for the first time since the United States went to war with Iran earlier this year. US Ambassador Mike Waltz said Trump's remarks would highlight peace deals reached across the Western Hemisphere and progress on his Gaza plan alongside a warning about Iran acquiring a nuclear weapon, as the gathering unfolds against a shaken global economy and growing anxiety over artificial intelligence.

Sources: WSLS (AP), France 24

Europe

Zelensky and Trump meet in New York after Ukraine's biggest-ever drone strike on Moscow

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky met with President Trump on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly on Tuesday, two days after Ukraine launched its largest drone attack of the war on the Moscow region, striking one of Russia's biggest oil refineries and killing at least three people. Zelensky said he wants to discuss with Trump a mutual pause on strikes against energy infrastructure and Black Sea targets, calling the meeting one that "could change a lot."

Sources: NBC News, Ukrainska Pravda (Reuters)

EU scrambles to save Russia sanctions list as Latvia blocks deal to delist two oligarchs

European Union sanctions on nearly 3,000 Russia-linked individuals and entities were due to expire at midnight Tuesday after Latvia rejected a compromise that would have lifted sanctions on billionaires Alisher Usmanov and Mikhail Fridman in exchange for a three-year renewal of the rest of the list. Latvian Prime Minister Andris Kulbergs said on social media that "Latvia cannot accept such an offer," while France had separately pushed for Usmanov's removal after Azerbaijan detained two French nationals, a trade-off that drew objections from other member states.

Sources: Reuters (via Internazionale), Euronews

Middle East

UN warns tens of thousands more Yemenis could flee as fighting surges

The UN refugee agency said Tuesday that an upsurge in fighting has already displaced more than 130,000 people in Yemen this month, with planning projections showing as many as 100,000 more could be forced from their homes over the next three months. UNHCR spokesperson Babar Baloch said most of the displaced remain inside Yemen, though more than 3,000 people have fled by boat across the Red Sea to Djibouti and Somalia, a number expected to grow as seas calm.

Sources: Reuters (via The Print), UN News

G7 foreign ministers demand Houthis halt strikes on Saudi Arabia, press Iran to stop arming them

The foreign ministers of the G7 nations condemned what they called "unacceptable continued strikes" by Yemen's Houthi movement against Saudi Arabia in a joint statement issued Monday on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly. The ministers called on the Houthis to immediately halt attacks on civilian shipping and return to the political process, and urged Iran to end its arming and support of the group, which they said risked undermining international trade.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Arab News

Africa

Ethiopia suspends Tigray TV, downplays new rebel alliance seeking to oust Abiy Ahmed

Ethiopia's Media Authority indefinitely suspended Tigray Television on Monday, a day after the broadcaster aired the announcement of a new alliance of seven armed and political groups seeking to overthrow Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's government. Government spokesperson Billene Seyoum said the groups, which include former Tigray-war foes the TPLF and Amhara Fano movement, had already been collaborating with backing from Eritrea, adding that the announcement was "only attesting to what the Ethiopian government has been alleging."

Sources: Infobae (EFE), Al Jazeera

Amnesty says M23 rebels forced Congolese miners to work under threat, trafficked gold and coltan to Rwanda

Amnesty International said Monday that Rwanda-backed M23 fighters have committed serious abuses against artisanal miners in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo, including forced labor and at least 18 killings at two mining sites in North and South Kivu, while trafficking gold and coltan extracted from the areas to Rwanda. The rights group said evidence gathered from miners and its own analysts showed gold-bearing material from one site was transported by boat to Rwanda, and that the plunder likely amounts to the war crime of pillage.

Sources: dpa (via NewsBreak), UPI

Asia

EU and Philippines reach "substantial agreement" on free trade deal

The European Union and the Philippines reached a substantial agreement Tuesday on a free trade deal, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said after speaking with Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr., putting negotiations on a path toward formal conclusion in the coming months. The deal would grow roughly 30 billion euros in annual trade between the two sides and makes the Philippines only the third ASEAN country, after Singapore and Vietnam, to strike a bilateral free-trade agreement with the EU.

Sources: BusinessWorld, US News (AP)
