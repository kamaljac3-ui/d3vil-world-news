Americas

Drone attack on Colombian army base kills three soldiers, wounds at least 16

Explosive-laden drones struck an army compound in Santa Rosa del Sur, a gold-mining town in Bolívar department, around midday on November 7, killing three soldiers and wounding at least 16 others, the military said, according to the AP and Al Jazeera. No group claimed responsibility, though the area is contested by the National Liberation Army (ELN), the Gulf Clan and FARC splinter factions competing for control of local gold mines and smuggling routes. President Abelardo de la Espriella condemned the attack, saying those responsible "will have nowhere to hide."

Sources: AP (via CP24), Al Jazeera

Mexico captures Jalisco cartel cell leader tied to mayor's assassination

Mexican naval special forces captured Rafael Álvarez Ayala, known as "R2," in Zapopan on November 8 after a shootout that wounded him and a sailor, Security Minister Omar García Harfuch said, according to Reuters and Al Jazeera. Authorities describe Álvarez as the new leader of "Los Rs," a Jalisco New Generation Cartel cell accused of extortion, kidnapping and dozens of killings, including the 2025 assassination of Uruapan Mayor Carlos Manzo; nineteen other suspects were also detained in the operation. The arrest followed intelligence-sharing with the US Drug Enforcement Administration, and Álvarez faces an outstanding Mexican warrant as well as a pending US extradition request.

Sources: Reuters (via Yahoo News), Al Jazeera

Europe

Russian strikes leave hundreds of thousands without power in Ukraine ahead of Abu Dhabi talks

Russian strikes on Ukraine's energy grid overnight into November 9 cut power to more than 600,000 people in the Dnipropetrovsk region and roughly 250,000 more around Kharkiv, utility operator DTEK and regional officials said, according to AFP and Al Jazeera. Ukraine's energy ministry reported new outages across 12 regions as temperatures dropped toward freezing, and President Volodymyr Zelensky accused Moscow of intensifying the strikes to pressure Kyiv ahead of US-brokered talks with Russia set for November 10–11 in Abu Dhabi. Russia's defense ministry had not commented on this specific strike.

Sources: AFP (via France 24), Al Jazeera

Europe wins a role at Abu Dhabi talks on Ukraine over Kremlin objections

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said on November 9 that the EU, together with Germany, France, the UK and NATO, will send representatives to the US-mediated Ukraine-Russia talks in Abu Dhabi, defying Russian objections that the format stay limited to Russia, Ukraine and the United States, AFP and the AP reported. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said he had "coordinated positions" with von der Leyen ahead of the talks, while the Kremlin's Dmitry Peskov said Moscow still expects the Tuesday–Wednesday sessions to remain trilateral. The dispute reflects European concern about being sidelined from decisions over the war's outcome and future security guarantees.

Sources: AFP (via France 24), AP (via OPB)

Middle East

Yemen's truce collapses into renewed war as village airstrike toll climbs

Yemen's government and the Houthi movement have resumed full-scale fighting after a UN-brokered truce that had largely held since 2022 broke down over the summer, with government forces now waging an offensive they call "Operation Dawn of Yemen" to retake Houthi-held territory, including Sanaa, the AP and The National reported. A November 7 airstrike on the village of al-Shariya near Sanaa killed dozens, according to the Houthi-run health ministry, though the toll has varied sharply between reports and has not been independently confirmed; Houthi forces say they struck back at Saudi Arabia with missiles and drones, and the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted two missiles aimed at Riyadh. The United Nations says more than 1,600 people have been killed and over 300,000 displaced since fighting resumed.

Sources: AP (via CP24), The National

Rains flood tens of thousands of tents sheltering displaced Gazans

Heavy rains flooded at least 70,000 tents housing displaced Palestinians across Gaza within a 12-hour span, Gaza's Government Media Office said, with civil defense crews fielding some 1,500 distress calls, according to the AP and Al Jazeera. The two outlets gave differing casualty figures — one reporting officials who said no deaths had been confirmed, another putting the toll at five, including four children — and aid groups say nearly 400,000 displaced people remain in flood-prone areas as winter sets in. Hamas blamed Israeli restrictions for blocking shelter materials, while Israel's military liaison office COGAT said it had delivered tens of thousands of new tents and tarps to offset the damage.

Sources: AP (via U.S. News), Al Jazeera

Africa

Train collision near Cairo kills at least seven, injures more than 100

Two passenger trains collided on November 8 in Al-Ayyat, on the southern edge of Giza province, after one train struck the rear of another between stations, derailing several carriages, Egyptian health officials said, according to Reuters and Al Jazeera. At least seven people, including a driver, were killed and more than 100 injured, though authorities cautioned the final death toll was still being confirmed. President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi ordered an investigation, and prosecutors said they were examining the technical cause of the crash.

Sources: Reuters (via The Week), Al Jazeera

Somalia's government defies court ruling, swears in its own chief justice

Somalia's government pressed ahead in defiance of last week's Supreme Court ruling that President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud's term had expired, with the Attorney General's office rejecting the decision as having no legal effect and the government swearing in its own pick, Ibrahim Idle Suleiman, as chief justice, according to the AP and allAfrica. Opposition figures say security forces surrounded the court building and forced out the sitting chief justice, Bashe Yusuf Ahmed, who says he no longer feels safe in Mogadishu, though those claims have not been independently verified. Former president Sharif Sheikh Ahmed, now an opposition leader, welcomed the original ruling and urged Mohamud to pursue a consensus-based resolution instead.

Sources: AP (via The Washington Post), allAfrica

Asia

Indonesia's president meets former police chiefs as wiretapping scandal widens

President Prabowo Subianto gathered the sitting police chief and ten former chiefs at his residence this week as investigators pursue a widening illegal wiretapping case that led to the dismissal of his own chief of staff, former police chief Gen. Listyo Sigit Prabowo, according to Antara and Al Jazeera. A government minister said the suspects face charges of illegal wiretapping and data deletion carrying up to 10 years in prison, not treason as initially suggested, after investigators said the surveillance had targeted the president, state officials, journalists and activists. Police denied local reports that two additional former chiefs had been arrested, saying the investigation remains ongoing.

Sources: Antara, Al Jazeera

Taiwan detains party leader accused of running for office on Beijing's orders

Taiwanese prosecutors detained Lin Te-wang, leader of the fringe Taiwan People's Communist Party, on November 8 on suspicion of running for a local borough-chief seat under Chinese Communist Party direction and funding, in violation of Taiwan's Anti-Infiltration Act, according to Focus Taiwan and the AP. Prosecutors allege Lin also directed a party colleague to run for office in Hsinchu and that the two repeatedly took eligible voters on trips to China in a suspected attempt to buy votes; Lin was ordered held incommunicado, while his colleague was released on bail. Lin had previously been acquitted of similar Chinese-infiltration allegations in 2025, and Beijing's Taiwan Affairs Office had not commented as of Monday.

Sources: Focus Taiwan (CNA), AP (via Manila Times)
