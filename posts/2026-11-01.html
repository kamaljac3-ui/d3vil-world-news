Americas

Rubio denies US role in Flávio Bolsonaro's flight as Trump says he can stay indefinitely

Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Saturday the US government "was not involved directly in any of the moves" that led defeated Brazilian presidential candidate Flávio Bolsonaro to leave Brazil for Miami via Argentina, even as President Trump said Bolsonaro could remain in the United States "as long as he wants." Reuters reported Bolsonaro was admitted under a one-year humanitarian parole, a status Trump's remarks do not formally extend, and Rubio said he expects to see Brazil's foreign minister again this month at a G7 meeting in France; Bolsonaro faces investigation in Brazil for alleged corruption, money laundering and tax evasion.

Sources: Reuters (via Internazionale), AP (via Yahoo News)

Bolivia declares national emergency as El Niño drought hits nearly 80,000 families

President Rodrigo Paz signed a decree Saturday declaring a national emergency over an El Niño-driven drought that has affected close to 80,000 families across six of Bolivia's nine departments and killed more than 75,000 head of cattle, with 63 communities already officially in drought and others bracing for flood risk. The decree is meant to speed the release of government funds and personnel and to unlock international aid, as Paz warned the current El Niño cycle "will have very hard blows" of both drought and flooding through early 2027.

Sources: EFE (via Infobae), Al Jazeera

Europe

Poland's president physically confronts judges as Tusk pledges tribunal will "function as it should"

President Karol Nawrocki cut short a state visit to Senegal and pushed past a judge during a confrontation at Poland's Constitutional Tribunal over the weekend, after a court faction aligned with Prime Minister Donald Tusk's government suspended the tribunal's chief justice and installed a judge of its own to run the court. Nawrocki called it a "forcible takeover" of a constitutional body and convened emergency talks with party leaders, while Tusk said he was certain the tribunal "will function as it should" and the European Commission urged Nawrocki to accept the disputed judges; riot police have guarded the Warsaw courthouse since Friday as protesters on both sides gather outside.

Sources: Reuters (via ThePrint), Al Jazeera

Looting and street violence break out in Frankfurt and Cologne after nationwide "Gen Z" protests

Demonstrations by the youth-led "Gen Z regelt" movement against living costs, school funding and planned compulsory military service drew tens of thousands across dozens of German cities on Saturday, largely peacefully by day, before groups smashed shop windows and looted stores along Frankfurt's Zeil shopping street that evening. Police said molotov cocktails were thrown at emergency responders and parts of the city center were sealed off before the unrest eased overnight, with similar flare-ups reported in Cologne, Hamburg and Berlin; a regional police union official said the "unfriendly turn" of the gathering left no choice but to disperse it.

Sources: dpa (via The Korea Times), Hessenschau

Middle East

Houthi missiles hit Aden's airport and presidential palace for the second time in a week

Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis fired ballistic missiles at Aden International Airport and the Maashiq presidential palace on Friday, a police official told AFP, halting air traffic and marking the second strike on the airport and the seventh on the palace since fighting resumed. An AFP journalist and residents reported hearing two blasts, and eyewitnesses said ambulances entered the palace grounds, though officials had not confirmed casualties or damage by Saturday; the attack follows a Wednesday strike on the same airport that wounded two civilians and forced a runway closure.

Sources: AFP (via Asharq Al-Awsat), Saudi Gazette

Iranian negotiators hold third day of Doha talks as Washington weighs resuming strikes

Iranian envoys met Qatari mediators in Doha for a third consecutive day on Sunday, continuing talks aimed at averting a resumption of the US-Israel-Iran war, Reuters reported, after Qatar proposed a seven-day plan of daily trust-building steps between Washington and Tehran. Iran is also weighing a letter from Vice President JD Vance delivered through Qatari channels, while Secretary of State Rubio said President Trump has not decided whether to resume strikes and Iran's army warned that any new war "could spread beyond the Middle East"; Tehran says it will not discuss its nuclear program until Washington meets its conditions, including lifting sanctions.

Sources: Reuters (via Bilyonaryo), The National

Africa

UN chief condemns Sudan drone strikes as Nyala market attack toll disputed between 100 and 188

UN Secretary-General António Guterres condemned a surge in drone strikes across Sudan after an attack on a market in Nyala's Dumaya neighborhood on October 26 killed well over 100 people, with the Sudanese rights group Emergency Lawyers putting the toll at 102 and the RSF-aligned health ministry in the area citing 188 dead and 356 wounded. The Sudanese army has not confirmed or denied responsibility for the strike, which local rights groups and the RSF-run civilian administration attribute to army drones; the UN says such strikes have become the leading cause of civilian deaths in Sudan's civil war.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), Al Jazeera

Congo's Ebola outbreak passes 5,000 deaths as cases top 10,000

The Democratic Republic of Congo's Ebola epidemic, caused by the Bundibugyo strain, has surpassed 10,000 confirmed cases and 5,000 deaths, Reuters reported, making it the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record with no approved vaccine or treatment for this strain. The World Health Organization has confirmed cases spreading beyond Congo into Kenya and Nigeria, while Uganda's smaller, linked outbreak has officially ended; health authorities say the epidemic is spreading faster than any previous Ebola outbreak.

Sources: Reuters (via Seoul Economic Daily), World Health Organization

Asia

North Korea tests cluster-bomb warhead for tactical missiles as Kim oversees launch with daughter

North Korea test-fired a tactical ballistic missile fitted with a new cluster-bomb warhead on Saturday, state media reported, with leader Kim Jong Un and his daughter Ju-ae overseeing the launch and Pyongyang vowing to field several warhead variants by year's end. South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff said it tracked the launch in close coordination with the US, and the test came as the top diplomats of the US, South Korea and Japan met in Atlanta to renew calls for North Korea's complete denuclearization; Secretary of State Rubio said Trump remains open to talks with Kim but that none is scheduled.

Sources: Yonhap (via The Tribune), Korea JoongAng Daily

Thailand flood death toll passes 80 as nearly a million remain affected across 20 provinces

Flooding that began in mid-September across Thailand has killed at least 83 people and continues to affect close to a million people in roughly 20 provinces and Bangkok, according to the country's Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation, up from a toll of 31 confirmed by the interior ministry in early October. Water levels are gradually falling in most areas, officials said, but authorities have continued to warn of further heavy rain in parts of the country even as flood-hit households total more than 375,000.

Sources: Reuters, The Nation (Thailand)
