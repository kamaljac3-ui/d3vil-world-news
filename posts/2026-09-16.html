Americas

Brazil's Supreme Court freezes its investigation of Justice Alexandre de Moraes for up to 90 days

Brazil's Supreme Federal Tribunal postponed Tuesday's vote on whether to open a formal investigation into fellow Justice Alexandre de Moraes, over allegations he advised a banker accused of running a multibillion-dollar fraud scheme, after Justice Flávio Dino invoked a "pedido de vista" — a procedural request letting a single justice hold the case for up to 90 days. The delay pushes any decision to December, with Moraes himself filing a lengthy denial calling the probe politically motivated, in a court crisis unfolding weeks ahead of Brazil's October presidential election.

Sources: TradingView (Reuters), The Rio Times

EU offers Canada a first-of-its-kind "associate member" status as Ottawa drifts from Washington

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen told the European Parliament in Strasbourg the EU is "opening the door" for Canada to become the bloc's first-ever associate member — a status not currently defined in EU treaties — and said the two sides will deepen cooperation on manufacturing, AI, critical minerals and energy. Lawmakers gave her a standing ovation before she embraced Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney, who was in the chamber for the address; the proposal comes as US tariffs continue pushing Ottawa toward closer ties with Europe.

Sources: CBC, CNBC

Europe

Russian drone strike on a passenger bus kills five near Ukraine's front line

A Russian FPV drone struck a passenger bus in the Nikopol district of Dnipropetrovsk Oblast early Wednesday, killing five people at the scene and injuring at least seven more, regional governor Oleksandr Hanzha said. The strike was part of a wider wave of attacks across Ukraine overnight, including a separate strike that started a fire at a warehouse in Kyiv, where residents reported hearing explosions.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Euronews

Middle East

A Gaza building collapse kills 19 as fragile truce conditions leave residents in unstable housing

A building housing roughly 100 people, including about 50 children, collapsed in Gaza City early Wednesday while residents slept, killing 19 people — at least 13 of them women and children — and injuring 25, according to Gaza's health ministry and the UN's humanitarian coordinator for the Palestinian territories. The structure had been weakened by earlier Israeli strikes; rescuers continued digging through the rubble for others still missing. The collapse underscored how dangerous housing conditions remain for Gaza's population even as a ceasefire between Israel and Hamas holds, though strikes have reportedly continued to kill people since the truce began.

Sources: AP, Local10 (AP)

Africa

Gold mine collapse in Sudan's West Kordofan kills at least 60 miners

Shafts began collapsing Tuesday at the Al-Zaraa gold mine near El-Nahud in Sudan's West Kordofan state, and by the next morning rescuers had pulled 60 bodies from the rubble, with more miners feared still trapped. Survivor Khair al-Sayyed Jabara told AFP after an overnight rescue effort that "we don't know how many more are still inside," while local sources told Reuters the high toll reflected the absence of basic safety procedures at the informal mining site — a large-scale rescue operation is effectively impossible in the region, which is under Rapid Support Forces control amid Sudan's ongoing civil war.

Sources: Arab News (AFP), Asharq Al-Awsat (AFP)

Asia

India summons Pakistan's envoy after warships collide in the Arabian Sea — the first such incident in 15 years

India's Ministry of External Affairs summoned Pakistan's Charge d'Affaires in New Delhi on Wednesday and lodged a formal protest after a Pakistani naval vessel, shadowing an Indian warship on a routine surveillance mission in the North Arabian Sea, closed in at high speed and collided with it on the evening of September 15. India said the Pakistani ship's maneuver violated Article 10 of a 1991 bilateral agreement requiring naval vessels of the two countries to keep at least three nautical miles between them, and called the conduct "unacceptable and unprofessional"; no major damage or injuries were reported. It is the first collision between Indian and Pakistani warships since 2011, and the second close encounter between the two navies in the Arabian Sea in six months.

Sources: IANS, The Tribune
