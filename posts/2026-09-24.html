Americas

Venezuela's Delcy Rodríguez makes first presidential UN address, credits Trump with reopening ties

Venezuela's acting president, Delcy Rodríguez, addressed the UN General Assembly for the first time since taking power, telling delegates elections would be held once Venezuelans determine "the right moment" and that her government has opened talks with opposition sectors. She thanked President Trump for his "readiness" to resume relations with Caracas, the first Venezuela-US presidential-level contact since 2009 and Rodríguez's first trip to the US since the American military removed her predecessor, Nicolás Maduro, from the country.

Sources: WFMZ (AP), NPR

Bolivia's unions weigh nationwide strike after diesel subsidy scrapped under IMF deal

Bolivian President Rodrigo Paz eliminated the country's last diesel subsidy last week, hours after Congress approved a $1.9 billion IMF loan, pushing diesel prices up roughly 83%. Drivers' unions meeting in Santa Cruz this week are deciding how hard to push back, with a national 24-hour vehicle withdrawal planned for Monday and warnings the action could escalate into an indefinite strike.

Sources: WSLS (AP), Rio Times

Europe

51 nations urge Russia to accept ceasefire in Ukraine; Lavrov says Moscow won't pause fighting for talks

More than 50 UN member states, in a joint statement delivered by Danish Foreign Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen, called on Russia to accept a full, immediate and unconditional ceasefire in Ukraine, noting Kyiv agreed to such terms back in March 2025. Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov rejected pausing the fighting for negotiations after meeting US Secretary of State Marco Rubio, though the two sides discussed a more limited ceasefire covering energy and grain-related targets.

Sources: US News (AP), Cyprus Mail

Russian drones kill 2, wound dozens in Kyiv hours before Zelensky's UN address

Russia pounded Kyiv with drones in daylight strikes Wednesday, killing two people and wounding more than 40, hours before Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky addressed the UN General Assembly. The attack sparked fires and damaged buildings across the capital and elsewhere in Ukraine, part of intensified strikes even as Kyiv and Moscow traded rival ceasefire proposals at the UN.

Sources: KSAT (AP), Washington Post

Middle East

Nearly a year into Gaza ceasefire, more than 1,400 Palestinians reported killed by continued strikes

Almost a year after Israel and Hamas reached a ceasefire, Israeli forces continue near-daily strikes on Gaza that Palestinian health officials say have killed at least 1,400 people, mostly civilians, since the truce took effect; Israel's military says four of its own soldiers have also been killed. The latest strike Wednesday killed two men in Khan Younis, which Israel said targeted a Hamas finance official, as both sides trade blame over ceasefire violations and reconstruction remains stalled.

Sources: US News (AP), NPR

Syria's al-Sharaa demands Israeli pullback from Golan at UN, cites 500 strikes since Assad's fall

Syrian President Ahmed al-Sharaa told the UN General Assembly that Israel has carried out nearly 500 air and artillery strikes and more than 1,200 incursions into Syrian territory since Bashar al-Assad's fall in December 2024, detaining around 300 people. He called for Israel to withdraw to the lines held before that upheaval under the 1974 disengagement agreement, while saying Syria remains focused on reconstruction and rebuilding international ties.

Sources: ABC News (AP), Al Jazeera

Africa

Morocco's PAM wins most seats in parliamentary election amid record-low turnout

Morocco's Authenticity and Modernity Party won the largest bloc of seats — 97 of 395 — in Wednesday's parliamentary election, ahead of coalition partners RNI (66) and Istiqlal (65), as the outgoing National Rally of Independents-led government suffered losses. Turnout was just 38%, the lowest since 2007, and coalition talks are now expected to determine Morocco's next government.

Sources: US News (AP), Al Jazeera

Tigray forces seize Ethiopian airports as fighting resumes, rebel leader declares "the beginning of the war"

Fighters from the Tigray People's Liberation Front seized control of three airports in Ethiopia's Tigray region, including Mekelle's, on Wednesday and clashed with federal forces, days after seven armed groups announced an alliance to topple Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's government. TPLF vice president Amanuel Assefa told AFP the offensive was "the beginning of the war," saying it was a response to federal airstrikes, as Ethiopian Airlines suspended flights to the region and fighting spread into neighboring Afar.

Sources: Euronews (AFP), Africanews (AFP)

Asia

Trump, Xi extend US-China trade truce to January as leaders open Washington summit

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced the US and China have extended their trade truce, under which both sides scaled back tariffs and paused new trade restrictions, until January 10, as Chinese President Xi Jinping opened a three-day state visit to Washington. Xi is pressing Trump to permanently halt arms sales to Taiwan and ease export limits on advanced chips, while Washington is seeking Beijing's help pressuring Iran, China's top oil buyer, amid its ongoing war with the US.

Sources: PBS News (AP), The National

Pakistan locks down Islamabad, detains Imran Khan's sisters ahead of planned PTI march

Pakistani authorities blocked major highways into Islamabad with shipping containers and deployed more than 23,000 security personnel ahead of a march planned for Sunday by supporters of jailed former Prime Minister Imran Khan's party. Three of Khan's sisters and hundreds of party activists have been detained in recent days, and Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi has warned the government will prevent any group from entering the capital.

Sources: US News (AP), Al Jazeera
