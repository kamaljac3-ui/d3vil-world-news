Americas

Hurricane Simon pounds Mexico's Pacific coast, forcing evacuations in resort towns

Hurricane Simon tore along Mexico's Pacific coast as a major Category 3 storm on Sunday, its eye staying just offshore while bringing catastrophic wind and storm surge to Jalisco, Colima and Michoacán. Mexico's navy deployed more than 4,600 personnel and moved residents from low-lying areas as flooding hit resort towns including Puerto Vallarta; local officials reported one death, from a collapsed wall in Chilpancingo, Guerrero, and two injuries in Acapulco. The US National Hurricane Center warned the storm's rain bands would keep bringing life-threatening flooding and mudslides into Monday.

Sources: WBIR (AP), NPR

Argentina's deadline for UK to halt Falklands oil project expires without resolution

A two-week ultimatum from Argentine President Javier Milei demanding the UK halt the Sea Lion oil project north of the Falkland Islands expired around Monday without Britain agreeing to stop the work. Milei has ordered arbitration under the Law of the Sea convention and plans to seek emergency measures from the International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea, while the UK Foreign Office rejected the arbitration and said the project falls under the islands' own legislation. Oilfield services giant Halliburton has already told Argentine authorities it will not work on Sea Lion amid the pressure campaign, Reuters reported.

Sources: Reuters (via TradingView), MercoPress

Europe

Trump announces Russia-Ukraine "energy ceasefire"; Zelenskyy says Kyiv learned of it from the media

President Trump said Sunday that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an immediate ceasefire on strikes against energy infrastructure, but offered no details of the arrangement. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukraine "didn't know about it" and only learned from media reports, adding Kyiv would honor such a truce if Russia does the same; Ukrainian officials said Russian strikes hit an energy facility near Kharkiv and a gas production site in the hours after Trump's announcement. The Kremlin did not confirm a deal, saying only that "contacts are ongoing."

Sources: PBS NewsHour (AP), CBS News

Brussels march against budget cuts draws 30,000, turns violent near main train station

About 30,000 people joined a union-led march through Brussels on Friday against Prime Minister Bart De Wever's plan to cut roughly €10 billion from Belgium's budget, police said. A smaller group of masked protesters broke off near Brussels-Midi station, throwing rocks and fireworks and setting fires that disrupted public transport and air travel, prompting police to fire tear gas and make roughly 20 arrests, mostly of minors; organizers said the breakaway group was not part of the peaceful union march. De Wever's coalition is working toward a deal on the cuts by October 13.

Sources: The Washington Post, Euronews

Middle East

Houthi missile attack on Riyadh airport kills at least 14 in deadliest strike yet on Saudi capital

A Houthi ballistic missile struck Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport over the weekend, killing at least 14 people and wounding more than 300, according to Reuters and AP figures cited by regional outlets; it was the third Houthi strike on Saudi airports in a week and the deadliest yet. Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Pakistan and Turkey condemned the attack, and the UN's special envoy for Yemen called strikes on civilian airports a "grave escalation." Flights were suspended for safety inspections, and the US, UK, Spain and Canada have warned citizens against using the airport.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Gulf News

Iran calls latest US proposal to end war "insufficient" as Hormuz attacks intensify

Iran's Supreme National Security Council said Sunday that Washington's latest proposal to end the war and fully reopen the Strait of Hormuz was "insufficient," as mediated efforts to end the conflict remain deadlocked and attacks around the strait pick up again. US Central Command said its forces disabled the Panama-flagged cargo vessel Ocean Molica after it ignored warnings while trying to breach the blockade of Iranian ports, Reuters reported. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu convened security officials amid growing concern the conflict could escalate further.

Sources: PBS NewsHour (AP), Al Jazeera

Africa

At least 139 dead after migrant boat capsizes off Djibouti en route to Yemen

A boat carrying around 305 migrants from Djibouti toward Yemen capsized near Obock overnight on October 8-9, killing at least 139 people, the UN's International Organization for Migration said, with dozens more still missing. IOM's Djibouti chief, Alexandre Coissac, attributed the sinking to overcrowding and said the confirmed toll could still rise as more bodies are identified; survivors, more than 80 in number, were all Ethiopian nationals. The disaster is among the deadliest on the Horn of Africa migration route in recent years.

Sources: ABC News Australia, The National

Airstrike on Sudan-Egypt border gold mine kills miners; toll disputed between 13 and 72

An airstrike hit an artisanal gold mine in the remote Border Triangle area where Sudan, Egypt and Libya meet on Saturday, with casualty figures sharply disputed: AP reported at least 13 miners killed and 28 wounded, citing the Sudan Doctors Network, while the Sudanese rights group Emergency Lawyers put the toll at 72 dead, a figure also reported by AFP and Al Jazeera. Responsibility for the strike is unconfirmed; AP said it was not immediately clear who carried it out, while Emergency Lawyers accused the Egyptian air force, which has previously described a "large-scale operation" against illegal gold mining in the area. Not yet independently confirmed.

Sources: AP (via Yahoo News), Al Jazeera

Asia

Pakistan imposes emergency rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, chief minister reportedly detained

Pakistan's federal cabinet moved to impose emergency and governor's rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa on Sunday, and President Asif Ali Zardari approved the measure under Articles 232 and 234 of the constitution, dismissing the provincial government led by Imran Khan's PTI party. The prime minister's office accused the provincial administration of "deliberate indifference" in fighting terrorism; PTI officials said Chief Minister Sohail Afridi and the party's provincial president were taken into custody, though police had not independently confirmed the detentions. The move follows days of a PTI-led march toward Islamabad demanding Khan's release, and PTI called the emergency "undemocratic."

Sources: Dawn, Al Jazeera

North Korea fires multiple ballistic missiles in show of force after South Korea's hypersonic test

North Korea fired several ballistic missiles toward the sea off its east coast starting around 9:20 a.m. Monday, South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff said, with one missile traveling more than 900 kilometers before landing in the Sea of Japan. A JCS spokesperson called the launch an apparent show of force, coming days after South Korea disclosed its first test of a hypersonic glide vehicle and weeks after North Korea tested its own hypersonic intermediate-range missile. No damage or injuries were reported.

Sources: Al Jazeera, CBS News
