Americas

White House proposes new tariffs on China, the EU and a dozen other trading partners

The Trump administration unveiled a new round of proposed tariffs on November 25 targeting 14 trading partners, including the European Union's 27 members, China, India, Japan, South Korea and several Southeast Asian economies, citing alleged subsidized overproduction. The plan would add a 20% tariff on Chinese goods on top of existing duties, with an official warning the rate could rise to 40% if Beijing does not honor an existing trade truce, while other partners would face rates between 10% and 18%. China's commerce ministry rejected the tariffs' rationale, and the measure is expected to face legal challenges after the Supreme Court struck down an earlier set of Trump-era tariffs this year.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), Washington Post

Europe

EU leaders open Brussels summit to fight over long-term budget and a new Ukraine loan

European Council President António Costa opened a two-day summit in Brussels on November 26 aimed at breaking a deadlock over the bloc's roughly €1.7 trillion long-term budget for 2028-2034 and a proposed €45 billion loan to fund Ukraine through next year, AP reported. Slovakia, Hungary, the Czech Republic and Bulgaria have objected to joining the loan, and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz has threatened to veto the entire budget unless all 27 member states back Ukraine's funding; leaders were not expected to reach a final deal before the summit's second day. Not yet independently confirmed whether a deal will be reached.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), France 24

Middle East

Iraq orders federal takeover of Kurdish oil fields and border posts, thousands protest in Erbil

Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi ordered federal authorities to take control of border crossings and oilfields in four Kurdish-administered provinces this week, a day after Iraq's Federal Supreme Court affirmed Baghdad's authority over borders, customs and the oil sector even inside the Kurdistan region, AP and regional outlets reported. Thousands of demonstrators gathered in Erbil on November 26 to protest what Kurdish regional officials call an unconstitutional move, and Iraq's top state authorities agreed to hold a national dialogue on the dispute on December 1.

Sources: AP (via WTOP), Rudaw

Israeli strike kills senior Hamas security official and three family members in Gaza City

An Israeli airstrike on a Gaza City apartment early on November 25 killed Mohammed Abu al-Ouf, identified by Gaza's Hamas-run interior ministry and by AFP as the internal security service's director for northern Gaza, along with his wife and two daughters, Reuters and AFP reported. Israel's military said it had targeted a "Hamas terrorist," and the strike was the second killing of a senior Hamas internal-security official in two days; Reuters reported Israeli forces now control roughly two-thirds of the Gaza Strip, up from just over half when October's ceasefire took hold.

Sources: Reuters (via Yahoo News), AFP (via Asharq Al-Awsat)

Africa

Fuel tanker fire on Cairo ring road kills at least 31, injures 21

A tire blowout sent a fuel tanker into a concrete barrier on Cairo's Ring Road in Giza's Warraq district on November 25, overturning the truck and igniting a fire that spread to more than two dozen vehicles, Egypt's Health Ministry said, according to AP. The blaze killed at least 31 people and injured 21 others, some with severe burns, and Egypt's Public Prosecutor's office said DNA testing was underway to identify victims as the death toll was still considered preliminary.

Sources: AP (via The Hill), Gulf News

South African court sets aside impeachment-linked report against President Ramaphosa

South Africa's Western Cape High Court set aside in full a parliamentary panel's report on the 2020 Phala Phala farm robbery on November 25, ruling the panel had misunderstood its own function and relied partly on hearsay evidence, Reuters reported. The ruling also voids the steps Parliament's impeachment committee had taken based on that report, though it stops short of clearing Ramaphosa of the underlying allegations; the African Transformation Movement, which brought the original complaint, is considering an appeal.

Sources: Reuters (via News24), Daily Maverick

Drone strikes blamed on Sudan's RSF kill civilians in Blue Nile state, reigniting dispute with Ethiopia

Drone strikes hit residential areas of Al-Damazin, the capital of Sudan's Blue Nile state, on November 25; a medical source told AFP at least 14 people including children were killed, while Reuters cited a Sudanese rights group's count of 19 civilians dead and 20 wounded across two towns. Sudan's army has accused Rapid Support Forces paramilitaries of launching the strikes from Ethiopian territory, a claim Addis Ababa denies, and Khartoum has referred the allegation to the UN Security Council and the African Union; more than 100,000 people have fled into Al-Damazin as the RSF advances in the area, according to the UN.

Sources: AFP (via Arab News), Reuters (via Yahoo News)

Asia

Kim Jong Un calls for wider deployment of North Korea's tactical nuclear weapons

North Korean leader Kim Jong Un toured nuclear material production and weapons research facilities and called for the country to expand its use of tactical nuclear weapons across multiple military domains, state media KCNA reported, according to Reuters. Kim warned that any attempt to deny North Korea's status as a nuclear-armed state would be treated as "an open declaration of war," and South Korea's Defence Ministry said it was taking the stated intent seriously; KCNA-released photos reportedly showed equipment analysts said resembled uranium-enrichment centrifuges and miniaturized warheads.

Sources: Reuters (via Daily Maverick), Al Jazeera

China warns Japan and Australia against deepening defense ties with the Philippines

China's defense ministry on November 26 told Japan to stop "stirring up trouble" over South China Sea disputes, Reuters reported, after Japan pledged this month to supply the Philippines with radar systems and drones to boost coastal surveillance. Beijing separately warned, per AFP, that a new Australia-Philippines defense agreement covering joint maritime patrols and information-sharing could fuel regional tensions and said it would respond firmly to what it called provocations; the warnings follow repeated naval confrontations this year between China and the Philippines at Scarborough Shoal and Second Thomas Shoal.

Sources: Reuters (via WSAU), AFP (via Hong Kong Free Press)
