Americas

Guatemala declares state of emergency in two departments after a lawmaker's assassination

Guatemalan authorities decreed a 30-day state of emergency in the eastern departments of Zacapa and Izabal on Saturday, expanding security forces' powers to disperse gatherings, make arrests and enter homes without warrants, according to Reuters. The move follows a November 6 ambush in Río Hondo that killed lawmaker Julio César Portillo and three police officers; authorities have not identified suspects, but the decree comes days after Washington designated several criminal groups operating in Zacapa, which it ties to Mexican drug-trafficking cartels, as terrorist organizations.

Sources: Reuters (via Econotimes), Devdiscourse

Chile's Kast sinks to 24% approval as protests continue despite ending the truckers' strike

Chilean President José Antonio Kast's government struck a deal this week to cut diesel prices and freeze fuel costs, ending a days-long truckers' strike that had blockaded highways, but AP reports his approval has fallen to just 24% as separate protests continue in Santiago over living costs and a decree that cut the sentences of 56 prisoners. Police used water cannon against demonstrators angered by the sentencing decree, which Kast has since called an "error" while standing by the official who signed it.

Sources: NPR (AP), UPI

Europe

More than 100,000 rally across France for a fifth straight Saturday against cost of living and a police bill

France's interior ministry estimated roughly 106,000 people demonstrated nationwide on Saturday against the cost of living, public-service cuts and a proposed police bill, with student unions putting the turnout far higher, according to France 24. Authorities said 219 people were detained nationwide, and a television crew was attacked and had its camera stolen in Lyon, as roughly 70,000 police and gendarmes were deployed for the fifth consecutive weekend of unrest, Al Jazeera reported.

Sources: France 24, Al Jazeera

US envoys broker plan for war's largest prisoner swap as Russian strikes keep hitting Ukraine

US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow this week, then flew to Dublin to brief Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky, announcing a planned prisoner-of-war exchange that Kushner said could become the largest of the war; Zelensky said Europe would have a seat at the next round of talks, according to Reuters. The diplomacy came as Russian strikes on Zaporizhzhia wounded at least 22 people on Saturday, part of a broader barrage that killed at least seven people and injured 80 across Ukraine over the prior day, Ukrainian officials said.

Sources: Moscow Times (Reuters), Kyiv Independent

Middle East

Iran claims second drone strike on US-used radar base in Bahrain in three days

Iran's Revolutionary Guard said Sunday that its drones struck a US air-defense radar station at Bahrain's Riffa base, which hosts US helicopters, prompting Bahrain's first nationwide threat alert in months and residents reporting explosions before dawn, according to Al Jazeera. Bahrain's defense force said its air defenses intercepted and destroyed the incoming drones; the claim came two days after a similar Iranian strike claim that US Central Command said involved two drones it shot down with no injuries or damage.

Sources: Al Jazeera, The National

Houthis renew missile and drone attacks on Riyadh as Saudi coalition intercepts them

Saudi Arabia's coalition said Sunday it intercepted two Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh and three drones targeting the kingdom's oil-rich Eastern Province, according to Reuters, as the Yemeni rebel group continues escalating attacks on Saudi airports, ports and oil facilities. Saudi authorities say at least 26 people have been killed in Houthi drone and missile attacks inside the kingdom since late September, even as Saudi-led airstrikes on Houthi targets inside Yemen have intensified in response.

Sources: Business Standard (Reuters), Gulf News

Africa

Islamic State-linked gunmen kill at least 44 civilians in eastern Congo amid Ebola response

Fighters from the Allied Democratic Forces, a Ugandan rebel group affiliated with the Islamic State, killed at least 44 civilians in a string of attacks on villages in Ituri province this week, hacking many victims to death and burning health facilities, according to AFP. Three health workers were among the dead, and the World Health Organization's director-general, Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, said the surrounding instability is "one of the greatest obstacles" to containing the region's ongoing Ebola outbreak.

Sources: Macau Business (AFP), Africanews

South Sudan postpones first-ever national elections to February 2027

South Sudan's electoral commission said Saturday it is pushing the country's first national elections since independence from December 22 to February 11, 2027, citing the need for more time to register voters, according to the Associated Press. The delay comes amid growing violence and a political feud between President Salva Kiir and deposed former vice president Riek Machar; Yasmin Sooka, who chairs the UN's human rights commission for South Sudan, called the move "a constitutional coup through legislative reforms."

Sources: WRAL (AP), Al Jazeera

Asia

Myanmar airstrike on displacement camp kills 12, including a child

A Myanmar military airstrike hit the Byar La camp for people displaced by the country's civil war, in Rakhine State's Ponnagyun township, on Friday evening, killing 12 people and injuring 18 others, including a five-year-old child, according to AFP. Witnesses said a jet dropped bombs without warning on a site residents had considered safe, a day after a separate strike hit an area near a school in nearby Mrauk-U.

Sources: Daily Independent (AFP), Arab News

Trump, Xi and Putin set first-ever three-way summit in Shenzhen

The White House confirmed Friday that President Trump will meet Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen on November 19, calling it the first joint meeting of the three nuclear powers' leaders, according to the Associated Press. US officials said the agenda includes reducing nuclear arsenals and the war in Iran but not Russia's war in Ukraine, while China's foreign ministry said it would be "neither fair, reasonable nor realistic" to expect Beijing to join broader nuclear disarmament talks.

Sources: AP (via Yahoo News), France 24
