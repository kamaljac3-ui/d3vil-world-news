Americas

Paraguay's president resigns as bank-collapse fallout spreads

President Santiago Peña announced his resignation Thursday in a video posted to social media, saying he was stepping down to protect the country's democratic institutions after the central bank's seizure of Ueno Bank left roughly half the country's bank customers unable to reach their accounts, the AP reported. Peña, a shareholder in Ueno's parent company until last year, apologized but stopped short of accepting responsibility for the collapse; lawmakers from both wings of his own Colorado Party had signaled support for impeachment before he quit. Congress must still act on the resignation, after which Vice President Pedro Alliana would take over under the constitution.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), Reuters (via NBC News)

Wall Street posts its worst day in over a year as an AI-stock selloff erases $2.5 trillion

The S&P 500 fell 4.4% and the Dow lost more than 1,800 points on Thursday in Wall Street's worst session since April 2025, as a selloff centered on chip and AI-linked stocks wiped out roughly $2.5 trillion in market value, the AP reported. The Nasdaq, which fell 5.5% and entered correction territory, led the slide; coverage tied the rout to investor concern that this week's Democratic takeover of Congress could slow AI-infrastructure spending, though that explanation remains analysts' reading rather than a confirmed cause. The drop erased the prior day's rally, when markets had posted their best day in over a year following the midterm results.

Sources: AP (via Yahoo Finance), CNBC

Europe

France sees its largest day of protests in years over wages and living costs

Unions representing both public- and private-sector workers led a nationwide strike on Thursday that drew between 575,000 people, by the interior ministry's count, and 1.6 million, by organizers' count, across more than 200 demonstrations, Al Jazeera reported. Protesters demanded higher wages, tax changes and improved public services amid anger over inflation and the government's budget plans; authorities said at least 448 people were detained, and police used tear gas on marchers in Lyon. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu said he would hold a "social conference" with unions and employers on November 9, though some protest groups have called for further action this weekend.

Sources: Al Jazeera, ABC News

Belgium's king rejects the prime minister's resignation, orders budget talks to resume

King Philippe declined Prime Minister Bart De Wever's resignation on Friday and instructed him to resume negotiations over roughly €10 billion in budget savings needed to bring Belgium's deficit under the EU's 3% limit, the AP reported. De Wever had offered to step down on Monday after his five-party coalition failed to agree on the measures, and has acknowledged Belgium will start next year under an emergency "provisional twelfths" budget system while talks continue. The king asked De Wever to report back by November 16.

Sources: AP (via WTOP), The Brussels Times

Middle East

Israel's Herzog to name a government-forming candidate Sunday as Eisenkot leads Netanyahu

President Isaac Herzog said Thursday that factions representing 67 of Israel's 120 lawmakers had recommended former military chief Gadi Eisenkot to form the next government, against 46 for Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, and that he would announce his decision on Sunday, Al Jazeera reported. Herzog cautioned that the recommendation count does not guarantee either man can actually assemble a governing majority, citing "mutual vetoes" among the parties; the Arab-led Joint List backed Eisenkot but is expected to support him only from outside a formal coalition. Whoever receives the mandate will have 28 days, with a possible 14-day extension, to form a government.

Sources: Al Jazeera, The Times of Israel

Iran and the US remain locked in a standoff over the Strait of Hormuz

Iran's Revolutionary Guard said Friday that two more oil tankers struck mines and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz, the latest in a string of such claims this week, while the US continued enforcing a naval blockade of Iranian ports and escorting tankers through the waterway, CBS News reported, citing a tally from the UK's maritime security agency UKMTO of at least five vessel incidents since Sunday. Iran's foreign ministry says the strait cannot reopen until Washington lifts the blockade and meets a list of other conditions; President Trump spoke by phone with Qatar's emir on Friday about easing the standoff. Iran's mine-strike claims have not been independently verified.

Sources: CBS News (citing Reuters/UKMTO), Middle East Eye

Africa

Sudan's drone war keeps killing civilians in Kordofan and Darfur

Sudan's army and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces have each continued near-daily drone strikes this week that killed civilians across Kordofan and Darfur, including a strike regional monitors attributed to the RSF on a fuel station in South Kordofan state that reportedly killed at least seven people and has since been reported higher, Sudan Tribune and Darfur24 reported. Casualty figures for this week's individual strikes vary by source and have not been independently confirmed. The strikes extend a broader pattern Al Jazeera and AFP have documented for months, in which more than 160 people were reported killed in a single week of drone attacks across Sudan in October.

Sources: Sudan Tribune, Al Jazeera

Fighting in Ethiopia's Moyale region pushes more than 50,000 refugees into Kenya

Clashes between forces linked to Ethiopia's Oromia and Somali regional states around the border town of Moyale have displaced more than 50,000 people into northern Kenya, up from roughly 36,000 in late October, Al Jazeera reported, citing AP and Reuters reporting. Kenya's police chief ordered additional officers to the border region this week, and Kenyan officials said they are engaging Ethiopia's federal government to resolve the fighting, which Ethiopian media say was triggered by a dispute over flying the Somali regional flag in an Oromia-claimed district. An Oromo advocacy group has alleged Somali regional forces and allied fighters are behind a wider campaign against civilians; Somali regional officials have not responded to the claim.

Sources: AP/Reuters (via Al Jazeera), allAfrica

Asia

Hong Kong sentences Joshua Wong to 10 more years in a second national security case

Hong Kong's High Court sentenced pro-democracy activist Joshua Wong to nearly 10 more years in prison on Thursday after he pleaded guilty to conspiring to collude with foreign forces, the AP reported. Prosecutors centered the case on Wong's pre-2020 lobbying for foreign sanctions against Hong Kong officials; two months of the new sentence will run alongside the roughly two months remaining on his earlier national-security conviction, keeping him in custody for years longer than previously expected. China's foreign ministry said Wong pleaded guilty "in the face of overwhelming evidence," while the US consulate in Hong Kong called him "unjustly imprisoned."

Sources: AP (via NBC News), NPR

Bangladesh's garment-worker protests over wages stretch into a fifth day

Clashes between police and garment workers demanding higher pay continued in Bangladesh this week, with hundreds of factories near Dhaka still shut despite a government wage-hike pledge, the AP reported. Workers are seeking a monthly minimum of 30,000 taka, more than double the roughly 13,000 taka many currently earn; at least 57 people have been arrested since the unrest began Monday, and more than 10,000 police officers have been deployed to Dhaka's Mirpur area alone. Bangladesh's garment sector employs about 4 million workers, mostly women, and is the country's largest export industry.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), The Star (Malaysia)
