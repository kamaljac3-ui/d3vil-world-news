Americas

US import ban on nearly $1 billion of Canadian goods takes effect

A US ban on imports of Canadian alcohol, dairy products and motorcycles worth close to $1 billion took effect Tuesday, sharply escalating the trade dispute between the two countries. The ban followed Canada's own retaliatory tariffs taking effect hours earlier, which themselves answered 50% US tariffs imposed last month on roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods after negotiations broke down.

Sources: ABC News (AP), CBC News

UN Security Council extends Haiti anti-gang force mandate for six months

The UN Security Council voted Tuesday to extend the mandate of the Gang Suppression Force in Haiti to March 31, 2027, with the US- and Panama-sponsored resolution passing 12-0 as China, Russia and Pakistan abstained over concerns about its effectiveness. About 1,500 of the force's planned 5,500 troops are currently deployed against gangs that control much of Port-au-Prince, and the extension is meant to help secure conditions for Haiti's planned constitutional referendum and elections.

Sources: US News (AP), Al Jazeera

Europe

Russia strikes Kyiv's power grid overnight in what officials call a renewed winter campaign

Russia hit Kyiv's energy infrastructure overnight Wednesday with a combined missile and drone attack that killed at least four people, including a child, and cut power in parts of the capital and surrounding region. Ukraine's prime minister said the attack, which struck a hydroelectric plant, a thermal plant, a substation and a data center, was the largest of its kind since the end of last winter's heating season, and officials said it may mark the start of a renewed Russian effort to "weaponize winter."

Sources: US News (AP), Kyiv Independent

Norway's parliament opens public hearings into Epstein ties to politicians, diplomats

Norway's parliament began public hearings Wednesday into ties between Norwegian diplomats and politicians and late financier Jeffrey Epstein, summoning 11 current and former foreign and development ministers, including Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre, who has said he had no relationship with Epstein. The hearings follow the US Justice Department's release of Epstein-related files in January that named a former Norwegian prime minister and a former World Economic Forum chief among those with documented ties to him.

Sources: CP24 (AP), Euronews

Middle East

Iran weighs US counterproposal on Strait of Hormuz ceasefire plan

Iranian officials said Wednesday that Tehran had received a US counterproposal to its offer of a seven-day ceasefire and phased reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, days after President Trump had publicly rejected Iran's original terms. Under Iran's proposal, Washington would lift its naval blockade of Iranian ports, waive sanctions on Iranian oil sales and release roughly $12 billion in frozen Iranian assets in exchange for a ceasefire covering Lebanon and Yemen and the strait's reopening; mediators said indirect talks were continuing despite the public rejection.

Sources: PBS NewsHour (AP), Al Jazeera

Israeli strikes kill five in Gaza in two separate attacks

Israeli strikes killed at least five Palestinians in Gaza City on Wednesday, medics said, with an airstrike on a vehicle in the Tel al-Hawa neighborhood killing four and wounding 14, and an earlier strike on an apartment elsewhere in the city killing a woman. The Israeli military did not immediately comment on either strike.

Sources: US News (Reuters), Al Arabiya English

Africa

Islamic State-linked ambushes kill more than 50 Niger security forces

More than 50 members of Niger's security forces were killed in two separate attacks in the west of the country, sources said, after gendarmes were ambushed near Abala-Filingué and a second attack at Birni-N'Konni killed more than 30 soldiers. Islamic State's Sahel affiliate claimed responsibility for both ambushes, part of a worsening jihadist insurgency across Niger and its neighbors.

Sources: US News (Reuters), TimesLIVE

Ethiopian rebel offensive pushes into Afar and Amhara, threatening key trade corridor

A coalition of Tigrayan forces and the allied Fano militia expanded their offensive against Ethiopia's federal government this week, with fighting reported in the towns of Abala and Erebti in Afar and federal forces firing heavy weapons from a camp near Lalibela in Amhara. The rebel advance has raised alarm in Addis Ababa because it threatens the corridor to Djibouti's port, through which nearly all of Ethiopia's international trade passes, deepening fears the country's renewed internal conflict could widen further.

Sources: Euronews (AFP), Al Jazeera

Asia

Myanmar military airstrike on Rakhine market kills at least 50

A Myanmar military jet dropped bombs on a wholesale market in the town of Kyauktaw in opposition-held Rakhine state on Monday, killing at least 50 people and wounding 58 more, the Arakan Army rebel group and a local aid worker said. The dead included vendors, shoppers and workers handling cargo, making it Rakhine's deadliest single attack since a hospital bombing killed at least 30 people in December.

Sources: US News (AP), Al Jazeera

North Korea preparing to send 10,000 more troops to Russia, Zelenskiy says

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said Monday that more than 8,000 North Korean troops are already on Russian territory and that another 10,000 are being selected for training and deployment, citing new intelligence data. North Korea has sent an estimated 14,000 to 15,000 troops to support Russia's war effort since 2024, according to Ukrainian and South Korean estimates, with most deployed in the Kursk region.

Sources: US News (Reuters), Korea Times
