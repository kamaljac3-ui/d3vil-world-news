Americas

Bolivia ends diesel subsidies as Congress ratifies $1.9 billion IMF loan

Bolivia's Congress approved a $1.9 billion loan agreement with the International Monetary Fund on Friday, and President Rodrigo Paz followed hours later by ordering an immediate end to diesel subsidies to meet a key condition of the deal. The price of diesel jumped roughly 83% overnight under the decree, and the country's main labor federation warned the move could reignite the road blockades that paralyzed much of Bolivia in June and July.

Sources: KSAT (AP), Euronews

UK calls Argentina's crackdown on Falklands-linked firms "unacceptable" as sanctions bill advances

Argentina's Congress is weighing a bill from President Javier Milei that would toughen penalties on companies and individuals operating in the Falkland Islands' natural-resource sector, part of a wider push that has already opened sanctions proceedings against roughly 60 firms tied to offshore oil exploration there. Britain's minister for the overseas territories, Uma Kumaran, said Friday the measures were “unacceptable” and carried “no legal justification,” reasserting the islands' right to develop their own resources.

Sources: US News (Reuters), MercoPress

Europe

Russian glide bomb kills two city officials in Sumy apartment strike

A Russian guided aerial bomb struck a nine-story residential building in the Ukrainian city of Sumy on Friday evening, killing two Sumy city council employees and injuring eight people, including two children, Ukrainian officials said. Rescuers pulled four survivors, including a 7-year-old boy whose parents were among the dead, from the rubble after fires broke out between the building's third and fourth floors.

Sources: US News (Reuters), Kyiv Post

EU's Russia sanctions list at risk of lapsing amid a French-Slovak push to delist an oligarch

The European Union's roughly 3,000 sanctions listings tied to Russia's war on Ukraine are set to expire at midnight on September 22 after EU ambassadors again failed to agree on a renewal, with France and Slovakia insisting that businessman Alisher Usmanov be removed from the list. Diplomats said France's position follows pressure from Azerbaijan linked to the release of detained French nationals, while the bloc's other 25 members have so far refused to agree.

Sources: US News (Reuters), Kyiv Independent

Middle East

Israeli strikes kill at least four Palestinians in Gaza, including a health official's son

Israeli strikes across Gaza on Saturday killed at least four Palestinians, including Basir al-Bursh, son of Gaza health ministry director-general Munir al-Bursh, in the latest of near-daily violations of the year-old ceasefire, Gaza officials said. Gaza's health ministry says more than 1,300 Palestinians have been killed since the truce took effect last October, while Israel's military says four of its soldiers have died over the same period.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Al Arabiya

Houthis strike Riyadh's airport and an Aramco site in first direct hit on the Saudi capital

Yemen's Houthi rebels said they fired ballistic missiles and drones at “sensitive sites” in Riyadh and an Aramco facility in Yanbu on Saturday, the first time the group has targeted the Saudi capital since fighting with the Saudi-led coalition escalated in July. Coalition forces said air defenses intercepted a missile aimed at Riyadh, though a fire broke out at a fuel depot near King Khalid International Airport, prompting a heavy firefighting response before it was extinguished.

Sources: Al Jazeera, NBC News (Reuters)

Africa

US lets Tigray-era sanctions framework expire, dropping Eritrea's ruling party and military from blacklist

A national emergency underpinning US sanctions tied to Ethiopia's 2020-2022 Tigray war expired on September 18, prompting the Treasury Department to remove Eritrea's ruling party and its armed forces from the sanctions list they had been placed on in 2021. The move comes as Washington looks to expand its footing in the Red Sea region, though analysts cited by Al Jazeera said it reflects the framework's legal expiration rather than a broader policy shift toward Asmara.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Arab News

Nigeria names independent panel to probe 37 deaths in mining-suspect custody

Nigeria's federal government named a 10-member independent committee, chaired by a retired deputy director-general of the State Security Service, to investigate the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners who died in a crowded Niger State holding cell last week. The announcement came alongside the suspension of 20 more officers from the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps, as families of the dead accused officials of mistreating detainees held in the overcrowded cell.

Sources: Local10 (AP), Premium Times

Asia

North Korea fires two ballistic missiles toward the Sea of Japan

North Korea launched a short-range ballistic missile from its east coast on Sunday, followed hours later by a second projectile, with both believed to have splashed down outside Japan's exclusive economic zone, Japan's Defense Ministry said. The launches came two days after Pyongyang threatened to bolster its nuclear deterrent in response to what it called US hostility, prompting South Korea and Japan to step up surveillance and share tracking data with Washington.

Sources: KSAT (AP), The Japan Times

Bank of Japan raises rate to 31-year high of 1.25% as inflation persists

The Bank of Japan raised its benchmark interest rate by a quarter point to 1.25% on Friday, the highest level since 1995, in a 7-2 vote that saw two board members dissent. The bank cited inflation running above its 2% target amid rising energy prices and a weak yen, with the new rate taking effect September 24.
