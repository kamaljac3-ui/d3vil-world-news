Americas

Lula wins a fourth term as Brazil's president; Bolsonaro vows legal fight

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva defeated Senator Flávio Bolsonaro in Sunday's presidential runoff, with Brazil's electoral court confirming he won roughly 51.8% of the vote to Bolsonaro's 48.2% after a count delayed by a last-minute two-hour extension of voting. The result hands the 81-year-old Lula an unprecedented fourth term and halts a recent run of conservative election wins across Latin America, but Bolsonaro, a Trump ally, has said he will not accept the outcome and is pursuing a legal challenge to annul it. Markets were volatile Monday as investors weighed whether Lula can lower borrowing costs while containing debt.

Sources: AP (via NPR), Reuters (via Investing.com)

Salvadoran-led force battles Haitian gangs as hunt for four missing soldiers continues

El Salvador's Gang Suppression Force said clashes with gangs during its search for four missing Salvadoran aviation technicians have killed at least seven gang members and wounded four of its own personnel, more than a week after the men disappeared following an October 18 ambush near Port-au-Prince. President Nayib Bukele rejected a report that burned remains recovered in the search area belong to the missing men, saying the bodies included a child and a man taller than any of the technicians. The four soldiers' fate has not been independently confirmed.

Sources: AP (via Washington Post), Haitian Times

Europe

French arrests top 1,300 after a second weekend of mass cost-of-living protests

French police arrested at least 1,314 people over the weekend as protests over living costs and public services drew an estimated 410,000 to 800,000 people nationwide, the interior ministry said, with 53 officers injured in clashes. Authorities also reported dozens of fires and looted shops during Saturday's demonstrations, and far-right and far-left groups clashed separately in Nice. Unions have called for a further nationwide strike on November 5.

Sources: AFP (via Arab News), France 24

Zelensky confirms November trilateral talks with US, Russia as strikes kill four in Ukraine

President Volodymyr Zelensky said Sunday that Ukraine, Russia and the United States will hold trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi in the first half of November, after Russian President Vladimir Putin agreed "in principle" to resume negotiations following a meeting with US envoy Steve Witkoff. The announcement came hours after Russian strikes on Dnipro and Vinnytsia killed four people and wounded about 28, according to local officials, with Zelensky saying Russia had launched nearly 2,300 attack drones and dozens of missiles against Ukraine over the past week. Security guarantees for Ukraine remain unresolved ahead of the talks.

Sources: AP (via Euronews), Euronews

Middle East

Iran drafts plans to cut Gulf internet cables as US weighs renewed strikes

Senior Iranian security officials told Reuters that Tehran has detailed plans to strike undersea communications cables beneath the Strait of Hormuz, and to block their repair, if the United States and Israel resume strikes on Iran. The disclosure came as US media reported the Pentagon has drawn up plans for a renewed bombing campaign after November's midterm elections, though President Trump said Sunday there would be no strike "today" while leaving open the possibility of a negotiated deal. Iran's Foreign Ministry says it continues to pass messages to Washington through intermediaries.

Sources: Reuters (via Internazionale), Al Jazeera

Israeli strikes kill at least seven across Gaza as ceasefire's death toll keeps climbing

Israeli strikes killed at least five to seven Palestinians across Gaza on Sunday, including three people at Gaza City's Islamic University, according to local health officials; the Israeli military did not immediately comment. More than a year after a ceasefire took effect, Gaza's health ministry says over 1,500 Palestinians have been killed and more than 5,500 injured in continuing Israeli strikes that more than 100 aid organizations, including Amnesty International, say have left the truce holding "in name only." Exact casualty figures for Sunday's strikes vary by outlet and have not been independently confirmed.

Sources: AP (via Northeast Times), Deccan Herald, Amnesty International

Africa

UN says Sudan drone strikes have killed over 300 people in three weeks

The UN human rights office said drone strikes by Sudan's army and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces killed at least 306 people in the first three weeks of October, with High Commissioner Volker Türk warning that drones now account for most conflict-related civilian deaths in the war. The deadliest strike hit Amiet market in the contested Abyei region on October 19; local officials and Sudan's army have blamed each other, and tolls for that single strike range from the UN's count of more than 80 to local officials' count above 150. Sudan's Foreign Ministry has denied its forces carried out the Abyei strike.

Sources: OHCHR, Radio Tamazuj

At least 40 killed in Taraba State village attack, Amnesty says

Amnesty International said gunmen killed at least 40 people in a Friday attack on Binnari village in Taraba State, northeastern Nigeria, setting homes and shops ablaze and stealing cash, food and motorcycles; police initially confirmed only 10 deaths before more bodies were recovered, and Taraba's state government later put the toll at 25. Survivors told AFP the attackers arrived on roughly 50 motorcycles. Taraba Governor Agbu Kefas condemned the killings and called for stronger security protection in the area. The death toll remains disputed between Amnesty, state police and the Taraba state government.

Sources: Reuters (via Arab News), allAfrica

Asia

Typhoon Champi batters eastern China after killing one, injuring over 600 in Taiwan

Typhoon Champi, the strongest storm of the year in the region, grounded more than a hundred flights and forced the evacuation of roughly 62,400 people in Shanghai as it moved along China's eastern coast Sunday, after sweeping past Taiwan over the weekend. Taiwan's fire department said the storm killed one person and injured more than 600 there, and authorities in China's Zhejiang province also relocated thousands of coastal residents as a precaution.

Sources: South China Morning Post, New Straits Times

China's Communist Party opens plenum focused on discipline ahead of Xi's expected fourth term

China's Communist Party opened a four-day plenum of its Central Committee in Beijing Monday, with President Xi Jinping delivering a work report on tightening "comprehensive and strict party governance" rather than new economic planning, Reuters and AFP reported. Analysts said the session, attended by roughly 300 members with 15 currently under investigation on corruption-related matters, could be the last major party gathering before a 2027 leadership reshuffle expected to grant Xi a fourth term; AFP noted it may be the smallest plenum since Mao Zedong's death in 1976.

Sources: Reuters/AFP (via ThePrint), Seoul Economic Daily
