Americas

Brazil heads to Oct. 25 runoff as Bolsonaro edges Lula in first-round vote

Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, son of imprisoned former president Jair Bolsonaro, topped Sunday's first-round presidential vote with 47.3% against incumbent Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva's 44.9%, according to Brazil's Superior Electoral Court, confounding pre-election polls that had shown the race essentially tied. Neither candidate cleared the 50% threshold needed to win outright, sending Latin America's largest democracy into a three-week runoff campaign ahead of the October 25 vote, which will also decide control of Congress.

Sources: PBS NewsHour (AP), Philippine Daily Inquirer (AFP)

Quebec votes with separatist Parti Québécois favored to win a majority

Roughly 6.4 million registered Quebec voters cast ballots Monday in a provincial election in which the separatist Parti Québécois, led by Paul St-Pierre Plamondon, entered as the frontrunner after leading most pre-election polling over Premier François Legault's governing coalition. St-Pierre Plamondon has pledged to hold a third sovereignty referendum during his term but not until after Donald Trump leaves the White House, even though polling shows roughly 69% of Quebecers oppose independence.

Sources: CTV News (The Canadian Press), The Globe and Mail

Europe

Spain's Sánchez calls snap election for Nov. 29 after housing-bill defeat

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez called an early general election for November 29 on Monday, a day after his minority coalition's legislative defeat on emergency housing measures and nationwide protests that drew tens of thousands into the streets over an affordability crisis. Opinion polls suggest a coalition led by the conservative People's Party with the hard-right Vox is now the most likely outcome, which would bring a far-right party into Spain's central government for the first time since the return of democracy.

Sources: U.S. News (AP), The National (AFP)

Bosnian Serb separatist Dodik's ally wins presidency seat as nationalists sweep election

Milorad Dodik, the Bosnian Serb leader barred from public office after a conviction for defying the international peace envoy, declared victory Sunday as his ally Zeljka Cvijanovic won the Serb seat on Bosnia and Herzegovina's three-member presidency with more than 55% of the vote. Nationalist candidates also led the Bosniak and Croat presidency races, with turnout down roughly 9.5 points from 2022, as Dodik credited Donald Trump's election and Vladimir Putin's support for the result.

Sources: France 24 (AFP), PBS NewsHour (AP)

Middle East

Yemen's government launches major offensive against Houthis as Saudi coalition pledges support

Yemen's internationally recognized government announced the start of a large-scale offensive against the Iran-backed Houthi movement on Sunday, with the Saudi-led coalition pledging full support as fighting intensified around the besieged city of Taiz. The Houthis said they struck Saudi Aramco facilities near Riyadh and in Khurais in retaliation for what they described as 106 Saudi air and missile strikes on Sanaa over the previous 24 hours. The Aramco strike claims have not been independently confirmed.

Sources: France 24 (AFP), Al Jazeera

Israel marks third anniversary of Oct. 7 attack amid ongoing war and election campaign

Israel held state ceremonies at Mount Herzl and lowered flags to half-mast Monday to mark three years since Hamas's October 7, 2023 attack, with bereaved families and former hostages leading memorial events, a nationwide moment of silence, and protests. The anniversary falls three weeks before Israel's October 27 general election, which polling and regional reporting describe as a referendum on Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's wartime leadership and the security failures that preceded the attack.

Sources: AAP, Al Jazeera

Africa

Fighting spreads beyond Tigray into Amhara and Afar as hospitals are overwhelmed

Renewed fighting between Ethiopian government forces and Tigrayan rebels has spread into the neighboring Amhara and Afar regions, with aid workers reporting more than 720 people wounded and at least 52 civilians killed since combat resumed on September 22. Hospitals in the towns of Korem and Alamata have taken in roughly 480 wounded, while telecommunications remain cut across much of Tigray, hampering aid access nearly four years after a 2022 deal ended the region's earlier civil war.

Sources: Al Jazeera, BBC (via Daily Post)

France steps up Ebola aid to DR Congo as outbreak death toll passes 4,000

French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot pledged additional funding and support for the fight against Ebola during a visit to Bunia in eastern Congo's Ituri province, as the country's health ministry reported 4,042 deaths among 8,376 cases since the outbreak was declared in May. Barrot, among the first Western officials to visit the affected region, warned the outbreak could quickly resurge without continued vigilance, with further funding to be discussed at a humanitarian conference in Paris on October 6.

Sources: U.S. News (AP), AOL (PA Media)

Asia

India and Pakistan trade diplomatic protests after deadly Punjab border shooting

India's Border Security Force shot and killed two men attempting to cross the border fence in Punjab's Ferozepur sector on October 2, prompting both countries to summon each other's senior diplomats in formal protest. India's foreign ministry said the men were linked to cross-border narcotics smuggling networks and ignored warnings to turn back, while Pakistan's foreign office said they were unarmed civilians and accused Indian forces of using unprovoked lethal force.

Sources: Business Standard, Deccan Herald

North Korea claims unstoppable hypersonic missile as South Korea disputes the flight data

North Korean leader Kim Jong Un oversaw a hypersonic missile test Saturday that his sister, senior official Kim Yo Jong, said flew roughly 1,000 kilometers using low-altitude, AI-assisted maneuvering she called impossible to intercept; South Korea's military and Japan's defense ministry separately put the flight distance at about 700 and 680 kilometers. Seoul called the launch a violation of UN Security Council resolutions and urged Pyongyang to halt further tests, days after accusing North Korea of planting land mines that wounded three South Korean soldiers along the demilitarized zone.

Sources: The Korea Herald (Yonhap), U.S. News (AP)
