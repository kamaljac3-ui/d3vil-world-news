Americas

Venezuelans hold first nationwide protest in nearly two years to demand elections

Thousands of Venezuelans marched in Caracas, Valencia, Maracaibo and other cities Thursday in the first nationwide opposition demonstration in nearly two years, demanding free elections and the return of exiled opposition leader María Corina Machado. The rally, organized by Machado's Vente Venezuela party, came as interim President Delcy Rodríguez's government has set no date for a presidential or legislative vote nearly ten months after Nicolás Maduro's capture by US forces. Venezuela's intelligence service detained at least seven people during the demonstrations, according to opposition accounts.

Sources: AFP (via BusinessWorld), Al Jazeera

El Salvador launches military operation to rescue troops held by Haitian gangs

Salvadoran President Nayib Bukele said he has launched a military operation to free four Salvadoran aviation technicians taken hostage after gunmen ambushed their UN-backed Gang Suppression Force vehicle in Croix-des-Bouquets on October 18. Bukele, who described the men as non-combat personnel supporting medical evacuations, warned of a "stronger response" if they are not released, though officials have not said where the men are being held or by which gang. The men's location and condition have not been independently confirmed.

Sources: AP (via Washington Post), Al Jazeera

Europe

Russia fires 21 missiles at Kyiv, Ukraine sends drones toward Moscow, as peace push continues

Russia struck Kyiv with at least 21 ballistic missiles in three waves overnight, three of them carrying cluster munitions, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said, while Ukraine sent hundreds of drones toward Moscow in return. Russia's defense ministry said it shot down more than 800 Ukrainian drones overnight; casualty figures from the Kyiv strikes varied by source, with at least one death confirmed. The exchange came hours after US envoy Steve Witkoff met Russian President Vladimir Putin in St Petersburg as Washington pushes to revive stalled peace talks.

Sources: Reuters (via Internazionale), Euronews

Macron bans "illegitimate" protests as France braces for a fourth weekend of unrest

French President Emmanuel Macron said authorities will ban demonstrations by groups it deems "illegitimate" in the coming days, blaming "seditious" far-left groups for the looting and rioting that followed cost-of-living protests on October 17. Local officials in Paris, Lyon, Marseille and Grenoble have banned or restricted gatherings ahead of a large demonstration planned for Saturday, when "yellow vest" activists are expected to join. Authorities say more than 2,400 people have been arrested since the unrest began.

Sources: Reuters (via US News), Al Jazeera

Middle East

Houthi-linked strikes kill four in Saudi Arabia, force Aramco evacuation

An attack on King Fahd International Airport in Dammam killed two people, a Saudi citizen and an Indian national, and wounded eight on Wednesday, Saudi civil aviation authorities said, while a separate drone strike the same day killed two more at Saudi Aramco's Shaybah oilfield. The Houthis have not claimed the airport attack, and a Houthi military spokesman denied launching drone strikes that day, even as the group says it has hit Saudi defense and energy targets repeatedly over the past two weeks. Saudi Aramco ordered a full evacuation of its Dhahran headquarters, and Washington ordered nonessential US embassy staff to leave the kingdom. Responsibility for the Dammam airport attack has not been confirmed.

Sources: Reuters (via Yahoo News), Arab News

Israeli strikes and gunfire kill four in Gaza as ceasefire remains fragile

Israeli strikes and gunfire killed at least four Palestinians in separate incidents in Gaza City, Khan Younis and near the Jabalia refugee camp, local health officials said, as near-daily Israeli attacks continue despite the year-old ceasefire. Gaza's health ministry says at least 1,538 Palestinians have been killed since the truce took effect, while Israel's military says it has lost four soldiers and that it struck Hamas militants, not civilians. Hamas denies rebuilding its arsenal and accuses Israel of undermining the US-brokered truce.

Sources: Reuters (via Detroit News), Al Jazeera

Africa

Second Tanzanian opposition official charged with treason as crackdown widens

Tanzanian prosecutors charged Chadema national council member John Pambalu with treason, an offense that carries a possible death sentence, accusing him of inciting the public to obstruct last year's election. He is the second senior Chadema figure charged after party leader Tundu Lissu, whose own treason trial has resumed with a verdict due November 6; opposition groups say roughly 2,000 people died in protests following the 2025 election that President Samia Suluhu Hassan won with 98% of the vote, while a government inquiry put the toll at 518. Chadema has denied the allegations against Pambalu.

Sources: AFP (via Ghanaian Times), allAfrica

WHO warns Ebola outbreak now spans three countries as Nigeria confirms first case

The World Health Organization's Africa office warned that the Ebola outbreak which began in the Democratic Republic of Congo in May has grown "more complex" after Nigeria confirmed its first case, an imported Bundibugyo-strain infection in a woman in Abuja with recent travel through Uganda. Africa CDC has convened an emergency meeting of health ministers and says it is monitoring 44 contacts in Nigeria, even as Uganda disputes claims the woman was infected there. The precise chain of transmission has not been confirmed.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), Al Jazeera

Asia

Magnitude-7 earthquake kills at least 16 in China's Tibet region

A magnitude-7.0 earthquake struck a sparsely populated part of Nagqu in China's Tibet Autonomous Region on Thursday night, killing at least 16 people and injuring dozens, with officials warning the toll is likely to rise. At least 339 homes collapsed and rockfalls blocked a section of National Highway 317, state media reported, as President Xi Jinping ordered an all-out rescue effort and more than 1,600 personnel were deployed to the area. The final death toll has not been confirmed.

Sources: AP (via CP24), Al Jazeera

Seoul mayor acquitted on appeal, easing threat to his political future

A Seoul appeals court acquitted Mayor Oh Se-hoon on Friday of Political Funds Act violations tied to opinion-poll payments ahead of a 2021 by-election, overturning a July conviction that could have cost him his office. The court said testimony from the case's central witness, political broker Myung Tae-kyun, was not reliable enough to sustain a conviction "beyond a reasonable doubt," and it acquitted two co-defendants as well. Prosecutors said they plan to appeal to South Korea's Supreme Court, so the case is not fully closed.

Sources: Korea Times, Korea JoongAng Daily
