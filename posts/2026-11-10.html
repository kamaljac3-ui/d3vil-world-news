Americas

Trump says Venezuela's elections will come "at some point" as US asserts control

President Trump said Monday that the United States is "totally in charge" of Venezuela and that elections there will take place "at some point," though he set no date, according to Reuters. The remarks come roughly ten months after a US military operation captured former president Nicolás Maduro, who faces narco-terrorism charges in New York, leaving Vice President Delcy Rodríguez as interim leader. Secretary of State Marco Rubio has said a vote could "ideally" happen next year, contingent on Venezuela first reconstituting its Supreme Court and electoral council.

Sources: Reuters (via TradingView), Rio Times

Amnesty International presses El Salvador to disclose terms of its Haiti troop surge

Amnesty International called Tuesday for El Salvador and Haiti to publish the agreement governing the roughly 2,000 to 2,500 Salvadoran troops President Nayib Bukele has deployed against gangs in Haiti, according to AFP and Al Jazeera. The surge followed the October disappearance of four Salvadoran aviation technicians in Haiti and President Trump's endorsement of the deployment; most of the troops are operating outside the UN-backed Gang Suppression Force framework. Haiti's prime minister, Alix Didier Fils-Aimé, is due in El Salvador on November 16 to discuss the force's future.

Sources: AFP (via France 24), Al Jazeera

Guatemalan lawmaker and three police officers killed in cartel-linked ambush

Gunmen killed congressman Julio César Portillo Paz and three National Civil Police officers in an ambush in Río Hondo, in Guatemala's Zacapa department, on November 6, according to the AP and AFP. Guatemala's interior ministry called it a direct attack on the lawmaker; the attackers fled and had not been identified, though AFP noted the local drug trade is controlled by the Lorenzana family, which has ties to Mexico's Sinaloa cartel. President Bernardo Arévalo said the officers died in the line of duty and ordered security forces to cooperate with prosecutors.

Sources: AP (via The Spokesman-Review), AFP (via The Daily Star)

Europe

US, Russia and Ukraine open first trilateral peace talks since February in Abu Dhabi

Negotiators from the United States, Russia and Ukraine began two days of talks in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday aimed at ending the war, their first trilateral session since February, with security guarantees topping the agenda, according to the AP and NPR. Russia's delegation is led by presidential aide Vladimir Medinsky and Ukraine's by Rustem Umerov, and European officials and NATO are also attending despite the Kremlin's insistence the format stay trilateral. US envoy Jared Kushner called the talks an initial step that could "build momentum," though Moscow is still pressing for control of Ukraine's Donetsk region and Russia launched 162 drones and a ballistic missile at Ukraine overnight.

Sources: AP (via CBS News), NPR

COP31 climate summit opens in Turkey under pressure to show results

The UN's COP31 climate conference opened in Antalya, Turkey on November 9 and runs through November 20, with UN climate chief Simon Stiell warning progress is "nowhere near fast enough," according to the AP and UN News. Turkey and Australia are sharing the presidency, with a leaders' summit set for November 11–12, and negotiators face disputes over who pays for climate losses and damage and whether wealthy nations will meet their finance pledges. The United States, which withdrew from the Paris Agreement earlier this year, is not sending an official delegation.

Sources: AP (via PBS NewsHour), UN News

Middle East

Trump holds off on new Iran strikes after Gulf appeals, keeps Hormuz blockade

President Trump said Monday he will hold off on further military strikes against Iran for now after appeals from Qatar, Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Bahrain, while keeping the US naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz in place, according to Reuters. US forces struck Iranian radar and missile-launcher sites on Qeshm Island overnight, and Iran has continued targeting tankers in the Gulf while its Houthi allies strike Saudi Arabia; US and Gulf officials say a diplomatic breakthrough is not imminent, with Trump saying "we're locked and loaded" should strikes resume.

Sources: Reuters (via Investing.com), CBC

Gaza reconstruction remains stalled as flooding kills four in tent camps

A sand embankment collapsed onto flooded tents in Deir al-Balah, central Gaza, on November 9, killing a woman and three children, as heavy rains flooded more than 70,000 tents sheltering displaced Palestinians, according to Reuters and Al Jazeera. Most of Gaza's roughly two million residents still live in tents or the ruins of bombed buildings, and Israel says reconstruction cannot begin until Hamas disarms; negotiators have not agreed on terms for disarmament or an Israeli troop withdrawal. "Tents can't protect people... the only solution is reconstruction," Gaza civil defense spokesman Mahmoud Basal told Reuters.

Sources: Reuters (via CBC), Al Jazeera

Africa

More than 50,000 flee into Kenya as Ethiopian border clashes continue

Fighting between Ethiopia's Oromia and Somali regional forces over disputed territory near the border town of Moyale has driven more than 50,000 people into Kenya since late October, according to Reuters and Al Jazeera. A Kenyan government and IOM assessment found most of the displaced households had fled with little food, and the UN refugee agency has launched an $8.9 million emergency appeal; casualty figures remain uncertain, though Reuters reported at least nine deaths in the fighting's early days. Kenya's police have ordered extra officers to the border to manage the crossings.

Sources: Reuters (via Arab News), Al Jazeera

Congo's renamed M23 rebels advance toward strategic town in new eastern offensive

Fighters from the Front for the Refoundation of the Nation (FRN) — the former M23 movement, now led by ex-president Joseph Kabila — launched an offensive in South Kivu overnight on November 6 and advanced roughly 50 kilometers toward the strategic town of Walikale without reaching it, according to AFP. The fighters seized several towns including Bunyakiri, and a local territory administrator estimated the push has displaced more than 160,000 people, even as Congo's army says it is counterattacking and holding some positions. The offensive comes despite a US-brokered peace deal between the DRC and Rwanda reached earlier this year.

Sources: AFP (via News24), AFP (via New Vision)

Asia

Taiwan says China is running "area denial" drills in the Western Pacific

Taiwan's defense minister, Wellington Koo, said Tuesday that China's Liaoning carrier group and other naval formations are conducting "area denial" drills in the Western Pacific, days before President Trump is expected to meet President Xi Jinping at the APEC summit in Shenzhen, according to Reuters and Taipei Times. Taiwan's Joseph Wu reported more than 100 Chinese naval and coast guard ships operating around Taiwan and into the Pacific, and China's defense ministry had not responded to requests for comment. G7 foreign ministers, meeting separately in Paris, reaffirmed "strong opposition to any unilateral attempts to change the status quo by force," including across the Taiwan Strait.

Sources: Taipei Times (CNA), Japan Times

China extends pause on new rare-earth export controls through January

China's Ministry of Commerce said Monday it will extend a suspension of expanded export controls on rare earths, lithium-battery technology and industrial diamonds for two more months, through January 10, 2027, according to Reuters. The move is tied to broader trade talks with Washington following a Trump-Xi summit in South Korea, and comes days before Trump is expected to meet Xi again at the APEC summit in Shenzhen. The original controls, introduced in 2025, had already been suspended for a year as part of the trade de-escalation.

Sources: Reuters (via Investing.com), US News
