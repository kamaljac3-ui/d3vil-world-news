Americas

Ecuador declares a 60-day state of emergency and reinstates a nighttime curfew over surging homicides

President Daniel Noboa signed a decree Tuesday declaring a 60-day state of exception across eight provinces and three municipalities, citing "grave internal commotion" from organized crime, and reinstated a midnight-to-5am curfew from September 17-30 in the coastal provinces of Guayas, El Oro, Los Ríos and Manabí — the country's third curfew of the year. The decree suspends the constitutional right to inviolability of the home, letting police and soldiers conduct warrantless raids where criminal activity is suspected, and cites 442 intentional homicides recorded between August 16 and September 10 alone, with Guayas province accounting for 187 of them.

Sources: BERNAMA, Infobae

Europe

Russia's largest air assault on Kyiv in weeks kills at least 10, with a rare strike near the EU's diplomatic offices

Russia launched a large wave of missiles and drones at Kyiv and other Ukrainian regions overnight into Thursday, including a rare strike on the capital's city center that damaged a building near the European Union's diplomatic offices there. Ukrainian officials said at least 10 people were killed and dozens more wounded nationwide, though some local reports put the toll in the capital alone as high as 17; final figures were still being confirmed as rescue crews searched damaged buildings.

Sources: PBS (AP), The Kyiv Independent

Middle East

France, Lebanon and Jordan open Paris talks to support Lebanon's army as UN peacekeepers prepare to leave

The leaders of France, Lebanon and Jordan met in Paris on Thursday to prepare a donor conference aimed at bolstering Lebanon's underfunded military, as the UNIFIL peacekeeping mission in southern Lebanon approaches the end of its mandate later this year. Officials discussed options for a replacement international presence, including a France- and Italy-backed multinational force or a European Union-led mission, to help the Lebanese army take over security duties from Israeli forces under a stalled withdrawal framework.

Sources: News4Jax (AP), Arab News

Africa

A suspected methanol-tainted local drink kills 49 people in Nigeria's Ondo state

Ondo State's health commissioner said Wednesday that 49 people have now died and about 170 others have fallen ill after drinking a locally brewed alcoholic mixture known as "Monkey Tail," with the outbreak concentrated in the Odigbo and Irele local government areas since early September. Police have arrested a suspect, identified as Oloruntoba Babatunde, over the drink's production, and substances suspected to be methanol have been recovered and sent for forensic testing; doctors say the toxin causes kidney failure, blindness and brain damage depending on the dose consumed.

Sources: AP, Vanguard (Nigeria)

Asia

Captain found liable as death toll from a Philippine ferry fire reaches 77, with 12 still missing

The Philippine Coast Guard said the captain of the MV June Aster, Roel Sarmiento, bears liability for the September 9 fire that swept the passenger ferry off Coron, Palawan, after he turned himself in five days after the disaster and is now being questioned about his actions in its early minutes. The death toll rose to 77 this week after a surviving crew member died of injuries, with 12 of the 132 people who were aboard still unaccounted for; the vessel had passed mandated safety checks and was not overcrowded when the fire broke out, officials said, and the investigation is now focused on the crew's response and the captain's conduct.

Sources: ABC News (AP), Philstar
