Europe

Ukraine launches its largest drone attack of the war, striking Russia's Crimean bridge

Ukraine sent more than 1,300 drones into Russia overnight into Monday in what appeared to be its biggest aerial attack since the 2022 invasion began, with Russia's defense ministry saying it intercepted 1,336 of them across 16 regions, according to Reuters. The strikes hit the Kerch Bridge linking Russia to annexed Crimea, halting traffic, and an oil depot in Rostov region's Azov, though AFP reported the Kremlin described the bridge damage as minor. Russian strikes on Ukraine overnight killed at least one person each in Dnipro, Chernihiv and Zaporizhzhia, Ukrainian officials said.

Sources: NBC News (Reuters), Yahoo News (AFP)

Estonia to close all border crossings with Russia after foiled sabotage plot

Estonia will shut its three remaining road crossings with Russia, at Narva, Koidula and Luhamaa, starting December 1, Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna said Sunday, citing "clear evidence" that Russia's military intelligence agency ordered a failed arson attack on drone maker Threod Systems near Tallinn on November 14. Eight Latvian nationals were detained over the plot, and Tsahkna said he informed counterparts in Finland, Latvia and Lithuania; Russia's foreign ministry called the closure "aggressive and unbridled Russophobia," according to Al Jazeera.

Sources: Estonia Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Al Jazeera

Spain logs its biggest single-day migrant arrival in the Canaries since 2020

Nearly 1,500 migrants reached Spain's Canary Islands in a single day on Saturday, the highest one-day count since 2020, and almost 2,200 arrived over the full weekend, according to the Associated Press. Spain declared a migration emergency on the islands this month, which officials tie to a rise in departures from sub-Saharan Africa; the Canaries have taken in more than 13,000 of the roughly 28,000 people who reached Spain by sea between January and the end of October.

Sources: NBC News (AP), Irish News (AP)

Middle East

Saudi-backed Yemeni forces retreat, ceding the Bab el-Mandeb Strait to Houthi rebels

Yemen's Saudi-backed Southern Armed Forces said Sunday they were withdrawing from positions around the Bab el-Mandeb Strait and Dhubab airport to reinforce the defense of Aden, effectively ceding the strategic Red Sea strait to Iran-aligned Houthi fighters after more than a month of fighting, according to Reuters. Houthi forces advanced to within roughly 60 kilometers of Aden after seizing the Al-Maqatirah and Al-Madaribah districts, AFP reported, while the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted Houthi missiles and drones aimed at Riyadh and the kingdom's Eastern Province.

Sources: CNBC (Reuters), France 24 (AFP)

Israeli strike kills senior Hamas commander in Gaza despite ceasefire

An Israeli airstrike killed Samir Abu Younis, commander of Hamas's Rafah Brigade, in Gaza's al-Mawasi area on Saturday, and both the Israeli military and Hamas confirmed his death, according to the AP. Abu Younis had led the brigade for roughly two months after his predecessor was killed in September, and Israel's military said he had been working to rebuild the unit's capabilities; the strike came despite the US-brokered ceasefire that took effect in October 2025.

Sources: AP (via Daily Gazette), Al Jazeera

Africa

Africa CDC warns Congo's Ebola outbreak could become the worst on record

Africa CDC Director-General Jean Kaseya told Reuters Sunday that the Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo remains "not under control" and could become the worst the world has seen if it isn't stopped soon. Congolese authorities have recorded more than 11,200 infections and over 5,500 deaths since the outbreak was declared in May, and Kaseya said only about $1.3 billion of the $2.9 billion pledged by donors has been released so far. The World Health Organization's Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus struck a more optimistic note the same day, saying transmission is slowing in several of the outbreak's earliest-hit areas.

Sources: Reuters (via Yahoo News), Sowetan

Asia

ASEAN and China fail to finish South China Sea code of conduct under Philippine chairmanship

ASEAN and China failed to conclude a long-sought code of conduct for the South China Sea during the Philippines' year as bloc chair, reaching only a provisional agreement on about 80% of the draft text, according to Reuters. Philippine Foreign Secretary Ma. Theresa Lazaro said the two sides still disagree over whether the code should be legally binding, and the unfinished task now passes to Singapore, which takes over the ASEAN chairmanship next year.

Sources: Reuters (via U.S. News), Philippine Star

ASEAN leaders extend Myanmar junta chief's summit ban

Southeast Asian leaders meeting in Manila for the 49th ASEAN summit agreed Monday to extend a ban on Myanmar's military leader attending top-level meetings, citing the junta's failure to make progress on the bloc's 2021 five-point peace plan, according to the AP. Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr., this year's ASEAN chair, pledged "constructive and principled engagement" with Myanmar even as its civil war continues; the exclusion of junta chief Min Aung Hlaing from summits has been in place since the 2021 coup.

Sources: Manila Bulletin (AP), Japan Times
