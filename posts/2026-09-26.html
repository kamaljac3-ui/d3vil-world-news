Americas

Venezuela's Rodríguez returns from Washington without a deal or an election date

Interim President Delcy Rodríguez left New York and Washington this week with photographs alongside President Trump but no concrete agreements on debt, energy or mining, after seeking to show investors Venezuela is open for business again following her predecessor's removal in January. She has pledged elections as part of a transition to "full democracy" but still has not set a date, and analysts say the court and electoral-authority reforms Caracas says are needed could push a vote into 2027 or later.

Sources: US News (Reuters), TimesLIVE (Reuters)

Europe

US proposes Ukraine-Russia technical talks in the UAE as both sides trade ceasefire terms

Washington has proposed that Russian and Ukrainian officials hold a preparatory, technical-level meeting in the United Arab Emirates, President Volodymyr Zelensky said, though no date has been set. The Kremlin said a trilateral meeting with the US could happen soon, and Zelensky said Washington would report back to Kyiv within about ten days with either a Russian counteroffer or a result, reviving a format last used in earlier rounds of talks this year.

Sources: US News (Reuters), Free Malaysia Today (Reuters)

Russian strikes kill several across Ukraine as both sides' aerial attacks intensify

Russian missiles and drones hit homes and infrastructure in Kyiv, Zaporizhzhia, Odesa and the Sumy and Kharkiv regions on Saturday, killing at least seven people combined across the latest strikes, including two women in Zaporizhzhia, officials said. President Zelensky said the war's aerial exchanges are growing more brutal on both sides and that the world "must choose stronger responses to keep the war from spreading further."

Sources: US News (AP), ClickOrlando (AP)

Middle East

UN Security Council condemns Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia in rare show of unity

The UN Security Council issued a joint statement condemning continued Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, including strikes that have wounded civilians and hit energy infrastructure, delivered by French envoy Jérôme Bonnafont on behalf of the full council. The rebuke comes as Houthi forces wage a naval blockade and ground offensive that has displaced more than 138,000 people in Yemen since August, threatening both Saudi oil exports and the wider Red Sea shipping route.

Sources: Al Arabiya, Yahoo (AP)

Iran rules out nuclear concessions even as it floats a week-long Hormuz reopening plan

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran will not make concessions on its nuclear program even if Washington accepts its proposal to end fighting and reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days, in exchange for the US lifting its naval blockade of Iranian ports, releasing frozen funds and waiving oil sanctions. The two sides remain deadlocked over which side must act first, and Iranian hardliners have criticized Tehran's negotiators for even meeting with what lawmaker Ebrahim Rezaei called "the aggressor enemy."

Sources: US News (Reuters), Baltimore Sun (AP)

Africa

Sudan's army recaptures North Kordofan town of Sodari from the RSF

Sudanese armed forces and allied former rebel factions seized the town of Sodari in North Kordofan on Friday after heavy fighting, pushing back the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces in the latest gain of an offensive the army launched in late July. Sodari sits on supply routes linking North Kordofan to Darfur and West Kordofan, and its capture extends a westward push by government-aligned forces as the RSF continues besieging the nearby city of El-Obeid.

Sources: Sudan Tribune, Radio Tamazuj

Ethiopian drone strikes on two Tigray schools reported to have killed 14 civilians

The mayor of Alamata in southern Tigray said Ethiopian drones and a fighter jet carried out more than 21 strikes overnight on September 22, two of which hit secondary-school buildings and killed 14 civilians and wounded 22 more, telling reporters federal forces may have suspected the schools were being used by Tigrayan fighters. The claim comes as fighting spreads across Tigray, Amhara and Afar after the Tigray People's Liberation Front and six allied groups launched an offensive aimed at toppling Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's government. Not yet independently confirmed by Ethiopia's federal government.

Sources: Al Jazeera, BBC

Asia

Pakistan says 13 Afghan Taliban fighters killed in border clash; Kabul denies it

Pakistan's information ministry said its forces repelled an "attempted Afghan aggression" in the Gulistan sector near the Chaman border crossing, killing at least 13 Taliban personnel in overnight fighting, while Afghanistan's defense ministry denied suffering any casualties and accused Pakistani forces of destroying one of its border posts. Residents in Chaman reported hours of exchanged artillery and gunfire before an uneasy calm returned to the border on Friday. Neither side's casualty claims have been independently verified.

Sources: US News (AP), ClickOrlando (AP)

Imran Khan's party postpones Islamabad march to October 4 after security lockdown

Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf postponed its planned march from Peshawar to Islamabad, originally set for September 27, to October 4, after the government blocked highways into the capital with shipping containers and deployed tens of thousands of security personnel and detained hundreds of party activists. The party, which is demanding better jail conditions and the release of former Prime Minister Imran Khan, said through a spokesperson that it could still move the march up depending on the government's response.

Sources: US News (Reuters), Dawn
