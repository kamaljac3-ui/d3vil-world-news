Americas

Tornado and flash floods trigger red alert across greater Santiago

An EF-2 tornado with winds up to 218 km/h tore through Requínoa, about 100 km south of Santiago, on Monday, damaging roughly 70 homes and a school, Chile's weather service said. Overnight rain then sent waterways over their banks in ten Santiago-area communes, forcing evacuations and leaving dozens sheltered under a red alert from disaster agency Senapred, which warned of further storms, hail and snow in the days ahead.

Sources: AFP (via La Jornada), Emol

UN vote condemning US embargo on Cuba draws weakest support in 30 years

The UN General Assembly voted Wednesday, for the 34th consecutive year, to call for an end to the US economic embargo on Cuba, but the 130-17 tally with 25 abstentions was its narrowest margin since 1995, down from 165-7 a year earlier. The United States, Israel, Argentina and Ukraine were among the 17 countries voting against the non-binding measure after a US lobbying push; the resolution carries no legal force, since only the US Congress can lift the embargo.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), Al Jazeera

Europe

US-brokered truce in Ukraine's Oleshky holds into a third day

A localized ceasefire around the Russian-occupied town of Oleshky in Kherson region, arranged with the help of US first lady Melania Trump, held into its third day Thursday as mine-clearance crews worked to open roads for aid deliveries and civilian evacuations. Ukraine's human rights ombudsman said both sides were observing the truce, due to run until the evening of November 1; President Volodymyr Zelensky has confirmed Ukrainian, Russian and US delegations will hold further talks in Abu Dhabi next month.

Sources: AP (via ClickOrlando), Al Jazeera

Serbia's Student List faces 90-day clock to form a government after ousting Vučić

Serbia's parliament must seat the Student List's roughly 139-seat majority and then elect a government within 90 days or face fresh elections, after the student-backed movement routed Aleksandar Vučić's party in Sunday's snap vote. The list has yet to settle on a prime ministerial candidate from a shortlist that includes cardiologist Ilija Srdanović and former central bank governor Dejan Šoškić, while Vučić's own resignation as president in September separately triggers a presidential election that must be held by December 27.

Sources: The Canadian Press/AP (via CTV News), Al Jazeera

Middle East

Herzog opens coalition consultations as Israel's election leaves no clear winner

With about 90% of votes counted from Tuesday's election, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc held 53 seats to 48 for a rival bloc led by former military chief Gadi Eisenkot, both short of the 61 needed for a Knesset majority, while Arab-led parties won a record 19 seats. President Isaac Herzog said he would wait for official results before consulting party leaders and urged them to approach coalition talks with an "open mind" to avoid a repeat election; Eisenkot has ruled out serving under Netanyahu in any arrangement, leaving the Arab parties as likely kingmakers.

Sources: AP (via PBS), Al Jazeera

Trump rejects Iran's Hormuz reopening plan as the strait stays shut

President Trump said he rejected Iran's proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, which has carried only a handful of vessels a day since the US and Israel struck Iran in February, after Tehran conditioned any reopening on the US lifting its naval blockade and sanctions. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran remains "in close and ongoing contact" with mediators, while Vice President JD Vance separately sent a letter to a senior Iranian security official that the White House described as humanitarian outreach rather than a resumption of peace talks.

Sources: AP (via PBS), Al Jazeera

Africa

US sanctions Rwanda's defense ministry over support for Congo rebels

The US Treasury sanctioned Rwanda's Ministry of Defence and a military-linked financial institution, Zigama Credit and Savings Society, accusing Rwandan forces of fighting alongside M23 rebels in eastern Congo and supplying them weapons, training and surface-to-air missile systems. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the measures were meant to enforce the Washington Accords Rwanda and Congo signed in December and demanded Rwanda "halt its support for M23 and withdraw its troops from eastern DRC"; a Rwandan government spokesperson said Congo, not Rwanda, had undermined the agreement.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), Free Malaysia Today (AFP)

UK sanctions 15 Sudanese commanders over El Fasher atrocities

Britain imposed travel bans and asset freezes on 15 commanders from Sudan's army and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces, including RSF Colonel Hussein Abu Jalak, over a year-long campaign of killings and sexual violence following the RSF's capture of El Fasher. Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband said the measures, paired with £15 million in new aid for survivors, reflect findings that "siege tactics and the systematic obstruction of aid" are still being used as weapons of war in Sudan.

Sources: dpa (via Ghana News Agency), Al Jazeera

Asia

Hong Kong's housing minister resigns after inquiry into deadly Tai Po fire

Housing Secretary Winnie Ho resigned after an independent inquiry found the Housing Bureau's own checking unit bore the greatest responsibility, among four government agencies, for failing to oversee renovation work at Wang Fuk Court before the fire that killed more than 150 people last November. Chief Executive John Lee apologized and said Ho "should bear political responsibility" despite no finding of personal wrongdoing, and announced a separate three-month investigation into whether staff at the four agencies were derelict in their duties.

Sources: AP (via WRAL), SCMP

North Korea fires dozen-plus ballistic missiles as Seoul's talks offer goes unanswered

North Korea fired roughly a dozen short-range ballistic missiles from the Wonsan area toward the Sea of Japan on Thursday, its 17th such launch of 2026, days after South Korea proposed military talks to confirm the inter-Korean border and prevent accidental clashes. South Korea's National Security Office held an emergency meeting and said the launches violated UN Security Council resolutions; Japan confirmed detecting the missiles, which fell outside its exclusive economic zone, and China's foreign ministry issued an unusual statement expressing hope "relevant parties" would work toward peace.

Sources: Yonhap (via Aerotime), The Japan Times
