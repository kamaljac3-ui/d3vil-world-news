Americas

Brazil heads into Sunday's vote with Lula seeking a historic fourth term against Flávio Bolsonaro

Brazilians vote Sunday in a first round that could hand President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, 80, an unprecedented fourth term, with polls showing him narrowly ahead of Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, who is barred from running after being convicted over a coup plot. Recent BTG Pactual/Nexus polling put Lula's first-round support around 42% to Bolsonaro's 37%, with the two locked in a tighter statistical tie for a potential October 25 runoff.

Sources: Reuters (via Internazionale), Arab News

Europe

Russia damages Kyiv's Northern Bridge, a third day of strikes on the capital's Dnipro crossings

A Russian strike damaged Kyiv's Northern Bridge early Saturday, suspending traffic for a third consecutive day after earlier attacks forced the full or partial closure of three of the city's six road bridges over the Dnipro River, the Associated Press reports. Mayor Vitali Klitschko said the strike damaged the road surface and trolleybus power lines, and Russia's Defense Ministry acknowledged the attack, saying the bridge is used for Ukrainian troop movements and military supply deliveries. AP reports that Russian attacks elsewhere in Ukraine killed at least six people over the same period.

Sources: Associated Press (via KSAT)

Middle East

UAE opens investigation into flydubai cockpit attack as Trump says Iran involvement is likely

The UAE attorney general has ordered an investigation into the September 30 attack aboard a flydubai flight from Dubai to Tel Aviv, in which the co-pilot stabbed the captain and, Israeli officials say, tried to bring the plane down before passengers and crew subdued him; the aircraft diverted to Tabuk, Saudi Arabia, where the co-pilot remains in custody and has not been charged. Asked whether Iran was involved, President Donald Trump said the answer was yes “based on what I'm hearing,” adding that the matter was still being worked on and warning of a forceful response if confirmed. The attacker's motive and any link to Iran have not been independently confirmed.

Sources: Associated Press (via KSAT, Oct. 1), Associated Press (via KSAT, Oct. 2), Al Jazeera

Houthi missiles target Saudi Arabia again, with a projectile striking a school in Najran

The Saudi-led coalition in Yemen said it intercepted and destroyed three Houthi ballistic missiles fired toward the southwestern city of Khamis Mushait on Friday, and Saudi Civil Defense said a separate Houthi projectile struck a school in Najran, causing material damage; no casualties were reported in that statement. The Associated Press reports the attacks come amid intense fighting between the Houthis and Saudi-backed forces in Yemen, with the World Food Program warning that humanitarian conditions there are worsening.

Sources: Arab News, Associated Press (via KSAT)

Africa

Congo's Ebola outbreak passes 4,000 deaths as soldiers burn a patient transit center

Congo's Health Ministry reported 4,018 deaths among 8,300 confirmed Ebola cases this week, making the Bundibugyo-strain outbreak the largest and deadliest in the country's history, surpassing the 2018–2020 epidemic, according to Reuters; the World Health Organization has called it the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak on record, AP reports. Soldiers searching for weapons burned a camp housing an Ebola transit center on the edge of Bunia, forcing about 19,000 people to flee, AP reports, and at least 50 health workers have died of the virus. Reuters says the outbreak, declared May 15, now spans seven provinces.

Sources: Associated Press (via KSAT), Reuters (via CNBC Africa)

Ethiopian forces say they retook the airport at Mekelle as Tigray-led rebels reportedly pull back

Ethiopia's military regained control of the airport serving Mekelle, the Tigray regional capital, which rebels had seized on September 23, Reuters reported Saturday, citing a source with knowledge of events and a humanitarian source. AFP, citing a local journalist and residents, reported that Tigray People's Liberation Front leaders and fighters were leaving the city, though federal forces had not yet been seen inside Mekelle itself. The fighting follows a week of escalation that led Ethiopia and Eritrea to cut diplomatic ties. Not independently confirmed; communications in the region are restricted, and Al Jazeera said it could not verify the claims.

Sources: Al Jazeera (citing Reuters and AFP), Reuters (via U.S. News)

Asia

Pakistan seals Islamabad with shipping containers ahead of Imran Khan party's long march, set for Sunday

Authorities have lined Islamabad's approaches with an estimated 1,500 to 2,000 shipping containers for nearly four weeks ahead of the long march Imran Khan's Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf has now set for Sunday, October 4, to demand the jailed former prime minister's release, Al Jazeera reports. The party postponed the march from September 27, with a senior PTI leader saying the delay was meant to exhaust the government and security forces, while Information Minister Attaullah Tarar challenged the party to proceed “if you have the courage,” according to Dawn. The Express Tribune reported this week that PTI is weighing starting the march from southern Khyber Pakhtunkhwa districts rather than Peshawar.

Sources: Reuters (via Internazionale), Dawn, Al Jazeera, The Express Tribune

North Korea fires a ballistic missile days after denying responsibility for a DMZ mine blast

North Korea launched a ballistic missile from the Wonsan area toward the East Sea early Saturday, flying more than 700 kilometers, South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff said, according to AFP; Seoul, Washington and Tokyo are sharing information on the launch. It follows the September 21 mine explosion inside the Demilitarized Zone that wounded three South Korean soldiers, which Seoul and the UN Command attribute to a North Korean mine and Pyongyang denies; Kim Yo Jong has called Seoul's demand for an apology “quite ridiculous.”

Sources: AFP (via France 24), Associated Press (via U.S. News)
