Americas

El Salvador rushes about 1,000 troops into Haiti after gang ambush of its soldiers

A security official in Haiti told Reuters that roughly 1,000 Salvadoran troops have arrived in the country in recent days, as El Salvador widens its search for four special-forces soldiers whose armored vehicle was ambushed by gangs in Croix-des-Bouquets on October 18. President Nayib Bukele ordered a rescue operation after the ambush, and joint raids by Salvadoran forces and Haitian police have since killed several gang members without locating the missing men. Neither El Salvador's government nor the United Nations has confirmed the troop figure, and gang leaders have released videos denying involvement in the attack. The four soldiers remained missing as of publication.

Sources: Reuters (via Internazionale), AP (via Washington Post)

Europe

Protesters storm pro-Vučić TV station in Belgrade after Serbia's election upset

Demonstrators celebrating the Student List's landslide election win entered the Belgrade newsroom of Informer, a television station aligned with outgoing leader Aleksandar Vučić, late Monday, interrupting broadcasts and surrounding editor-in-chief Dragan Vučićević while calling for his arrest. A separate crowd gathered outside the pro-Vučić Pink TV station, which police cordoned off. Vučićević said the intruders destroyed cameras and other equipment worth tens of thousands of euros; Vučić called the incident an assault on press freedom, while Serbia's independent journalists' association said it would seek to have Informer's broadcast license revoked.

Sources: Reuters (via Internazionale), Reuters (via US News)

Middle East

Israel's election leaves Netanyahu and Eisenkot blocs deadlocked

Partial results from Tuesday's Israeli parliamentary election showed Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc and a rival bloc led by former military chief Gadi Eisenkot both short of the 61 seats needed for a governing majority in the 120-seat Knesset, with roughly three-quarters of votes counted. Eisenkot said he would try to form a government despite lacking a majority, while Netanyahu's camp pushed for what it called a "strong national right-wing government"; final results are not expected until November 4. Turnout was reported at its highest level in years.

Sources: AP (via Durango Herald), Al Jazeera

Qatar presents new draft deal to Iran to end Strait of Hormuz standoff

Qatari mediators presented Iran with a new draft agreement in Tehran aimed at ending the closure of the Strait of Hormuz and the US naval blockade of Iranian ports, setting out a seven-day timeline of reciprocal confidence-building steps; Iran was weighing a response expected by the end of the week. An Iranian official said reopening the strait still requires Washington to lift sanctions, unfreeze Iranian assets and end the blockade, while US officials continue to demand that Iran significantly cut its uranium-enrichment capacity before any deal is reached. No agreement had been reached as of publication.

Sources: AP (via UNN), Al Jazeera

Africa

At least 62 killed in bandit attack on Nigerian village in Taraba state

Gunmen described by authorities as bandits attacked Binnari, a community in Taraba state's Karim-Lamido area, during a weekly market last Friday, killing at least 62 people and wounding 52 others, Nigeria's National Emergency Management Agency said. Amnesty International Nigeria said residents were chased toward the Benue River as they tried to flee, and rescue workers have recovered bodies from the water; police said the toll could still rise as more remains are found. The attack struck a region where armed bandit groups routinely carry out mass killings and kidnappings for ransom.

Sources: AP (via WRAL), Washington Post

DR Congo's Ebola outbreak passes 10,000 cases, becoming the country's deadliest

Confirmed Ebola cases in the Democratic Republic of Congo surpassed 10,000 this week, with government data putting the toll at 10,060 infections and 4,887 deaths since the outbreak began in May. The Bundibugyo-strain outbreak, for which no vaccine or treatment is approved, is now the largest and deadliest in Congo's history and the second-largest Ebola outbreak ever recorded worldwide, behind West Africa's 2014-2016 epidemic; cases have also been confirmed in Kenya, Uganda and Nigeria. The World Health Organization said escalating insecurity and population displacement in affected areas are hampering the response.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), AFP (via Insider Paper)

Asia

Cyclone Arnab kills at least three as it strikes India's eastern coast

Cyclone Arnab made landfall on India's eastern coast Wednesday, killing at least three people and destroying at least 180 houses in Andhra Pradesh, officials said, after more than 180,000 people were evacuated from Andhra Pradesh and Odisha ahead of the storm. Two of the deaths were attributed to a falling tree and electrocution, authorities said, while India's weather agency recorded up to 30 centimeters of rain in parts of both states. High winds brought down roughly 220 trees and disrupted power supply in several areas.

Sources: Al Jazeera, AP (via KSL)

Pakistan strikes Taliban positions after deadly cross-border attack in Waziristan

Pakistan's military said it destroyed multiple Afghan Taliban checkposts in South Waziristan's Angoor Adda area on Tuesday after Taliban forces fired on civilian homes and a market, killing one person and wounding five, Reuters reported. The exchange came amid renewed clashes further north in Afghanistan's Paktika province, where residents reportedly fled as fighting intensified. A Taliban spokesperson had not responded to Reuters' request for comment; Pakistan separately renewed calls for Kabul to act against militants it says launch cross-border attacks from Afghan territory.

Sources: Reuters (via US News), Geo News
