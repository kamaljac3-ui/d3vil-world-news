Americas

Lula and Bolsonaro camps trade bids to disqualify each other days before Brazil's runoff

Brazil's presidential race intensified ahead of Sunday's runoff, as backers of President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and challenger Flávio Bolsonaro each pushed Brazil's top electoral court to disqualify the other over alleged social-media interference in the campaign. The court has issued a string of rulings this month ordering both camps to pull ads it found crossed legal lines, including one this week barring a Lula campaign ad that linked Flávio to harassment accusations. The winner of the tightly polled race takes office January 5.

Sources: AP (via Telangana Today), Al Jazeera

Four Salvadoran peacekeepers remain missing after Haiti gang ambush

Four Salvadoran air force members attached to the UN-backed Gang Suppression Force remain unaccounted for days after gunmen ambushed their armored vehicle in Croix-des-Bouquets, near Port-au-Prince, in the force's first major attack since it began operating. Rescue helicopters sent to search for the men came under fire themselves, and no group has claimed responsibility. The ambush comes as Haiti's presidential and legislative elections, already postponed indefinitely, face further delay; the UN says gangs control roughly 70% of the capital and have killed more than 4,000 people this year. The fate of the four missing soldiers has not been confirmed.

Sources: AP (via NY1), Al Jazeera

Europe

France bans more protests and keeps Paris curfew in place as unrest toll grows

French authorities banned planned demonstrations in several cities, from Toulon to Le Havre, and kept an overnight curfew around central Paris landmarks in place, as the country's worst unrest in years continued over the cost of living and school conditions. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said more than 2,500 people have been taken into custody since the violence began, with dozens of criminal investigations still open. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu met union leaders Thursday as the government tries to calm tensions ahead of a planned November 5 union mobilization.

Sources: AP (via PBS), France 24

Truckers' diesel protest empties supermarket shelves and strands planes across southern Italy

A week-long truckers' blockade over diesel prices left supermarket shelves and fuel pumps empty in Naples, with shortages also reported in Sicily, Calabria and Puglia, authorities said. The airports of Crotone and Reggio Calabria ran out of aircraft fuel, and trade group Federdistribuzione warned that fresh food was rotting in warehouses as stockpiling worsened the shortages. Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni called an emergency cabinet meeting Thursday to approve relief measures for hauliers and farmers, after clashes with police and looting were reported in Naples and smaller protests disrupted Turin and Bologna.

Sources: AP (via Rochester First), Euronews

Middle East

UK sends RAF Typhoon jets to defend Saudi Arabia from Houthi attacks

Britain's Ministry of Defence said it will deploy RAF Typhoon fighter jets, an air-to-air refuelling tanker and military advisers to help defend Saudi cities and infrastructure against Houthi missile and drone attacks, after strikes on Riyadh's international airport and Saudi Aramco facilities killed and wounded hundreds of people in recent weeks. Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said the mission is strictly defensive and the jets will not strike targets inside Yemen. The deployment, one of new Prime Minister Andy Burnham's first major foreign-policy decisions in office, extends years of UK training and refuelling support to the Saudi-led coalition in Yemen's civil war.

Sources: Reuters (via Washington Post), CNBC

Israeli strikes and demolitions continue in south Lebanon as US-hosted military talks yield no breakthrough

Israeli forces carried out air strikes and a structure demolition this week in the southern Lebanese village of Kfar Tebnit, Lebanon's state news agency reported, as a Lebanese military delegation held a second day of US-hosted talks with Israeli officers in Tampa, Florida, aimed at halting the attacks. Lebanon's army has rejected Israeli accusations that it failed to clear Hezbollah weapons from south of the Litani River, while Israeli Defense Minister Yisrael Katz has said forces will not withdraw from a southern buffer zone until Hezbollah disarms nationwide and warned of strikes on Beirut's southern suburbs if rocket fire resumes. No outcome from the Tampa talks has been confirmed.

Sources: Reuters (via Internazionale), Middle East Eye

Africa

Burkina Faso's junta-led parliament reinstates the death penalty

Burkina Faso's legislature, appointed by the ruling military junta, unanimously adopted a new penal code this week that reinstates capital punishment for terrorism, treason, espionage and parricide, reversing the country's 2018 abolition. Justice Minister Edasso Rodrigue Bayala argued the previous code's leniency had helped armed groups recruit fighters amid an ongoing jihadist insurgency, while Human Rights Watch and Amnesty International warned the law could also be used to suppress government critics. Burkina Faso has not carried out an execution since 1988, according to Human Rights Watch.

Sources: AP (via Gazettextra), Bloomberg

Abyei market drone-strike toll keeps climbing as South Sudan protests to Khartoum

The death toll from Monday's drone strike on a market in the disputed Abyei region kept rising this week past 153, with a local official telling AFP it was likely to climb further as rescuers said many victims remain missing. South Sudan's foreign ministry has summoned Sudan's ambassador in Juba to protest the strike, which it blames on Sudan's military, while a Sudanese army officer has said the attack hit fuel tankers rather than civilians directly. The final casualty figure and attribution for the strike have not been independently confirmed.

Sources: AFP (via The National), Sudan Tribune

Asia

Kim Yo Jong threatens to activate anti-South loudspeakers "within days" as Seoul weighs response

North Korean leader Kim Jong Un's sister, Kim Yo Jong, said anti-South Korea loudspeaker broadcasts along the border will be ready "within days" and that unspecified countermeasures have been placed on alert, days after she first said Pyongyang was considering restarting the broadcasts and deploying more weapons there. South Korea's defense ministry said it is considering a "proportionate measure" in response but has made no decision, as a standoff that began with border mine blasts in September continues to escalate. Kim has warned North Korean troops would "mercilessly eliminate" South Korean forces if warning fire harmed North Korean personnel or territory.

Sources: Reuters (via Internazionale), Korea Times

Taiwan and China trade sovereignty broadcasts as coast guard standoff near Pratas Islands continues

A Chinese coast guard vessel that entered restricted waters near Taiwan's Pratas Islands this week broadcast that "the Republic of China does not exist," prompting a Taiwanese patrol ship to respond that Taiwan is a sovereign, independent country, Taiwan's coast guard said. China's government said its coast guard was merely "safeguarding order" around the atoll, while Taiwan accused Beijing of trying to assert jurisdiction over waters it does not control; the standoff follows an October 1 incident in which Taiwan's coast guard fired a water cannon at a Chinese vessel and an October 18 episode in which Taiwan said it drove off 16 Chinese ships. No resolution to the standoff has been reported.

Sources: Reuters (via Internazionale), Taipei Times
