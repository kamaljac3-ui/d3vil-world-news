Americas

Paraguay's ousted president moved to a military prison after reports of special treatment

Former President Santiago Peña was transferred to the Viñas Cué military prison on Monday, days after Paraguay's Senate removed him from office over the central bank's seizure of Ueno Bank, the Associated Press reported. A judge ordered the move after local media showed he had access to a private gym and a furnished suite at the Presidential Escort Regiment where he was first held; Vice President Pedro Alliana, now president, dismissed the regiment's commander over the special treatment. Peña faces trial over the bank's collapse and authorities have searched two of his four homes.

Sources: Washington Times (AP), WRAL (AP)

Chile's Congress freezes fuel prices, defusing a nationwide truckers' strike

Chile's Congress passed legislation Monday night capping gasoline and diesel prices into 2027, resolving a nationwide truckers' strike that had blockaded highways including Route 68 since early November over the sixth consecutive fuel-price increase since July, which followed President José Antonio Kast's government narrowing the country's price-stabilization mechanism by decree in March. The bill followed an agreement with major trucking confederations, according to Chilean outlet BioBioChile; Reuters had reported the blockades disrupting freight and mining supply routes nationwide earlier in the strike.

Sources: AJOT (Reuters), BioBioChile

Europe

Serbia's new parliament convenes with student-backed movement holding a commanding majority

Serbia's newly elected parliament held its first session Tuesday, with the student-backed Student List holding 140 of 250 seats to 103 for former President Aleksandar Vučić's Serbian Progressive Party, the Associated Press reported. The movement won more than 53% of the vote in last month's election, enough to govern alone; lawmakers are now set to elect a parliamentary speaker before forming a new government within 90 days, Euronews reported.

Sources: WRAL (AP), Euronews

Ukraine, US and European officials hold first joint peace talks as the war grinds on

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said Monday that Kyiv held new online talks with US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, joined for the first time by European officials, with all sides agreeing to a further round in Berlin with the "Coalition of the Willing" nations backing Ukraine, according to Reuters. Zelensky, who met US envoys in Abu Dhabi and Dublin earlier this month, said any lasting deal would require credible security guarantees and that he remained unsure whether NATO membership was still on the table; Russia has not confirmed it will accept European participation in the next round, Al Jazeera reported.

Sources: U.S. News (Reuters), Al Jazeera

Middle East

Iran strikes US-linked base in Bahrain for the third time in less than a week

Bahrain's air defenses intercepted a fresh wave of Iranian drones targeting a radar installation at Sheikh Isa Air Base on Tuesday, the third such attack in five days, according to Al Jazeera. Iran's Revolutionary Guard said the strikes retaliated for "hostile US actions" tied to the Strait of Hormuz, while Bahrain's foreign ministry condemned the attack; a nearly identical strike claim two days earlier was the first in the Gulf kingdom since September, Reuters reported, and the US military said it intercepted that one without damage or casualties.

Sources: Al Jazeera, BusinessDay (Reuters)

Saudi Arabia replaces Yemen coalition commander as Houthi forces close in on Aden

Saudi Arabia's King Salman issued a decree replacing Lt. Gen. Fahd bin Hamad Al-Salman as commander of the kingdom's Joint Forces with chief of staff Gen. Fayyad Al-Ruwaili, after Houthi fighters advanced along Yemen's southern coast following the government's retreat from the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, Reuters reported. Western diplomats linked the change directly to the battlefield losses; Houthi forces have seized the coastal area of Khor Omairah, roughly 100 kilometers from Aden, and are now within 25 kilometers of government positions, military sources told Reuters.

Sources: The National (Reuters), Korea Times (Reuters)

Africa

Mozambique's top opposition leader goes on trial over 2024 election protests

Venâncio Mondlane's trial opened Tuesday at Mozambique's Supreme Court in Maputo, where he faces five charges including incitement to terrorism tied to deadly protests after the disputed October 2024 election, the Associated Press reported. Mondlane, who denies the charges and could face more than 20 years in prison if convicted, said outside court that he arrived "calm and confident" in his legal team; the court barred live broadcasts of the proceedings but will allow public attendance and its own audio-visual recording, Club of Mozambique reported.

Sources: WRAL (AP), Club of Mozambique

Two unrelated mass shootings in South Africa kill 17 in a single night

Police said 17 people were killed in two separate mass shootings roughly 530 kilometers apart on Sunday night, the Associated Press reported: nine people were shot dead inside a shack in the Jeffsville informal settlement near Pretoria, and eight men were found dead in a bullet-riddled vehicle outside Pietermaritzburg in KwaZulu-Natal. National Police Commissioner Fannie Masemola condemned the killings and said investigators were examining a possible link between the Pretoria shooting and a similar attack that killed 12 people in nearby Saulsville last year; no arrests have been made and a motive has not been established in either case, Al Jazeera reported.

Sources: ABC News (AP), Al Jazeera

Asia

Trump arrives in Manila for ASEAN summit, signs trade statement with Thailand

President Trump landed in Manila Tuesday evening for the 49th ASEAN Summit, opening a three-day Asia trip that continues on to China for meetings with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin, the Associated Press reported. Trump met Quad leaders from Japan, India and Australia and signed a joint statement with Thai Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul marking "significant progress" on a reciprocal trade agreement, though Thai officials said the document falls short of a final deal, according to AFP. The summit closed out the Philippines' year as ASEAN chair, with the bloc handing the rotating chairmanship to Singapore for 2027.

Sources: NBC San Diego (AP), Yahoo News Singapore (AFP)

US tightens rule blocking China's remote access to advanced AI chips

The US Commerce Department issued an interim rule Monday requiring data-center operators in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil to obtain a US license before supplying advanced AI-chip computing power to customers in China, closing a loophole that let Chinese firms rent remote access to top-tier Nvidia chips they are barred from importing directly, Reuters reported. The rule, which also rescinds the Biden-era AI Diffusion framework, lands as Trump travels to Asia for meetings with Xi Jinping; Beijing has signaled it will retaliate.

Sources: Investing.com (Reuters), Al Jazeera
