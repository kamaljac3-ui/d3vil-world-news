Americas

Democrats retake full control of Congress in a rebuke to Trump's second term

Democrats won majorities in both the House and Senate in Tuesday's midterm elections, the Associated Press reported, flipping control of Congress for the final two years of President Trump's term. The AP said Democrats crossed the 218-seat threshold needed for a House majority, while the party's Senate count climbed past 50 with a handful of races, including Mississippi, still being finalized. Democratic gains included seats in states Trump carried comfortably in 2024, and House Speaker Mike Johnson said he will not seek the minority leader post after losing his majority.

Sources: AP (via Al Jazeera)

Paraguay lawmakers file impeachment charges against President Peña over bank collapse

Thirty-five Paraguayan lawmakers filed impeachment charges this week against President Santiago Peña and Vice President Pedro Alliana after the central bank seized Ueno Bank, a fast-growing digital lender whose roughly 3 million customers — in a country of 6.5 million — lost access to their accounts, AFP and Reuters reported. The charges cite Peña's past financial ties to Ueno's parent company; Reuters reported the opposition remains short of the two-thirds majority needed to advance his removal, though it has drawn support from some dissident lawmakers in Peña's own Colorado Party. Central bank governor Carlos Carvallo, who previously led Ueno, resigned this week amid the fallout, and eight bank executives have been detained.

Sources: AFP/Reuters (via UPI), Reuters (via WMBD)

Europe

Russia and Ukraine trade fresh strikes as the toll from a Zaporizhzhia aid-site attack holds at 31

Ukraine's air force said it intercepted 272 of 310 Russian drones launched overnight into Wednesday, while Russia's defense ministry said its forces struck a Nova Poshta delivery terminal and a ship, and Ukraine said it struck an oil facility in Russia's Ryazan region, Al Jazeera reported. The exchange follows Monday's Russian strike on a school used as an aid-distribution site in Zaporizhzhia region, which AP and Reuters reported killed at least 31 people, with dozens still missing; President Zelenskyy called it one of the deadliest attacks of the war. Russia's defense ministry says it targeted Ukrainian military units in the strike, a claim that has not been independently confirmed.

Sources: AP (via PBS NewsHour), Al Jazeera

Belgium's prime minister awaits the king's decision on his resignation after budget talks collapse

Belgian Prime Minister Bart De Wever offered his resignation to King Philippe on Monday after his five-party coalition failed to agree on roughly €10 billion in budget cuts and tax increases, Reuters reported. The king has withheld a decision while holding consultations with coalition leaders and is due to meet De Wever again on Friday, when he could send the coalition back to negotiate, ask another figure to form a government, or call early elections. Belgium must bring its deficit under the EU's 3% limit by 2029, and government bond yields have risen as the deadlock continues.

Sources: VRT NWS (citing Reuters), The Brussels Times

Middle East

Israel's president certifies election results, opens coalition talks after Netanyahu's bloc falls short

Israeli President Isaac Herzog on Wednesday accepted as legally binding the certified results of last week's parliamentary election, the AP reported, after the Central Elections Committee said it found no evidence of irregularities affecting the outcome. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc won 53 seats to opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot's 48, with Arab-led parties taking a record 19 — leaving neither side the 61 seats needed for a majority. Herzog began consultations with party leaders Wednesday and will pick whoever he judges best placed to form a government, almost certainly Netanyahu or Eisenkot; if neither succeeds, Israel would hold another election early next year.

Sources: AP (via PBS NewsHour), Al Jazeera

Houthi forces advance to within 80 km of Aden, the closest since 2015

Yemen's Houthi forces have advanced to within about 80 kilometers of Aden, the seat of Yemen's internationally recognized government, Reuters reported, citing four military sources — the closest the group has come to the city since briefly entering it early in the 2015 war. The advance threatens the main road linking Aden to Taiz, Yemen's third-largest city, which Houthi forces have encircled since early October. Saudi Arabia's military said it intercepted a Houthi ballistic missile and drone aimed at the kingdom's south, and Yemen's government reshuffled three ministers as the fighting intensifies.

Sources: Reuters (via US News), Al-Monitor

Africa

South Africa's ANC loses further ground as municipal election results come in

South Africa's governing African National Congress held about 32% of the vote with roughly 12% of ballots counted in Wednesday's municipal elections, narrowly ahead of the Democratic Alliance on 30%, Reuters reported — a steep drop from the ANC's 46% showing in the last such vote in 2021. Analysts told Reuters an ANC-DA coalition would be the most stable option in major cities such as Johannesburg, though the ANC could instead partner with the Economic Freedom Fighters or Jacob Zuma's uMkhonto weSizwe party. It was the first local vote contested nationally by Zuma's MK party, with 546 parties competing; final results were expected by the weekend.

Sources: Reuters (via Polity), AP (via ABC News)

Ethiopia expels Sudanese diplomats as the two countries trade accusations over the RSF

Ethiopia ordered six staff at Sudan's consulate in the border town of Assosa to leave within 48 hours, Reuters reported, days after Sudan's government lodged a complaint with the UN Security Council and the African Union accusing Ethiopia of letting the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces launch drone strikes from Ethiopian territory. Ethiopia denies the accusation and in turn accuses Sudan's army of backing rebels in Ethiopia's Tigray region; Sudan responded by closing Ethiopia's consulate in Gedaref and expelling its diplomats. Both countries deny each other's claims, which Reuters says have raised concern the two countries' separate conflicts could become entangled.

Sources: Reuters (via Citizen Digital), Middle East Monitor

Asia

Indonesia's ousted presidential chief of staff arrested as wiretapping case escalates toward treason charge

Indonesian President Prabowo Subianto dismissed his chief of staff, former national police chief Listyo Sigit Prabowo, on Wednesday, a day after Listyo and two senior police officers were arrested on suspicion of wiretapping Prabowo, cabinet ministers, journalists and others since early 2025, Reuters reported. The president's office said the case could amount to treason, though officials have given differing accounts of its legal severity, and the motive and full list of surveillance targets have not been disclosed. The military said thousands of troops were being mobilized in Jakarta as a security precaution; analysts link the case to a rift between Prabowo and former president Joko Widodo, of whom Listyo was considered a loyalist.

Sources: Reuters (via Internazionale), Nikkei Asia

China sends record 49 military aircraft near Taiwan as the island's local elections near

Taiwan's defense ministry said it detected 49 Chinese military aircraft, six warships and six government vessels around the island in the 24 hours to November 3, CNA reported — the highest aircraft count recorded in 2026 — with 31 of the sorties crossing the median line into Taiwan's air defense identification zone. Taiwan's Mainland Affairs Council said it expects Beijing to further escalate military intimidation and election interference as campaigning for the island's local elections enters its final weeks. China's government did not comment on the figures reported by Taipei.

Sources: CNA (via Newswav), Taipei Times
