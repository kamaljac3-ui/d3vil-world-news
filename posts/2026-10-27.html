Americas

Machado returns to Venezuela from exile as Rodríguez dismisses it as a "show"

Opposition leader María Corina Machado arrived back in Venezuela after a period in exile, with US Secretary of State Marco Rubio saying Washington had asked weeks earlier that she be allowed to return. Acting President Delcy Rodríguez dismissed the return as a "show" on state television and denied that political persecution exists in the country, while demanding Machado show remorse for having backed outside intervention. A reported transition roadmap would eventually have Rodríguez declare the presidency vacant to clear the way toward elections in 2027.

Sources: AP (via CBS Miami), France 24

Bolsonaro's own party recognizes Lula's win over his objections as Trump calls vote "rigged"

Flávio Bolsonaro's Liberal Party said Monday it accepts Brazil's electoral court certification of Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva's runoff victory, a day after Bolsonaro himself refused to concede and vowed to seek the result's annulment. Party president Valdemar Costa Neto said it would still press for answers on pre-election government spending, while Bolsonaro has said he will lead the opposition and has surrendered his passport under a court-ordered travel ban. President Trump said the election "looked like another rigged election" without offering evidence, a claim Brazil's electoral court chief rejected.

Sources: AP (via Japan Times), Rio Times

Europe

Serbia's Vučić concedes defeat to student-led movement in snap election

President Aleksandar Vučić conceded defeat Sunday in Serbia's snap parliamentary election, acknowledging that the student-led Student List had won enough seats to govern alone in the 250-seat parliament. Preliminary results put the Student List near 52% of the vote against about 39% for Vučić's Serbian Progressive Party, with turnout reaching its highest level since Yugoslavia's dissolution. "It is clear that we have lost these elections," Vučić said, blaming foreign interference for the loss. The movement grew out of nearly two years of protests following a 2024 railway-station canopy collapse that killed 16 people.

Sources: Al Jazeera, NPR

Russian missile and drone attack kills one, injures more than a dozen in Kyiv

A Russian ballistic missile and drone attack on Kyiv overnight killed one woman and injured at least 14 people, Ukrainian officials said, with strikes damaging a university campus, a metro bridge and nearby residential buildings. Ukraine's air force said Russia launched ballistic missiles and roughly 280 drones overnight, and that air defenses intercepted or suppressed 255 of the drones along with two ballistic missiles. Separately, a UN-mandated commission of inquiry said Monday that Russian forces have committed war crimes and crimes against humanity by targeting Ukrainian civilians and energy infrastructure.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), UN News

Middle East

Israelis vote in tight Netanyahu-Eisenkot contest with outcome too close to call

Israelis voted Tuesday in a parliamentary election pitting Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu against former military chief Gadi Eisenkot, with final pre-election polls showing neither bloc clearing the 61-seat majority needed in the 120-seat Knesset. Turnout was reported running well ahead of recent elections by midday, and exit polls were expected shortly after voting closed Tuesday night, though a final governing outcome could take weeks to resolve. Results had not been confirmed at the time of publication.

Sources: NPR, Al Jazeera

Houthis claim missile strike on Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura oil refinery

Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthi movement said Tuesday it struck Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura oil refinery, one of the kingdom's largest with a capacity of 550,000 barrels a day, saying a ballistic missile caused a fire at the site. Saudi Arabia had not confirmed the attack as of Tuesday. The refinery has been targeted repeatedly during the broader regional conflict, including a drone strike in March that forced a partial shutdown.

Sources: Reuters (via US News), Malay Mail

UN commission says Gaza ceasefire has not stopped Israeli "genocidal acts"

A UN-mandated independent commission of inquiry said Israeli forces have continued genocidal acts and crimes against humanity in Gaza and the West Bank despite last year's ceasefire, reporting that Israeli-held territory in Gaza has expanded from 53% to at least 60% since the truce began. The commission said Israeli fire along the advancing front has killed at least 196 Palestinians, including 43 children, since the ceasefire took hold, while Gaza's health ministry puts the broader post-ceasefire death toll above 1,400. Israel's mission in Geneva rejected the findings, calling the report part of a "misinformation campaign" and calling for the commission to be dissolved.

Sources: AP (via US News), The National

Africa

More than 60 Orthodox Christians killed in attacks in Ethiopia's Oromia region

The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church said at least 60 people were killed in two separate attacks on Christian villages in Oromia's Arsi Zone this month, with 14 killed on October 20 in Merti district and 46 more on October 24 in Shirka district. AFP, citing residents and the ACLED conflict monitor, put the Shirka toll at a minimum of 43 dead and 18 wounded, reporting that an unidentified armed group attacked the villages; no group has claimed responsibility. Ethiopia's government has previously blamed the Oromo Liberation Army for similar attacks, which the group denies, and regional authorities have pledged to pursue those responsible.

Sources: Al Jazeera (AFP), The Namibian

Drone strike on market in Sudan's Nyala kills at least 21, wounds 40

A drone strike hit a market in northern Nyala, the Rapid Support Forces' de facto capital in Darfur, around 8 p.m. Monday, killing at least 21 people and wounding more than 40, AFP reported, citing a medical source and witnesses. No group had claimed responsibility as of Tuesday, and residents said the market was frequented by both civilians and RSF fighters. The UN says drone strikes killed more than 300 people in Sudan in the first three weeks of October alone, as Sudan's army presses an aerial campaign against RSF-held areas of Darfur.

Sources: Arab News (AFP), Yahoo News (AFP)

Asia

Dam protests turn violent in India's Arunachal Pradesh, near China border

Protesters set fire to a hydropower company office and a police station in India's Arunachal Pradesh on Friday night during demonstrations against the planned 11,200-megawatt Siang Upper Multipurpose Project, state Chief Minister Pema Khandu said. Police fired tear gas to disperse crowds, and about 40 officers were injured, AFP reported, with the clashes following an earlier confrontation over a 12-hour shutdown called by dam opponents. India casts the dam as a counterweight to a far larger Chinese hydropower project upstream in Tibet, and New Delhi's foreign ministry says the two countries remain in contact over broader border tensions.

Sources: Arab News (AFP), Manila Times

Pakistan rejects civilian-death claims as border conflict with Afghan Taliban continues

Pakistan's foreign ministry again rejected Afghan and UN accounts that its airstrikes have killed civilians, saying operations against "terrorists and their logistical support" in Afghanistan have been restrained, as border clashes with Taliban forces continued into a third week. The UN mission in Afghanistan has said at least 10 civilians, most of them children, were killed in an earlier round of strikes, while the Associated Press reported the attacks hit homes in Helmand and Kunar provinces; the Taliban called the strikes "an act of aggression." Pakistani trade data show exports to Afghanistan have collapsed by more than 88% this fiscal year as Islamabad keeps border restrictions in place pending Taliban action against militants it blames for a September 18 suicide bombing.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), Dawn
