Americas

Brazil excludes Milei from Lula's inauguration as diplomatic rift with Argentina hardens

Brazil's government will not invite Argentine President Javier Milei to President-elect Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva's January 5 inauguration, a Brazilian government source said this week, extending a rift that began after Milei called Lula a "thief" at a July campaign rally for a rival candidate. Brasília recalled its ambassador to Buenos Aires in July and formally downgraded relations to the level of chargé d'affaires in August, and Argentina's own ambassador to Brazil has since left his post as well. Argentina's foreign minister said Buenos Aires had received no "official confirmation" of the snub, and Milei has still not recognized Lula's election win.

Sources: AFP (via France 24), Buenos Aires Times

Colombia charges two suspects after Cartagena supermarket hostage siege freed dozens

Colombian prosecutors have charged two men with aggravated kidnapping after a failed armored-truck robbery turned into an overnight hostage standoff at an Éxito supermarket in Cartagena, with security forces storming the store before dawn on November 25 to free roughly 30 hostages, AP reported. President Abelardo de la Espriella said one of the captors was killed in the rescue operation, and authorities said the gunmen had initially demanded about $2 million and safe passage out of the store. The siege, which lasted more than 15 hours, came amid what former president Gustavo Petro called the country's worst wave of violence in a decade.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), World Today News

Europe

EU summit ends without budget or Ukraine loan deal, pushing fight to December

European Union leaders broke up an extraordinary summit in Brussels on November 27 without agreement on either the bloc's 2028-2034 long-term budget or a proposed €45 billion loan for Ukraine, with Slovakia, Hungary and Czechia still refusing to back the loan's military-funding component, Reuters reported. Germany and the Netherlands separately pushed back on the overall size of the budget, and leaders agreed only to try again at a summit on December 17-18. Commission President Ursula von der Leyen had offered holdout states the option of joining only the loan's €20 billion civilian-support strand while staying out of its €25 billion military strand.

Sources: Reuters (via U.S. News), The Copenhagen Post

Russia strikes two foreign grain ships near Ukraine's Odesa, Kyiv says

Russian forces struck two foreign-flagged cargo ships carrying more than 100,000 metric tons of grain near the Ukrainian port of Odesa overnight on November 25-26, Ukrainian Prime Minister Sergiy Koretskyi said, according to Reuters. Russia's defense ministry confirmed striking a separate dry cargo vessel south of Odesa, saying it carried "dual-purpose" cargo for Ukraine's military, a claim Reuters said it could not independently verify. Ukrainian grain exports through Odesa's ports have fallen sharply since late July amid intensified Russian strikes on terminals and merchant vessels.

Sources: Reuters (via U.S. News), Cyprus Mail

Middle East

Israel kills third senior Hamas official in Gaza in three days

An Israeli strike on an apartment in Deir al-Balah early on November 26 killed Khaled Abu Nada, identified by Hamas and Israeli officials as the group's deputy education minister in Gaza, along with his wife, Reuters reported. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Defense Minister Israel Katz said the strike was part of a campaign against Hamas officials they accuse of violating last year's ceasefire, and said Israeli forces have killed roughly 80 Hamas militants in the past month; Abu Nada was the third senior Hamas official killed in three days. Gaza's Hamas-run government media office says more than 1,600 Palestinians have been killed in ceasefire violations since it took effect in October 2025, a figure not independently verified.

Sources: Reuters (via Yahoo News), Al Jazeera

Iran vows to keep tightening Hormuz closure as Trump renews strike threat

Iran's army chief, Maj. Gen. Amir Hatami, said this week that the Strait of Hormuz will stay closed to shipping and grow "increasingly closed" until Tehran's seven conditions for de-escalation are met, IANS reported, as mediation by Qatar and Pakistan continues. President Trump, speaking by phone with US troops on Thanksgiving, said Washington would "absolutely hit" Iran's fortified Pickaxe Mountain nuclear site if fighting resumes, though he said no new activity had been detected there and set no timetable for renewed strikes. Iranian claims of mine strikes on tankers in the strait have not been independently verified.

Sources: IANS (via Social News XYZ), Business Standard

Africa

Guinea-Bissau's military government delays elections to 2028, extending its rule

Guinea-Bissau's transitional military government, led by Maj. Gen. Horta Inta-a, issued a decree on November 26 postponing presidential and legislative elections from December to November 2028, two years later than the one-year transition it had promised, Reuters reported. The announcement came on the first anniversary of the coup that ousted elected President Umaro Sissoco Embaló three days after a disputed general election whose results were never finalized. The junta cited a lack of the "technical, material and financial" resources needed to organize a credible vote, while the regional bloc ECOWAS had urged a swift return to civilian rule.

Sources: Reuters (via U.S. News), TimesLIVE

Fighting in eastern Congo forces Ebola response teams to evacuate

Escalating clashes between Congolese government forces and the armed group CRP north of Bunia, in Ituri province, forced health workers to evacuate parts of the area this week, disrupting the response to one of the country's largest Ebola outbreaks on record, AP reported. UN peacekeepers helped evacuate about ten health workers, including two from the World Health Organization, from a hospital near the fighting, and thousands of civilians have fled toward Bunia since Monday. The CRP said it was suspending its advance toward the city, while Congolese authorities said government forces had pushed the group back; the outbreak has infected more than 12,000 people.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), CIDRAP

Asia

Indonesia's Prabowo nominates two ministers for vacant vice presidency

Indonesian President Prabowo Subianto submitted Agriculture Minister Andi Amran Sulaiman and Coordinating Minister Sugiono as nominees to fill the country's vacant vice presidency, Antara reported, after Gibran Rakabuming Raka resigned the post on November 12. The People's Consultative Assembly, or MPR, postponed its plenary session to select and swear in a new vice president to December 11 and said the two nominees, both sitting ministers, do not need to resign their posts to run. Parliament's two largest parties, Gerindra and Golkar, have both lined up behind Sugiono ahead of the vote, which will be decided by lawmakers rather than a public ballot.

Sources: Antara, The Jakarta Post

US jury convicts Huawei of fraud and Iran sanctions violations

A federal jury in New York convicted Chinese technology company Huawei and three of its subsidiaries on November 24 of bank fraud and sanctions violations tied to concealed business dealings in Iran, along with trade-secret theft, Reuters reported. Prosecutors said Huawei ran its Iran operations through a front company, Skycom, while lying to banks about the relationship; jurors acquitted the company on a separate racketeering conspiracy charge. Huawei's lawyer said the company would appeal, with sentencing for the convicted entities set for September 2027.

Sources: Reuters (via U.S. News), CNN
