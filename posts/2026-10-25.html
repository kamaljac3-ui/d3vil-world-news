Americas

Brazil votes in razor-thin runoff between Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro

Brazilians voted Sunday in a presidential runoff between President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, with polling averages showing the race inside the margin of error. Flávio led the October 4 first round with 47.03% of valid votes to Lula's 45.16%, and final pre-runoff polls split narrowly in his favor. Brazil's electoral court began publishing results after polls closed at 5 p.m. Brasília time. The winner had not been declared as this edition went to press.

Sources: AP (via NBC News), Al Jazeera

Bukele disputes that bodies found in Haiti are the four missing Salvadoran soldiers

Salvadoran President Nayib Bukele said Saturday that four badly burned bodies recovered outside Port-au-Prince are not the four aviation technicians who went missing after a gang ambush on their armored vehicle near Croix-des-Bouquets on October 18, despite a New York Times report that officials believed the remains were the missing men. Bukele said on social media the bodies included a child, a man considerably taller than the technicians, and identification from a Chadian soldier; a Salvadoran official told the AP the bodies' identities remain unconfirmed pending autopsies. The identities of the four missing soldiers and the recovered remains have not been independently confirmed.

Sources: AP (via Washington Post), EFE (via Infobae)

Europe

Mass protests sweep France, Italy and Spain for a second straight weekend

More than 400,000 people protested across France on Saturday over the cost of living and public services, with nearly 1,000 people detained nationwide and clashes reported in Marseille and Bordeaux, France's interior ministry said. The unrest spread the same day to Italy, where police briefly detained 371 people across eight cities including Milan, Rome and Naples and said more than 300 protesters and officers were injured, and to Spain, where pensioners marched in Madrid over retirement benefits. Demonstrations also took place in Albania and Portugal.

Sources: Euronews, Al Jazeera

Russia and Ukraine agree to Oleshky ceasefire, plan November peace talks in Abu Dhabi

Russia and Ukraine agreed to a ceasefire in the occupied town of Oleshky, brokered through US first lady Melania Trump, set to take effect October 27 to allow aid deliveries and evacuations from a town whose population has fallen from roughly 24,000 before the war to as few as 1,700. President Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukrainian, Russian and US negotiators had agreed to hold technical-level talks in Abu Dhabi in the first half of November, following meetings between US envoy Steve Witkoff and President Vladimir Putin in St. Petersburg. Russia continues to demand Ukraine cede all of the Donbas region, including territory it does not control militarily, which Kyiv rejects.

Sources: Reuters (via US News), The Globe and Mail

Middle East

Trump says new Iran strike possible "maybe tomorrow" as Iran vows to bar US warships from Gulf

President Trump told reporters Saturday that Washington could strike Iran soon, saying "we're thinking about doing it, and maybe tomorrow," while also floating a "big, beautiful" diplomatic agreement as an alternative. Iran's armed forces chief of staff, Gen. Ali Abdollahi, said Tehran would not allow US military vessels to re-enter the Persian Gulf "under any circumstances," as the US maintains two aircraft carrier strike groups in the region with a third approaching. Iranian officials warned that any new attack would draw a response "several times greater" than before. No decision to strike has been publicly confirmed by either government.

Sources: AP (via Business Standard), Al Jazeera

Houthis claim seven strikes on Saudi airports and Aramco sites as Riyadh airport prepares to reopen

Yemen's Houthi rebels claimed attacks on Saudi Arabia's King Khalid and King Fahd international airports, an airbase at Khamis Mushait, and Aramco facilities including the Yanbu refinery and East-West pipeline on Saturday, part of what spokesman Yahya Saree called seven military operations. Saudi authorities did not confirm the claims but said they intercepted two ballistic missiles fired toward Yanbu and Taif; Riyadh's international airport said flights would resume Sunday after a two-week closure. Houthi media said separate Saudi-led strikes killed 11 people in Sanaa, a claim Riyadh has not addressed. Saudi Arabia has not confirmed which, if any, Houthi-claimed strikes hit their targets.

Sources: Al Jazeera, AFP (via France 24)

Africa

Ethiopian regional forces clash in Moyale, thousands flee into Kenya

Fighting between forces from Ethiopia's Somali and Oromia regions in the border town of Moyale has killed at least nine people and wounded roughly 30 since October 18, a hospital official told Reuters, after a dispute over a Somali regional flag turned violent. Kenyan county officials said about 2,500 people have sheltered in Kenyan Moyale, while a Kenya Red Cross worker put the number as high as 10,000; casualty and displacement figures have not been independently confirmed by either government. Death toll and displacement figures vary significantly by source and are not independently confirmed.

Sources: AFP (via Arab News), Addis Standard

Three charged after mob killing of foreign national in Durban unrest

South African authorities charged three men — Kebede Belachew, 38, Getachew Tirikaso, 48, and Wodaje Girma, 29 — with attempted murder and public violence over Thursday's mob killing of a foreign national in central Durban, local outlet IOL reported Saturday. The charges follow two weeks of anti-migrant unrest in the city that saw vehicles torched and businesses looted; soldiers remain deployed, and the "March and March" anti-immigration group has threatened a citywide shutdown on November 10.

Sources: IOL, Al Jazeera

Asia

Typhoon Champi nears China's coast after killing one, injuring 500 in Taiwan

Typhoon Champi weakened to a tropical storm as it tracked toward China's eastern coast on Sunday, a day after sweeping past Taiwan, where it killed one person and injured more than 500, Taiwan's fire department said. Shanghai evacuated roughly 62,400 residents and Zhejiang province moved about 11,500 people from coastal areas, while authorities suspended ferries, some rail service, and dozens of flights ahead of the storm's approach. China had not reported any deaths from the storm as of this edition.

Sources: Reuters (via Investing.com), Hong Kong Free Press

Seoul takes North Korea mine dispute to UN Security Council

South Korea sent a letter to the UN Security Council on October 22 urging North Korea to take responsibility for a September 21 landmine blast that wounded two to three South Korean soldiers at the border, after a joint investigation with the US-led UN Command found North Korean mines caused the explosion. North Korea has rejected the findings, accused Seoul of rigging the investigation, and threatened to resume propaganda loudspeaker broadcasts and station additional weapons near the border. Seoul began removing North Korean mines from the area on October 5 and is demanding an apology.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), The Korea Times
