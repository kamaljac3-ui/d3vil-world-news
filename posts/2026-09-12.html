Americas

The United States marks the 25th anniversary of the September 11 attacks

The National September 11 Memorial in New York held its 25th-anniversary ceremony Friday, with family members reading the names of the nearly 3,000 people killed in the 2001 attacks and seven moments of silence marking the times each tower was struck and fell, the strike on the Pentagon, and the crash of Flight 93. Additional commemorations followed across the city, including an FDNY ceremony for fallen firefighters and a memorial concert by the New York Philharmonic.

Sources: National September 11 Memorial & Museum, NYC.gov (FDNY)

Europe

Russian strike on a crowded Ukrainian shopping center kills at least five

A Russian strike hit the "Gulliver" shopping center in Pavlohrad, in Ukraine's Dnipropetrovsk region, during business hours Thursday, killing at least five people and injuring more than 60, including children, officials said. Reuters later reported the toll from the broader wave of overnight strikes on the area had risen to seven; the attack was part of a wider barrage of drones, artillery, and aerial bombs across Ukraine that also struck a residential building in Kyiv.

Sources: Kyiv Independent, Yahoo News (AP)

Middle East

Houthi forces seize a Red Sea port city, tightening their grip on a key shipping strait

Iran-aligned Houthi forces captured the Yemeni port city of Mocha along with the nearby Hanish and Mayun islands, extending their control over the waters around the Bab el-Mandeb Strait — one of the world's busiest oil-shipping chokepoints. Yemeni government military sources told Reuters the advance, guided by Iran's Revolutionary Guards, opens a new front in the wider war between Iran and the United States and raises the risk to international shipping through the strait.

Sources: NPR (Reuters), Foreign Policy

Saudi Arabia shuts a major oil pipeline after a drone attack it says was launched from Iraq

Saudi Arabia's foreign and energy ministries said several drones launched from Iraq struck its 1,201-kilometer East-West Pipeline, causing injuries and forcing a precautionary shutdown of the line, which carries crude to the Red Sea port of Yanbu. Riyadh said it would hold off retaliating at Baghdad's request; Iraq's government condemned the attack and said its prime minister had ordered an investigation into the militia responsible.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Arab News

Israel says it has destroyed Hezbollah's underground tunnel complex in southern Lebanon

Israel's military said it used more than 1,100 tonnes of explosives to demolish a Hezbollah tunnel network stretching over two kilometers beneath the Ali al-Taher ridge near Nabatieh, an operation large enough that the U.S. Geological Survey recorded the blast as a magnitude 4.1 tremor. Defense Minister Israel Katz said the operation completed Israeli control over its self-declared security zone in south Lebanon, and that troops would stay until Hezbollah disarms.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Al Arabiya

Africa

Fire kills at least 26 students at two schools in eastern Congo

A fire that started at a house spread into two adjoining schools in the rebel-held city of Bukavu, eastern Democratic Republic of Congo, killing at least 26 students — 19 girls and seven boys — and injuring dozens more, provincial officials said. A local administrator told The Associated Press the blaze broke out around 8 a.m. in a densely built neighborhood of wooden housing; some students died in a stampede while fleeing, others from smoke inhalation, and officials said the toll could still rise.

Sources: Washington Times (AP), Gulf News

Asia

Nepal's flood death toll passes 1,100, with nearly 4,000 still missing three weeks on

The confirmed death toll from the August 26 flash floods along the Nepal-Tibet border climbed to at least 1,114 as of Wednesday, up from 1,003 the day before, Nepali officials said, while the government's missing count stood at 3,916. The floods followed a glacier collapse above the Trishuli River; China separately reported 16 dead and about 546 missing in the Tibet Autonomous Region, and more than 21,000 Nepali security personnel remain deployed on search and rescue.

Sources: ABC News, Al Jazeera

Xi Jinping arrives in New Delhi for BRICS summit, his first India visit in seven years

Chinese President Xi Jinping traveled to New Delhi for the 18th BRICS summit, running September 11-13 at the Bharat Mandapam venue — his first trip to India since 2019. Xi is expected to hold a bilateral meeting with Prime Minister Narendra Modi on the sidelines, with the 11-member bloc's agenda covering food and energy security, trade, and multilateral-finance reform against the backdrop of the Ukraine war and the Iran-U.S. conflict.

Sources: South China Morning Post, Business Today
