Europe

Zelensky asks lawmakers to swiftly remove Ukraine's prosecutor general

President Volodymyr Zelensky called on parliament to accept Prosecutor General Ruslan Kravchenko's resignation, which Kravchenko submitted last week after a corruption investigation reached his office; he has not been charged or named a suspect himself. Kravchenko then signed a "suspicion notice" against the head of the National Anti-Corruption Bureau, prompting NABU and Ukraine's anti-corruption prosecutor's office to call it part of a "systematic attack on independent anti-corruption bodies." Parliament was expected to vote on the resignation Tuesday.

Middle East

Austria bars Iran's nuclear chief from the IAEA's Vienna conference after the US blocks a sanctions waiver

Austria denied entry to Mohammad Eslami, head of Iran's Atomic Energy Organization, after a UN Security Council committee rejected Vienna's request to exempt him from a travel ban reinstated last year under the "snapback" mechanism triggered by France, Britain, and Germany. The United States had urged Austria not to seek the waiver. Iran's ambassador to the IAEA called the exclusion "a serious breach" of Tehran's rights as a member nation — the first time in more than four years Iran has been shut out of the agency's top annual gathering.

Sources: ABC News (AP), Washington Times

Africa

Nigeria's parliament suspends engagement with South Africa over anti-migrant violence

Nigeria's National Assembly announced Friday it is suspending official visits to South Africa indefinitely and boycotting South African-hosted legislative activities, citing continuing xenophobic attacks on Nigerians and other African nationals. Nigeria's foreign ministry said at least 98 Nigerians have been killed in mob attacks and hate-related violence in South Africa since 2022; the assembly's clerk said the move was meant to protect Nigerians' "safety, dignity and welfare," not to undermine the countries' broader ties.

Sources: ABC News (AP), Washington Times
