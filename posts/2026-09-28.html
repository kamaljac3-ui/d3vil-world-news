Americas

Category 3 Hurricane Polo makes landfall in Mexico's Baja California Sur

Hurricane Polo, weakened from a peak Category 4, made landfall on the Pacific coast of Baja California Sur late Monday with sustained winds near 120 mph, Mexico's civil protection authorities and the U.S. National Hurricane Center said. The storm forced port closures, school cancellations and evacuations across the peninsula, and Mexico's navy and defense ministry deployed roughly 7,000 personnel and opened 169 shelters ahead of landfall. Forecasters warned the storm could dump 4 to 8 inches of rain as it crosses into the northern states of Sonora and Chihuahua, raising the risk of flooding and mudslides.

Sources: US News (AP), UPI

Venezuelans rally for opposition leader's return as blackout crisis deepens

Hundreds of supporters of opposition leader María Corina Machado rode through Caracas and marched in other state capitals on Sunday, demanding her return and new presidential elections. The rallies came amid what residents describe as the country's worst stretch of blackouts in years, with power cuts of eight to sixteen hours reported across western states; acting president Delcy Rodríguez has blamed record electricity demand, extreme heat and alleged sabotage for the outages.

Sources: Al Jazeera, US News (AP)

Europe

Serbia's Vučić resigns as president, will run for prime minister in snap election

Serbian President Aleksandar Vučić resigned Sunday, seven months before his term was due to end, clearing the way for him to run for prime minister in an Oct. 25 snap parliamentary election. Vučić, barred by the constitution from a third presidential term, said he would lead his ruling Serbian Progressive Party into the vote, which pits it against a youth-led anti-corruption movement that grew out of protests over a 2024 train-station canopy collapse in Novi Sad that killed 16 people. Prime Minister Ana Brnabić has assumed the presidency on an interim basis pending a new election.

Sources: US News (AP), Euronews

Russian drone barrage on Kyiv and Odesa kills 14, injures 57

Russia launched roughly 170 attack drones at Ukraine overnight into Sunday, killing 14 people and injuring 57 across Kyiv and Odesa, Ukrainian officials said. The strikes damaged a data center, a school and communications facilities in Kyiv, and hit a residential building, a hospital and a grain storage site in Odesa, according to local authorities.

Sources: Yahoo News (AP), Africanews

Middle East

Iran-US talks to resume this week after Trump rejects Hormuz ceasefire proposal

President Trump said Saturday he had rejected an Iranian proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and resume nuclear talks within seven days in exchange for lifting the U.S. naval blockade and sanctions on Iranian oil, but told reporters he expects a new round of indirect talks with Tehran this week. U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly, and Qatari mediators are continuing shuttle diplomacy between the two sides. Araghchi said Tehran remains open to a deal, stating "only a negotiated solution can break this deadlock," while warning Iran is also prepared to resume the war.

Sources: Yahoo News (AP), The Tribune (India)

Africa

Bandits abduct 40 women in Nigeria's Zamfara state, dozens more farmers in Niger state

Armed gangs abducted 40 women from the village of Yargada in Zamfara state on Sunday, a day after at least 47 farmers were abducted while harvesting peanuts in neighboring Niger state, according to security reports seen by AFP. The Zamfara attackers reportedly spent more than four hours in the community, torching six vehicles and looting shops before leaving with their captives. The kidnappings are the latest in a years-long wave of ransom abductions by armed groups known locally as "bandits" across northwestern and central Nigeria.

Sources: EWN (AFP), Macau Business (AFP)

Ethiopia's army pledges "restraint" as fears grow of a new civil war

Ethiopia's military said Sunday it is responding with "wisdom and restraint" to fighting that has spread from Tigray into the neighboring Afar and Amhara regions since seven armed groups announced an alliance last week aimed at toppling Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's government. Army Chief of Staff Field Marshal Berhanu Jula accused Eritrea of funding the alliance, which includes the Tigray People's Liberation Front and the Amhara Fano militia, while denying Ethiopia intends to invade Eritrea. The army has reported killing hundreds of fighters in the North Wollo zone as the conflict raises fears of a return to full-scale civil war.

Sources: Yahoo News (AP), Al Jazeera

Asia

Trump-Xi summit ends with little progress on Taiwan arms sales, trade

President Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping's second meeting this year ended last week with little resolved on trade or Taiwan, officials said, as Beijing pressed Washington to curb arms sales to the island without success. Xi urged the U.S. to handle Taiwan arms sales with "utmost caution," while a pending $14 billion arms package remains on hold with two more Trump-Xi meetings planned before year's end. Taiwan's government said it is watching the outcome closely and hopes U.S. arms sales will continue.

Sources: ABC News (AP), Taipei Times
