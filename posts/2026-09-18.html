Americas

UN mission tells Human Rights Council Venezuela's "repressive apparatus remains intact" despite reforms

The UN's Independent International Fact-Finding Mission on Venezuela told the Human Rights Council Friday that the state institutions responsible for repression, gross human rights violations, and crimes against humanity remain intact even as the country has seen limited political changes since the January 2026 US military intervention that led to Nicolás Maduro's capture. The mission said long-term detentions and enforced disappearances have declined significantly since then, but documented a recurring pattern of short-term arbitrary arrests meant to intimidate opposition activists, journalists, and civil society figures, with non-governmental groups estimating between 379 and 500 people still held as political prisoners.

Sources: UPI, OHCHR

Europe

Trump threatens "very heavy" tariffs on the EU if it makes Canada an associate member

President Trump threatened Wednesday to impose sweeping new tariffs on the European Union, or halt trade with it altogether, if the bloc proceeds with a plan to make Canada its first associate member, calling the proposal a potential "hostile act," according to Reuters. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen had floated the associate-membership plan as Canada and the EU pursue closer trade and security ties amid Ottawa's separate tariff dispute with Washington; the two sides are due to meet in Montreal in late October to work out details.

Sources: The Hill (Reuters), CNBC

Middle East

Houthi rebels complete their hold on the Bab el-Mandeb Strait as Saudi Arabia shuts a key oil pipeline

Yemen's Houthi rebels captured the islands of Perim and the Hanish archipelago in the Bab el-Mandeb Strait this month, Reuters reported, giving the group control of Yemen's entire Red Sea coastline and the narrow waterway linking the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden. Saudi Arabia shut down its East-West oil pipeline after a drone strike it attributed to the escalation, and Brent crude rose above $108 a barrel as the closure combined with ongoing blockades around the Strait of Hormuz to threaten a large share of the world's seaborne oil trade.

Sources: Al Jazeera, NPR

Africa

At least 37 illegal-mining suspects die in Nigerian security custody, sparking an investigation

At least 37 people arrested on suspicion of illegal mining in Nigeria's Niger State died in the custody of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defense Corps this week, officials said, with authorities initially blaming a suspected disease outbreak. A survivor told the AP that 65 detainees had been packed into a poorly ventilated cell, suggesting the deaths may have resulted from suffocation rather than illness; Niger State's governor declared three days of mourning as police opened an investigation.

Sources: The Hill (AP), Al Jazeera

Police clash with protesters across DR Congo as opposition rallies against a Tshisekedi third-term bid

Police fired tear gas and warning shots as demonstrators rallied in Kinshasa, Lubumbashi and Uvira on Tuesday against proposed constitutional changes that critics say would let President Félix Tshisekedi seek a third term, Reuters and AP reported. The Kinshasa protest, organized by the opposition C64 coalition, turned violent when clashes broke out between demonstrators and pro-government supporters, and police said at least 10 people were arrested.

Sources: WPRI (AP), Al Jazeera

Asia

Nepal raises its flood missing toll to over 6,000 as the combined Nepal-China death count passes 1,400

Nepal's disaster authority sharply raised the number of people missing after the August 26 glacial flood on the Nepal-Tibet border to 6,150, a spokesperson told AFP, after verifying names collected from district offices. Nepal has recovered 1,403 bodies so far, and combined with China's reported toll across the border, the flood has now killed at least 1,446 people and left more than 6,600 missing in the two countries.

Sources: Philstar (AFP), Al Jazeera
