Americas

Haiti police and private contractors kill 34 suspected gang members in Martissant raid

Haitian police, working alongside contractors from Vectus Global, a private security firm founded by Erik Prince, raided a stronghold of the Grand Ravine gang in Port-au-Prince's Martissant neighborhood this week, killing at least 34 suspected gang members, Al Jazeera and France 24 reported. Police said the operation targeted a hideout tied to gang leader Renel Destina, known as "Ti Lapli," though it remains unclear whether he was among the dead; officers said none of the roughly 2,500 Salvadoran troops stationed in Haiti took part. Martissant, a corridor linking the capital to Haiti's southern and western regions, has been under gang control since 2021.

Sources: Al Jazeera, France 24

Mexican forces capture FBI-wanted cartel boss "Chapo Isidro," sparking gun battles in Sinaloa

Mexico's Defense Secretariat said soldiers captured Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, known as "Chapo Isidro" and wanted by the FBI with a $5 million reward on his head, during an operation in Guasave, Sinaloa, according to EFE. Some local reports said authorities were still verifying the identity of those detained as of Saturday morning. The raid set off hours of gunfire, road blockades and burning vehicles across northern Sinaloa, including near a stadium where fans fled a baseball game in progress, amid an ongoing turf war between his faction and the rival Sinaloa Cartel.

Sources: La Hora (EFE), Newsweek

Europe

Denmark expels Russian defense attaché over foiled plot to assassinate Ukrainian-American rapper

Denmark ordered Russia's defense attaché to leave within seven days after its intelligence service, PET, said Russian security services planned to assassinate Ukrainian-American rapper Kyivstoner at a Copenhagen-area concert in September, Reuters reported. PET chief Rasmus Kieffer-Kristensen said the plan was “planned by the Russian intelligence services”; police arrested suspects linked to the Danish criminal underworld shortly before the show, which was cancelled, and two men now face charges including attempted murder. Russia's embassy in Copenhagen called the accusations "unsubstantiated," while Denmark is pressing fellow EU states to take "concrete actions" against Russia.

Sources: Reuters (via Yahoo), The Copenhagen Post

COP31 climate talks in Antalya run into overtime as revised draft drops fossil-fuel language

UN climate negotiations in Antalya, Turkey ran past their scheduled Friday close into a second extra day after a revised draft text removed references to transitioning away from fossil fuels and dropped a proposed $120 billion adaptation-finance target, Al Jazeera reported. Panama said it could not accept any deal that omits a specific cap on global oil production, and Irish broadcaster RTE reported tense late-night exchanges between the European Union and the Turkish and Australian COP presidencies over the text. Host Turkey separately announced more than $26 billion in national energy deals alongside the talks.

Sources: Al Jazeera, RTE

Middle East

Saudi-led coalition intercepts three Houthi missiles aimed at Riyadh, second attack on capital this week

The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted and destroyed three Houthi ballistic missiles targeting Riyadh early Saturday, the second attack on the Saudi capital in days after a Houthi strike hit the tower housing the kingdom's sovereign wealth fund on Thursday, Al Jazeera and Arab News reported. A Reuters witness saw a projectile streak across the Riyadh sky before a loud bang; the coalition also said it downed a drone headed toward southern Saudi Arabia. The Houthis have since threatened Jeddah and other Red Sea ports and warned airlines that Saudi airspace is unsafe, with fighting at its highest level since a rough 2022 truce.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Arab News

Iran strikes US-linked sites in Kuwait after American jets hit missile base on Qeshm Island

Iran said it targeted US drone and missile sites in Kuwait this week, its first attack on the Gulf state since September, hours after American fighter aircraft struck a Revolutionary Guard missile-launch site on Iran's Qeshm Island, Reuters reported. Kuwait's army said air defenses intercepted the incoming fire, and the Gulf Cooperation Council's secretary-general held Iran responsible for the escalation; Bahrain said it intercepted an Iranian attack for the third time in a week. Iranian state media gave conflicting accounts of a further explosion on Qeshm Island on Friday, first calling it a new missile strike before officials said it was the disposal of unexploded munitions.

Sources: Reuters (via Red Lake Nation News), Al Jazeera

Africa

Somalia expels UAE ambassador, accuses Abu Dhabi of an "open invasion" of its sovereignty

Somalia's government ordered the United Arab Emirates' ambassador to leave within 72 hours and recalled its own envoy from Abu Dhabi, accusing the UAE of violating its sovereignty, the Associated Press reported. Information Minister Abdifatah Kasim Mohamud did not detail what prompted the move, though President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud has previously accused the UAE of signing port deals in Berbera, Bosaso and Kismayo without federal approval. The UAE's foreign ministry called Somalia's decision "escalatory and hostile" and denied the allegations, in a dispute that follows Somalia's cancellation of all its agreements with the UAE in January.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), Al Jazeera

Ethiopia's Moyale border conflict displaces hundreds of thousands, pushes refugees into Kenya

Fighting between Oromo and Somali communities around the disputed town of Moyale, which resumed on November 16 after breaking out in October, has displaced close to 700,000 people in southern Ethiopia, aid group Islamic Relief says, while UN agencies separately count 30,000 to 50,000 people who have crossed into Kenya. The displacement figures vary widely between sources and have not been independently confirmed by a wire service. Al Jazeera reports the clashes began over a disputed regional administration office, and mediation by local elders and Ethiopia's federal government has so far failed to produce a lasting ceasefire.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Arab News Pakistan

Asia

Bangladesh unrest kills at least four as banned Awami League enforces nationwide lockdown

Political violence killed at least four people and set off arson attacks on police stations and ruling-party lawmakers' homes in several districts, including Gopalganj and Narail, ahead of a nationwide "lockdown" called by the banned Awami League, Reuters reported. The strike marked the first anniversary of a tribunal verdict sentencing former Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina to death in absentia; police arrested more than 100 suspected Awami League-linked activists in Dhaka within 24 hours. Prime Minister Tarique Rahman again blamed India for fueling the unrest, as Hasina has vowed to return to Bangladesh from exile in December.

Sources: U.S. News (Reuters), Dhaka Tribune

Trump calls China's nuclear buildup "hot and heavy," a day after Shenzhen summit with Xi and Putin

President Trump told reporters Friday that China is expanding its nuclear arsenal at a "hot and heavy" pace and predicted Beijing would match US and Russian stockpiles within a decade, the Associated Press reported, a day after his trilateral summit with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen. Trump's own figures, citing roughly 400-500 Chinese warheads against 4,000 for the US and 3,800 for Russia, diverge from the Pentagon's assessment that China's stockpile is in the low 600s and set to top 1,000 by 2030. Trump said the three leaders had agreed to hold further talks on nuclear proliferation and on "joint guardrails" for artificial intelligence.

Sources: AP (via Yahoo), AP (via WRAL)
