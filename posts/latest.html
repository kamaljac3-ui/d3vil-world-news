Americas

Paraguay's ousted president moved to military prison awaiting bank-collapse trial

A Paraguayan judge ordered former President Santiago Peña transferred to a military prison after reports he had been held in a suite with a private gym, the Associated Press reported. Peña, removed by the Senate on November 6 over the collapse of Ueno Bank, faces charges including breach of trust and criminal association, and denies wrongdoing; Vice President Pedro Alliana has been sworn in to serve out the term through 2028.

Sources: ABC News (AP), NBC News

Canada and India say a free trade deal is close after 16 years of on-and-off talks

Indian High Commissioner Dinesh Patnaik said Tuesday the two countries are on the cusp of finalizing a free trade agreement, with Prime Minister Narendra Modi expected to visit Canada in December to sign it, Canadian Press reported. Canada's trade minister wants the deal signed before year's end, and Patnaik said a defense cooperation agreement is also likely to be signed during Modi's visit; two-way trade reached $30.4 billion in 2025.

Sources: CTV News (Canadian Press), The Globe and Mail

Europe

UK-EU summit draft shows both the ambition and the limits of the post-Brexit reset

Britain and the EU are set to hold their second formal summit since Brexit in Brussels on Friday, and a leaked draft communique shows negotiators still disagree over integrating the UK into the EU's electricity market, the Associated Press and Euronews reported. Both sides have committed to concluding the broader negotiations, which also cover linking the UK and EU emissions trading systems, sometime in 2027.

Sources: ABC News (AP), Euronews

Odesa observes a day of mourning after Russian strikes kill at least four

Ukraine's Black Sea port city of Odesa marked a day of mourning Thursday for victims of Russian strikes that hit its UNESCO-listed city centre three times, killing at least four people and wounding 13, AFP reported. Regional governor Oleh Kiper said the dead included a local television producer, and President Zelensky said rescuers and police were targeted a second time after first responding to the initial strikes.

Sources: Al Arabiya (AFP), Al Jazeera

Middle East

Israeli tanks enter Hamas-held Gaza for the first time since the ceasefire

Israel's military said Thursday it had concluded a tank-backed raid into central Gaza, west of the agreed "Yellow Line," to destroy what it called a Hamas tunnel threatening its troops, telling AFP "the operation is completed." Hamas called the incursion, which forced dozens of displaced Palestinians to flee tents near Nuseirat, a "blatant ceasefire violation," while the US-backed body overseeing the truce separately told the UN Security Council both sides are falling short of their commitments and warned the ceasefire could collapse without action.

Sources: Arab News (AFP), Al Jazeera

Houthi missile damages Riyadh skyscraper housing Saudi wealth fund

A missile fired from Yemen damaged a tower in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District that houses offices of the Public Investment Fund, Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund, three sources told Reuters. It was the second such strike on the Saudi capital this month after a November 8 attack damaged a building used by Aramco; the Houthis had not claimed the attack, and the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted a separate missile threat toward the city.

Sources: ThePrint (Reuters), France 24 (AFP)

Africa

Ethiopia and Eritrea mass troops along their border, fueling war fears

Ethiopia and Eritrea have built up forces near the border town of Badme, conflict monitors and people with ties to militias in Ethiopia's Tigray region told Reuters, raising fears of a return to the kind of war that killed tens of thousands between 1998 and 2000. Eritrea's information minister dismissed the reports as "propaganda," while Ethiopia accused Eritrea of backing rebel fighters near the border, an allegation Eritrea denies.

Sources: Korea Times (Reuters), Al-Monitor (Reuters)

Senegal's Faye rules by decree on budget after his own party expels him

President Bassirou Diomaye Faye said Thursday he would implement Senegal's revised 2026 budget by decree, Reuters reported, after parliament rejected the measure and lawmakers loyal to his former ally, Speaker Ousmane Sonko, voted down a 2027 budget bill as well. The decree follows the ruling Pastef party's formal expulsion of Faye and 31 allies this week, deepening a rift that threatens the budget deals underpinning Senegal's IMF debt-relief program.

Sources: Sowetan (Reuters), Africanews

Asia

Trump, Xi and Putin hold first-ever trilateral summit, pledge dialogue on nuclear risk

US President Trump, China's Xi Jinping and Russia's Vladimir Putin met for about 90 minutes on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen and issued a joint declaration affirming that "nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought," with Reuters reporting the three agreed to hold further dialogue on nuclear risk. The declaration also touched on energy supply and UN reform, but set no timetable for arms talks and made no specific commitments on ending the war in Ukraine or the conflict involving Iran.

Sources: ThePrint (Reuters), NPR

Myanmar's junta leader says he is reconsidering the country's ties to ASEAN

Min Aung Hlaing told Myanmar's parliament Wednesday his government is reviewing its relationship with ASEAN, accusing the bloc of unfair treatment after it voted at its Manila summit to keep Myanmar limited to non-political representation, Reuters reported. He said Myanmar would keep talking with individual member states and announced plans for a domestic peace conference in February, while ASEAN said it could restore Myanmar's full standing if the junta makes progress on the bloc's five-point peace plan.

Sources: ThePrint (Reuters), The Japan Times
