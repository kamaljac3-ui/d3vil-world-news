Europe

US envoy Witkoff brings Putin an updated Ukraine peace plan in St. Petersburg

US special envoy Steve Witkoff met President Vladimir Putin at St. Petersburg's Constantine Palace on Wednesday, delivering an updated proposal to end the war in Ukraine a day after talks with President Volodymyr Zelensky in Oslo. Putin aide Yuri Ushakov said he could not say whether the meeting would produce an agreement, while Jared Kushner joined by video link and Witkoff said he hoped to revive three-way talks among Washington, Moscow and Kyiv. Russia separately said it would release detained American Stephen Hubbard, though no ceasefire breakthrough was announced. No final outcome of the talks has been confirmed.

Sources: AP (via Chronicle Online), Kyiv Post

Romania swears in new government after months of political deadlock

Romania's parliament confirmed independent diplomat Luca Niculescu as prime minister Tuesday with 280 votes in favor, and his cabinet was sworn in Wednesday, ending a crisis that began when Ilie Bolojan's government fell to a no-confidence vote in May. President Nicușor Dan's first three nominees for the post either withdrew or failed to advance before Niculescu, backed by the Social Democrats and Liberals, cleared parliament's 233-vote threshold. Niculescu pledged to prioritize budget revisions and energy security, though analysts called his minority coalition's backing fragile beyond a six-month working arrangement.

Sources: AP (via US News), Balkan Insight

Middle East

Houthi missile toll in Yemen training-camp strike rises to 73 dead

The death toll from Monday's Houthi ballistic-missile strike on a government security-forces training camp in Yemen's Shabwa province rose to 73 killed and 14 missing, provincial officials said, the deadliest attack since a truce collapsed three months ago. Yemeni military sources said three missiles hit the al-Alam camp, where newly trained internal-security recruits were stationed, and a Houthi spokesperson confirmed the group carried out the strike, calling the camp a Saudi-linked target. The UN said more than 250,000 people have been displaced inside Yemen since fighting resumed, and the US mission to Yemen condemned the attack.

Sources: AP (via US News), AP (via Japan Times)

Israeli strikes kill seven in Gaza as ceasefire violations continue

Israeli strikes on separate locations in Gaza killed seven Palestinians Tuesday, according to the territory's civil defense service and hospital sources, including a married couple killed in a pre-dawn strike on an apartment near Gaza City's Al-Shifa Medical Complex. Gaza's health ministry said the toll from strikes since last October's ceasefire has reached roughly 1,500 killed and more than 5,400 wounded; Israel's military said one strike targeted a Hamas militant it said took part in an October 7 hostage-taking. Casualty figures come from Gaza's health ministry and have not been independently verified.

Sources: AP (via KIRO 7), Al Jazeera

Africa

Nigeria confirms first Ebola case, in traveler who transited Uganda

Nigerian health officials confirmed the country's first Ebola case Tuesday, in a woman who developed symptoms after arriving in Abuja earlier this month; authorities said she is isolated and 44 contacts have been traced. Uganda's health ministry said she had transited the country after travel to eastern Democratic Republic of Congo, and Nigeria's ministry said the exact location where she was infected remains under investigation. The World Health Organization said it is supporting Nigeria's response, as Kenya separately reported nine Ebola infections amid a Congo outbreak that has killed more than 4,000 people since May.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), NPR

Drone strike on Abyei market kills at least 153 in disputed Sudan-South Sudan border zone

A drone strike hit the Amiet market near Abyei town on Monday, killing at least 153 people and sparking a fire still burning the next day, Abyei's caretaker information minister said, adding the toll could still rise. South Sudan's foreign ministry blamed Sudan's military and summoned Sudan's ambassador, while a witness told Reuters there was no sign of Rapid Support Forces fighters at the market, disputing Sudanese military claims that RSF forces operate in the area. The Associated Press said it could not independently verify who carried out the strike, and the UN said it was alarmed by the escalating drone war across Sudan. Attribution for the strike is disputed and has not been independently confirmed.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), Al Jazeera

Asia

North Korea orders border troops to take "resolute action" against the South

North Korean state media said army chief Pak Jong Chon toured border fortifications and ordered troops to take "prompt and resolute action" if South Korean forces threaten their safety, a day after Seoul vowed to punish any further provocation from the North. Kim Yo Jong, sister of leader Kim Jong Un, said new firepower units had taken up firing positions at the border, while Pak dismissed Seoul's accusations as an "absurd assertion." South Korea says the North has sent up to 7,000 workers to fortify the border this year and has blamed North Korean mines for wounding two South Korean soldiers last month.

Sources: Reuters (via US News), Reuters (via Daily Maverick)

China sends navy to shadow US warship as Taiwan reports separate standoff

China's military said Tuesday it sent naval forces to monitor the USS Charleston after the ship "illegally" entered waters near Sabina Shoal in the Spratly Islands; the US Navy's 7th Fleet said the vessel was conducting routine operations in international waters. Taiwan's coast guard separately reported a standoff further north near the Pratas Islands, where a Chinese coast guard vessel approached with its tracking transponder switched off before Taiwan's patrol ship Taichung intercepted it; no clashes were reported in either incident. Taiwan's defense ministry logged a sharp rise in Chinese military activity near the island in the 24 hours before the standoffs, including 14 aircraft sorties.

Sources: Reuters (via Internazionale), Jerusalem Post
