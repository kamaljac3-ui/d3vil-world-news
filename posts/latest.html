Americas

Magnitude 6.7 earthquake kills one, leaves hundreds homeless in Peru's central Andes

A magnitude 6.7 earthquake struck Peru's central Junín region late Friday, killing one person at a hospital in neighboring Huánuco and injuring at least 16, the Associated Press reported. Peru's civil defense agency, INDECI, later said the quake left 853 people without homes and damaged or destroyed more than 150 houses, with landslides blocking stretches of the highway linking Lima to the central Andes; the shaking was felt as far as the capital, though officials reported no damage to critical infrastructure.

Sources: AP (via Washington Post), Gestión

Colombian airstrike kills five FARC dissidents in 11th bombing of de la Espriella's government

Colombia's armed forces and national police killed five members of a FARC dissident faction loyal to "Iván Mordisco" in a pre-dawn strike on a rural camp in Mapiripán, Meta department, President Abelardo de la Espriella said Saturday, according to EFE. The operation, code-named Ninfa, targeted the group's 44th Front and was the 11th aerial strike ordered by de la Espriella's government since August; officials say the strikes have killed 77 people and led to 16 captures, though rights groups have raised concerns after earlier operations killed minors recruited by armed groups.

Sources: El Nacional (EFE), El Tiempo

Europe

Russian missiles and a drone fall in Poland in attack NATO calls a "grave violation"

Two Russian cruise missiles and a jet-powered drone crashed in southeastern Poland on Friday during one of Moscow's largest missile-and-drone barrages yet against Ukraine, Polish officials said, with the debris landing near a NATO military exercise involving troops from eleven countries. Prime Minister Donald Tusk called the strikes "an intentional provocation," while NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte described the episode as a "grave violation" of Polish airspace; officials reported no injuries or damage to critical infrastructure, and Poland said it would give the United States access to the missile debris.

Sources: Al Jazeera, France 24

Britain and the EU strike post-Brexit "reset" deal on trade, migration and youth mobility

The United Kingdom and European Union sealed a package of agreements at a Brussels summit Friday easing food-export checks, linking their emissions-trading schemes and launching a youth-mobility scheme, the Associated Press reported, a decade after Brexit. Prime Minister Andy Burnham called the deal a "landmark," and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen called it a "turning point," though the two sides pushed back a decision on university tuition fees for EU students by six months, and Britain's opposition parties called the package a "surrender" of sovereignty.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), NPR

Middle East

Iranian strike hits civilian industrial facility in Bahrain as Gulf war widens

Bahrain said an Iranian attack struck a civilian industrial facility on Sitra Island on Saturday, sparking a fire but causing no injuries, Reuters reported, after Iran's Revolutionary Guards said they had targeted US positions at Bahrain's Al-Riffa and Awali bases. Bahrain's defence force said its air defenses intercepted other incoming fire, and the strike came the same day two tankers were damaged near the Strait of Hormuz; the Gulf Cooperation Council and Jordan condemned the attack, and Bahrain asked the UN Security Council to act.

Sources: Reuters (via Yahoo), Al Jazeera

Israeli strike on Gaza vehicle kills three; Israel and Hamas dispute who they were

An Israeli drone strike killed three people in a vehicle in Gaza City's Tel al-Hawa neighborhood on Saturday, Gaza's Civil Defense told AFP, prompting Hamas to demand emergency talks with ceasefire mediators. Israel's military said the vehicle carried three Palestinian Islamic Jihad fighters posing as aid workers while transporting supplies to militants, calling it a ceasefire violation by the group, while Gaza's government said the dead were aid workers distributing food; the US charity World Central Kitchen denied the vehicle belonged to it. The identities of those killed remain disputed between Israeli and Palestinian accounts.

Sources: Daily Times (AFP), The Media Line

Africa

Congo's Ebola outbreak becomes second-deadliest on record as toll tops 5,700

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo has killed more than 5,700 people and infected over 11,700 since it was declared in May, making it the second-deadliest Ebola outbreak ever recorded behind only the 2014-2016 West African epidemic, the Associated Press and Time reported this week. The outbreak, caused by the Bundibugyo strain, has spread to eight of Congo's 26 provinces and reached Uganda, Kenya and Nigeria; the World Health Organization is keeping it classified as a public health emergency of international concern while two experimental vaccines undergo trials.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), Time

Dozens missing after migrant boat capsizes off Senegal's coast

A boat carrying roughly 100 migrants capsized before dawn this week off Senegal near the town of Yenne, killing at least four people and leaving dozens more missing, the Associated Press and AFP reported. Senegalese authorities recovered bodies at the Sendou and Yenne-Todd beaches and resumed search operations for the missing, part of a wave of departures that the UN refugee agency says has left nearly 600 people dead or missing along West Africa's Atlantic migration route so far this year.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), InfoMigrants (AFP/Reuters)

Asia

South Korea's top court finalizes ex-first lady Kim Keon-hee's four-year prison sentence

South Korea's Supreme Court upheld a four-year prison sentence for Kim Keon-hee, wife of impeached former President Yoon Suk-yeol, over stock-price manipulation and accepting luxury gifts from the Unification Church, Reuters reported. The court dismissed appeals from both Kim and prosecutors, finalizing fines and the forfeiture of a Graff necklace she had received as a gift; Kim separately faces a five-year sentence handed down by a lower court in an unrelated bribery case.

Sources: Reuters (via Yahoo), The Korea Herald

China widens trade curbs on Japan after Xi declines formal meeting with Takaichi

China's foreign ministry said Japan must "correct its wrong words and actions" on Taiwan after President Xi Jinping declined a formal meeting with Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi at an APEC gathering in Shenzhen, AFP reported, deepening a dispute that began over Takaichi's remarks on a potential Taiwan contingency. Beijing has restricted exports to 40 Japanese companies it links to Japan's "remilitarization," cut commercial flights and discouraged tourism to Japan, the Associated Press reported, while Takaichi said dialogue with Beijing "is important."

Sources: Taipei Times (AFP), Barchart (AP)
