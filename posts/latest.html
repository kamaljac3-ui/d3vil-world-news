Americas

Venezuela's Rodríguez promises elections without a date as Machado presses for 2027

Interim President Delcy Rodríguez said Thursday that Venezuela would hold elections once a new electoral authority is installed, but declined to set a date, while opposition leader María Corina Machado said in a separate news conference that the United States has proposed a vote by mid-2027. It was described as the clearest public split yet between the two since Washington backed Rodríguez's rise to the presidency after Nicolás Maduro's ouster in January, with a State Department official saying Washington wants a new Supreme Tribunal and electoral council installed before any vote is scheduled.

Sources: Reuters (via Investing.com), Axios

Brazil summons Argentina's envoy over Flávio Bolsonaro's escape to Miami

Brazil's foreign ministry summoned Argentina's chargé d'affaires on Thursday to demand an explanation after Senator Flávio Bolsonaro crossed into Argentina and flew on to Miami via Ecuador rather than surrender his passports under a Supreme Court order. Argentina denied receiving any formal request for explanations and rejected accusations it had helped him leave, while Brazil also recalled its ambassador to Buenos Aires, deepening a rift between the two governments that began in July when President Javier Milei criticized Brazilian officials during a visit to São Paulo.

Sources: AP (via The Hill), Buenos Aires Times

Europe

Poland's top court plunges into crisis as a rival judge declares herself chief and police move in

A Constitutional Tribunal judge aligned with Prime Minister Donald Tusk's governing majority, Anna Korwin-Piotrowska, announced Friday evening that she was suspending tribunal president Bogdan Święczkowski and his deputy for a month and taking over the court's leadership herself, prompting police to be deployed to the Warsaw courthouse. The tribunal's own account called the move a judicial "coup d'état," and President Karol Nawrocki cut short a trip to Senegal and arrived at the court overnight as supporters of each side gathered outside; the dispute traces back to a standoff over whether four judges elected by the Tusk-aligned parliamentary majority may take their seats.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Euronews

Russian drone strike on a packed Dnipro train platform kills at least 14

A Russian drone struck a platform at Dnipro's main railway station on Thursday while a commuter train and an intercity service stood side by side, killing at least 14 people, including several railway workers, and wounding more than a dozen, Ukrainian officials said. Ukraine's air force said it intercepted three jet-powered drones in the same wave but a fourth evaded its defenses and hit the station; President Volodymyr Zelensky called the attack "horrific" and urged a response from Ukraine's allies, as Washington separately approved a potential sale of 500 Patriot missiles to Ukraine.

Sources: Reuters (via ABC News Australia), AP (via Euronews)

Middle East

Tanker disabled by limpet mines in the Strait of Hormuz as Iran claims wider attacks

A small boat attached explosive devices to the hull of a Panama-flagged tanker transiting the Strait of Hormuz on Thursday, disabling the vessel northeast of Oman's Khasab, Britain's maritime security agency said — the first confirmed limpet-mine attack on a ship there since the US-Iran war began in February. Iran's Revolutionary Guard separately claimed three more tankers caught fire after straying onto an "unauthorized" route near Omani waters, a claim Reuters could not independently verify, while Qatari mediators returned to Tehran to press a new ceasefire draft, with Iran's response expected by the weekend.

Sources: Reuters (via Jerusalem Post), AP (via Euronews)

Israel's Herzog opens coalition talks as election deadlock continues

Israeli President Isaac Herzog began formal consultations with party leaders this week after neither Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc, with 53 seats, nor rival Gadi Eisenkot's, with 48, reached the 61-seat majority needed to govern, leaving Arab-led parties holding the remaining 19. Herzog urged the parties to come to the talks "with an open mind" as he decides which leader gets the first attempt at forming a government; both Netanyahu and Eisenkot have ruled out partnering with Arab-led parties, and Eisenkot has repeatedly said he will not serve under Netanyahu, leaving a unity government as one of few remaining options.

Sources: AP (via ABC News Australia), NPR

Africa

Sudan accuses Ethiopia of hosting drone attacks that killed over 400 civilians

Sudan's government renewed accusations Thursday that 162 drone attacks launched from bases in Ethiopia's Assosa and Bahir Dar regions have killed more than 400 civilians and 102 soldiers, targeting power stations and fuel depots, including one strike that knocked out the Markhiyat station northwest of Omdurman. A foreign ministry official said Sudan "reserves the right to defend its territory" and accused Ethiopia and the United Arab Emirates of enabling the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces, though the claims have not been independently verified; Ethiopia has repeatedly denied supporting the RSF or hosting drone launches, calling the allegations unfounded.

Sources: AP (via Arabian Post), allAfrica

Nearly 50,000 displaced as Ethiopia-Kenya border fighting enters a second week

Fighting around the Ethiopian border town of Moyale, stemming from a territorial dispute between the Oromia and Somali regional states, has pushed close to 50,000 people into northern Kenya since Oct. 21, up from roughly 36,000 just days earlier, Kenyan authorities and the UN refugee agency said. Sporadic gunfire could still be heard from the Ethiopian side on Friday as civilians kept crossing and trucks remained stranded at the border, with most new arrivals — mainly women and children — sheltering in schools, churches and with host families while the UN appeals for $8.9 million to cover food, water and shelter over the next three months.

Sources: UNHCR, Al Jazeera

Asia

UN Command confirms North Korean mine violated the armistice as Kim Yo-jong warns of "merciless" retaliation

The UN Command said a joint investigation with South Korean forces confirmed an active North Korean mine was found on the southern side of the Demilitarized Zone, ruling that the blast that wounded South Korean soldiers in September was an armistice violation. Kim Yo-jong, the North Korean leader's sister, denied involvement, accused Seoul of fabricating the finding and warned of "merciless" retaliation, while North Korea separately fired more than ten short-range ballistic missiles toward the Sea of Japan on Oct. 29; Seoul has proposed military talks to confirm the border's exact location, proposals Pyongyang has not answered.

Sources: Yonhap (via The Korea Herald), Al Jazeera

UN investigator says Myanmar's junta is attacking civilians to terrorize them as arms purchases fuel the war

A United Nations investigator examining the civil war in Myanmar said the military government is deliberately attacking civilians to instill fear and force submission, and that graft and corruption schemes continue to fund the arms purchases sustaining the conflict. The assessment comes as the International Court of Justice is expected to rule by the end of the year on whether Myanmar's military committed or failed to prevent genocide against the Rohingya minority during its 2017 crackdown, a decision that could carry fresh legal consequences for the junta nearly five years into the civil war that followed its 2021 coup.

Sources: AP (via U.S. News), AP (via Yahoo News)
