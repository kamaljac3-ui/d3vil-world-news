Americas

Hurricane Trudy lashes Mexico's Pacific coast with flooding rains

Hurricane Trudy struck Mexico's Pacific coast with heavy rain and strong winds on Sunday, and the National Hurricane Center warned of potentially life-threatening flooding and mudslides, the Associated Press reported. A hurricane warning covered the coastline from Manzanillo to Cabo Corrientes, with forecasters predicting 4 to 8 inches of rain and isolated totals near 15 inches, while NBC News reported the storm had briefly strengthened to Category 2 before weakening as it closed in on the coast. Mexico's navy urged residents to stay alert to high waves and landslide risk.

Sources: AP (via WFMZ), NBC News

Europe

Cost-of-living protests across France turn violent, hundreds arrested

Hundreds of thousands of people marched across France on Saturday over the rising cost of living and fuel prices, a movement that began with student anger over underfunded schools and was joined by the "Yellow Vests," the CGT union, teachers and farmers, the Associated Press reported. Clashes, fires and looting broke out in several cities as the day wore on, and Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said roughly 400 people were arrested nationwide; the government put turnout at about 550,000, including 110,000 in Paris, while organizers claimed 800,000. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu warned the movement was turning into "a wave of urban violence."

Sources: AP (via ABC News), Al Jazeera

Czech opposition deals populist PM Babiš a blow in Senate election

Pro-Western opposition parties made large gains in Czech Senate elections held Saturday, keeping control of the chamber's upper house and weakening Prime Minister Andrej Babiš's governing coalition, the Associated Press reported. The populist billionaire's ANO movement failed to win a single one of the 27 seats up for contest out of the 81-seat Senate, a result read as a check on Babiš after his party's return to power in parliamentary elections earlier this year. The Senate cannot remove the government but can delay and amend legislation, giving the opposition a renewed foothold.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), AP (via Washington Post)

Middle East

Egypt pledges "all necessary support" to Saudi Arabia as Houthi war escalates

Egypt's National Defense Council pledged to give Saudi Arabia "all necessary and possible forms of support" against Houthi attacks, and President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi asked parliament to convene in an emergency session Sunday to authorize defensive action if Riyadh requests it, Al Jazeera and The National reported. The request follows a wave of Houthi missile and drone strikes on Saudi airports this month that officials called the deadliest attack on the kingdom in over a decade, and came after Saudi Arabia said it intercepted another round of incoming drones over Riyadh and the Eastern Province. Yemen's Houthi-run parliament warned Cairo against joining the fight, with its speaker telling his Egyptian counterpart the move could threaten regional security.

Sources: Al Jazeera, The National

Israeli settlers attack European diplomats during West Bank olive harvest

Israeli settlers attacked a group accompanying Palestinian farmers during the olive harvest near the village of Taybeh, east of Ramallah, on Saturday, striking a group that included Belgium's consul general to the Palestinians, French and Polish diplomats and a French mayor, Al Jazeera reported. French mayor Bernard Baudoux told AFP the assault showed "there is a tolerance on the part of the Israeli government" toward settler violence, and France's foreign minister called for Israel to arrest the "extremist settlers" involved. Israel's military said the visit had not been coordinated with it and no soldiers were present, but confirmed a "severe act of violence" had occurred and said police opened an investigation.

Sources: Al Jazeera, AFP (via ABC Australia)

Africa

Sudanese army airstrike on Darfur livestock market kills 30 as RSF holds thousands of detainees

Sudan's army intensified airstrikes on RSF positions in Nyala, the South Darfur capital, hitting a livestock market and killing at least 30 civilians on Friday, a military source told AFP; the paramilitary RSF blamed the army for the market deaths while denying responsibility for its own losses in the wider strikes. Separately, the Sudan Doctors Network said the RSF is holding more than 6,500 military personnel and civilians, including about 500 children, in El Geneina, West Darfur, and warned of worsening food shortages and a rising death rate among those held. Sudan's military chief, Abdel Fattah al-Burhan, has vowed this month to retake "every inch" of RSF-held territory.

Sources: Al Jazeera (AFP), Radio Tamazuj

Rival jihadist factions wage "unprecedented" turf war across the Sahel

Fighters from al-Qaeda's JNIM and a rival Islamic State affiliate have clashed on an unprecedented scale across the borderlands of Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger, with a UN experts' report calling it the most intense direct confrontation globally between an al-Qaeda branch and an Islamic State-linked group, AFP reported. The Islamic State affiliate claims it killed more than 400 JNIM fighters in an October 9 battle in Burkina Faso's Oudalan province, a toll analysts say has not been independently verified and that JNIM has not addressed; researchers say the fighting has since spread into western Niger and northwestern Nigeria. The casualty figures claimed by both sides remain unconfirmed.

Sources: AFP (via Africanews), AFP (via Asharq Al-Awsat)

Asia

Taiwan drives off Chinese vessels in "unusually large" coast guard operation

Taiwan's coast guard said it pushed back 16 Chinese coast guard, research and fishing vessels in a coordinated operation around Taiwan's main island and the Pratas Islands on Sunday, calling the deployment "unusually large," Malay Mail reported. CNA separately reported that two China Coast Guard vessels entered restricted waters around Pratas the same day, a standoff still unresolved when Taiwan's coast guard issued its statement; the Dongsha-Nansha branch said it has carried out 11 such operations this year. The incidents follow an October 1 confrontation in which Taiwan's coast guard used a water cannon against a Chinese fishing boat that had refused to leave the restricted zone.

Sources: CNA (via Taipei Times), Malay Mail

Toxic haze from Indonesia's wildfires triggers highest-level alerts across Southeast Asia

Months-long wildfires in Indonesia have spread toxic haze across the region, pushing air pollution in western Singapore to "very high," the island's top category, and prompting East Timor to shut schools nationwide for three days, AFP reported. By mid-October Singapore ranked second only to Baghdad on IQAir's global pollution index, with Kuala Lumpur third, and several Malaysian states had ordered school closures; the haze triggered the region's highest-level alert again over the weekend, with authorities warning conditions could worsen. Singapore said it would formally warn seven Indonesian companies it has identified as responsible for the fires, and the haze is expected to be raised at ASEAN meetings ahead of a November summit in Manila.

Sources: AFP (via Malay Mail), The Star
