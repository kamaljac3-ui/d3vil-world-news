Americas

Hurricane Simon and Tropical Storm Rachel's remnants leave at least six dead in Mexico

The death toll from Hurricane Simon and the remnants of Tropical Storm Rachel rose to six on Mexico's Pacific coast this week, officials said, as flooding and landslides struck Baja California and Guerrero. The victims included a 13-year-old girl, a 4-year-old boy and two women, while five more people remained missing in Baja California, among them a father and son who had gone fishing near San Felipe. Simon weakened to a Category 1 storm but continued moving slowly up Mexico's west-central coast, prolonging the flood risk.

Sources: AP (via ClickOnDetroit), Al Jazeera

Brazil's runoff remains a dead heat as Flávio Bolsonaro's brother is ordered jailed

Brazil's October 25 presidential runoff between President Lula da Silva and Senator Flávio Bolsonaro remains within the margin of error, with a Datafolha poll cited by Reuters putting Bolsonaro at 49% to Lula's 45%. Days before that poll, a Supreme Court justice ordered the arrest of Bolsonaro's brother, former congressman Eduardo Bolsonaro, directing him to begin serving a four-year sentence for coercion in judicial proceedings after his conviction became final; he has lived in the United States since 2025 and extradition is considered unlikely. Eduardo Bolsonaro called the order "arbitrary, ill-timed and ineffectual."

Sources: U.S. News (Reuters), AP (via WRAL)

Europe

Drone attack sinks second cargo ship off Bulgaria in a week, leaving 11 missing

Drones struck two merchant ships in Bulgaria's Black Sea waters overnight, sinking one cargo vessel and badly damaging the Ukrainian-owned Chelsea 3, whose 15-member crew was rescued, officials said. Eleven crew members from the sunk vessel remain missing, in the second such sinking in Bulgarian waters within a week; Prime Minister Rumen Radev stopped short of directly blaming Russia but said the outcome "benefits the Russian Federation," and Sofia summoned Russia's ambassador. Russia's envoy said she had no information from her country's defense ministry.

Sources: The Moscow Times (Reuters), Al Jazeera

Russian missiles hit Kyiv power plant days after Trump announced an energy "ceasefire"

Russian ballistic missiles struck Kyiv's Darnytsia combined heat and power plant and a nearby residential building Tuesday evening, killing at least two people and wounding about 20 others, officials said. President Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukraine received no advance warning from Western allies and that the strikes violated an "energy ceasefire" U.S. President Donald Trump had announced days earlier; Russia also hit a heating plant in Kharkiv, and more than 600,000 people in and around Kyiv lost power. Casualty figures have varied by outlet and may still rise.

Sources: AFP (via Modern Ghana), Euronews

Albanian student protests over education and jobs turn violent in Tirana

Thousands of Albanian students marched nationwide over university conditions and job prospects, and the Tirana demonstration turned violent when protesters threw bricks and concrete blocks at the Education Ministry, prompting police to respond with tear gas and water cannon, Reuters reported. Albania's Health Ministry said at least 13 people were treated for injuries and police said as many as 23 protesters were detained; some marchers also called for Prime Minister Edi Rama's resignation. The State Police chief suspended four officers after footage showed a 19-year-old with a broken leg being carried away by officers.

Sources: The Star Malaysia (Reuters), Al Jazeera

Middle East

Houthi missile hits Saudi Aramco's Dhahran headquarters as Yemen's war escalates

Yemen's Houthi movement struck Saudi Aramco's historic headquarters complex in Dhahran with a missile, Saudi officials said, days after a Houthi attack on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport killed at least 13 people and wounded more than 300 in the kingdom's deadliest strike in over a decade. Saudi Arabia's energy ministry said the Aramco strike caused a limited fire with no reported casualties, while fighting between Houthi forces and the Saudi-backed Yemeni government has intensified around the city of Taiz. Oil prices rose on the news, with one analyst telling Reuters the conflict is "driving prices higher."

Sources: Al-Ahram English (AFP), Al Jazeera

Iran says cargo ship sank after hitting naval mine in Strait of Hormuz

Iran's Revolutionary Guard navy said a cargo ship sank Wednesday after striking a naval mine in the Strait of Hormuz, state television reported — the first such sinking Iran has acknowledged during its ongoing standoff with the United States over the waterway. The Guard gave few details, including on the fate of the crew, and said the vessel had been traveling what it called an "illegal and dangerous route." The claim has not been independently verified, and no U.S. or shipping-industry confirmation was immediately available.

Sources: AP (via WFMZ), AP (via PANow)

Africa

Congo's Kabila names M23 commanders as deputies in new anti-government coalition

Former Democratic Republic of Congo president Joseph Kabila has named M23 military commander Sultani Makenga and former electoral-commission chief Corneille Nangaa as deputies in his new Front for the Refoundation of the Nation, a political-military coalition aimed at ousting President Félix Tshisekedi. A spokesperson said the group would pursue its goal "by any means necessary, including the use of force," and analysts say Kinshasa is likely to harden its stance toward Kabila and seek to isolate the new alliance regionally, complicating Doha-mediated peace talks with M23. Congo's government had not formally responded to Monday's announcement as of Wednesday; Kabila was convicted of treason and sentenced to death in absentia last year.

Sources: Diggers News (AP), The Washington Post

Egyptian truck drivers shot dead in Sudan in revenge for disputed border mine airstrike

A gunman killed two Egyptian truck drivers and wounded a third south of Khartoum on Monday, Egypt's foreign ministry said; Sudanese police said the suspect confessed to avenging his brother, who died days earlier in an airstrike on an illegal gold-mining site near the Egypt-Sudan border. Death-toll estimates for that airstrike vary widely, from 13 to more than 200 by different survivor and rights-group accounts, and survivors have accused Egypt's air force, though neither government has said who carried it out. Sudan's army chief ordered a fact-finding inquiry into the miners' deaths, while Egypt's foreign minister pressed for the drivers' bodies to be repatriated.

Sources: Khaleej Times (AFP), Sudan Tribune

Asia

Pakistan detains PTI leaders, including Imran Khan's sisters, under new provincial emergency

Pakistani authorities detained hundreds of activists from jailed former prime minister Imran Khan's party, including three of his sisters, after President Asif Ali Zardari imposed emergency rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and suspended its PTI-led provincial government, Reuters reported. The federal government cited the province's failure to counter Islamist militancy, a justification PTI rejects; the party's supporters had gathered to march on Islamabad demanding Khan's release, and the capital has used shipping containers to block access routes. PTI spokesperson Zulfikar Bukhari called the move "more of a coup disguised as emergency."

Sources: U.S. News (Reuters), Al Jazeera

Taiwan holds its own live-fire drills with US-made tanks as Chinese exercises continue

Taiwan began live-fire exercises using US-made M1A2T Abrams tanks this week, responding to a run of Chinese live-fire drills along the mainland coast that Taiwan's defense ministry has tracked closely since Beijing's navy began maneuvers following President Lai Ching-te's National Day speech. Taiwan's defense minister, Wellington Koo, said the Chinese exercises appear to be coastal artillery drills rather than a blockade rehearsal, and no no-sail zone has been declared around Taiwan itself. The scale and planned duration of Taiwan's own exercises were not immediately clear.

Sources: U.S. News (Reuters), SBS News

India's opposition takes "vote theft" dispute to Supreme Court after mass Delhi protests

Congress leader Rahul Gandhi and ten other opposition INDIA bloc leaders petitioned India's Supreme Court to halt a contested revision of electoral rolls and investigate alleged irregularities, following nationwide protests in Delhi the party called "Chalo Delhi — March for Democracy." The petition also asks the court to examine whether Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar acted without his fellow commissioners' approval; the Election Commission has rejected the allegations and asked Gandhi to file a sworn declaration. Delhi police registered at least two cases connected to the march, according to officials cited by Indian media.

Sources: The Tribune (PTI), Bloomberg
