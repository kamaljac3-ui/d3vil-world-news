Americas

Wildfire smoke shuts down schools and offices in Brazil's largest Amazon city

Thick wildfire smoke forced Manaus, the capital of Brazil's Amazonas state, to cancel in-person school and suspend municipal offices on Friday after pollutant levels reached hazardous levels, local officials said. Satellite data from Brazil's national space research institute recorded more than 3,700 fire hotspots across the state in September alone, nearly four times the prior year's count, as temperatures in the city reached 40°C (104°F). Brazil's environment ministry deployed firefighting crews and aircraft to Amazonas, though no date has been set for schools to reopen.

Sources: ABC News (AP), The Washington Post

Europe

Death toll from Zaporizhzhia apartment strike climbs to 20, including three children

The death toll from Saturday's Russian strike on a residential building in Zaporizhzhia rose to at least 20 people, including three children, as rescue crews finished clearing rubble from the partially collapsed structure, Ukrainian emergency officials said. Guided aerial bombs hit the five-story block and damaged roughly two dozen nearby buildings, with 18 others reported injured. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy linked the strike to a US deal easing sanctions on Russian diesel exports announced a day earlier, calling that deal "weak."

Sources: ABC News Australia (AP), Ukrainska Pravda

Poland hit by third school attack in eight days, prompting nationwide security checks

A woman wounded three people at a private post-secondary school in Warsaw on Saturday, the third attack on a Polish educational institution in just over a week, following a stabbing spree that injured nine people in Ośtrołęka and a caretaker killed at a primary school northeast of Warsaw. Authorities inspected nearly 50,000 schools nationwide and detained 76 people as Prime Minister Donald Tusk called for stronger rules against online incitement to violence. Opposition leader Jarosław Kaczyński alleged a link to Russian hybrid warfare, a claim authorities have not confirmed.

Sources: Al Jazeera, The Washington Post

Middle East

Trump lifts 41-year US ban on direct flights to Lebanon

President Trump revoked four decades of presidential orders barring direct air links between the United States and Lebanon, clearing a legal path for flights severed after the 1985 hijacking of TWA Flight 847, officials announced this weekend. Lebanese President Joseph Aoun welcomed the move, though officials cautioned that regulatory and security arrangements must still be finalized before any carrier can schedule a route, and the US State Department's "do not travel" warning for Beirut remains in place. Trump first signaled the change during Aoun's visit to Washington in July.

Sources: Arab News (AP), US News (AP)

UN envoy warns Yemen has slid back into "full-scale war"

UN special envoy Hans Grundberg told the Security Council on Friday that Yemen has returned to "full-scale war," with Saudi-backed government forces and Iran-aligned Houthi fighters clashing on multiple fronts near the Red Sea coast. He said more than 200,000 people have been displaced and at least 46 civilians killed since August, as Houthi missile and drone strikes on Saudi airports in Abha and Riyadh killed three people and wounded dozens of others this month. Grundberg urged a return to political talks, saying battlefield gains cannot substitute for a negotiated settlement.

Sources: Al Jazeera, The National

Africa

Ethiopia and Eritrea sever diplomatic ties as fighting spreads along their border

Ethiopia and Eritrea cut diplomatic relations and expelled each other's diplomats this week, after Ethiopia's government told the UN Security Council that Eritrean forces had advanced up to 60 kilometers into its territory in support of Tigrayan rebels. UN Secretary-General António Guterres said he was "gravely concerned" by the renewed fighting near Adigrat and Idaga Hamus and called for an immediate end to hostilities, as Ethiopian drone strikes hit Eritrean positions and Eritrean troops later withdrew from the border town. The neighbors restored relations in 2018 after a 1998-2000 border war that killed tens of thousands; Eritrea has denied Ethiopia's accusations and called the fighting an "unprovoked war of aggression."

Sources: France 24 (AFP), NBC News

UN Security Council renews Darfur arms embargo, rejects US push to widen it nationwide

The UN Security Council voted unanimously on Thursday to extend an arms embargo on Sudan's Darfur region for two months, after the United States failed to win support from Russia and China for expanding the ban to cover all of Sudan. The council also extended the mandate of the expert panel monitoring sanctions compliance to January 2027, as war between Sudan's army and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces continues into its fourth year. Sudan's government argued that a nationwide embargo would undermine its right to self-defense.

Sources: Al Jazeera, The National

Asia

India locks down central Delhi, detains thousands to block protest against election chief

Indian police deployed thousands of officers, suspended mobile internet in parts of central Delhi and closed dozens of metro stations on Saturday to stop a youth-led protest demanding the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar over a voter-list revision that critics say favors Prime Minister Narendra Modi's party. A police source told Reuters that about 200 people were detained near the protest site, while a Home Ministry official later said more than 2,000 people, including the protest movement's founder, had been detained across the city. The Election Commission and Modi's party have denied the bias allegations; the demonstrations come months ahead of state elections beginning in early 2027.

Sources: Reuters (via Internazionale), South China Morning Post
