Americas

Paraguay's ousted president under house arrest as new leader takes office

Paraguayan lawmakers swore in former Vice President Pedro Alliana as president on November 6, a day after impeaching and removing Santiago Peña over the collapse of Ueno Bank, which froze access to savings for roughly half the country's adults, Reuters and Al Jazeera reported. Peña was placed under house arrest on suspicion of breach of trust, and a judge is now weighing prosecutors' request that he be held in pretrial detention; he denies wrongdoing. Authorities have separately detained several senior Ueno executives, including the bank's founder and the former central bank president who oversaw it.

Sources: Reuters (via U.S. News), Al Jazeera

Brazil investigates circumstances of Flávio Bolsonaro's departure to the US

Brazilian authorities are examining how Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, left the country for the United States after losing Brazil's presidential runoff, in defiance of a Supreme Court order that he surrender his passport, CBS News and Courthouse News reported. He and his family have since been granted US visas and he has made public appearances in Florida, including at a Mar-a-Lago gala, but federal police have not confirmed exactly how or when he crossed the border. Bolsonaro disputes characterizations that he "fled," saying he left Brazil "for the sake of Brazil."

Sources: CBS News, Courthouse News

Europe

Gunman kills at least six in attack on Kraków's main square

A gunman opened fire at multiple points in Kraków's Main Market Square on November 7, killing at least six people and wounding more than a dozen before police shot and wounded him, the AP and CBC reported. Officers later found three more bodies, including the suspect's parents and sister, at his home, and Poland's government called the attack an "act of terror." One of the dead was a British national, and one wounded police officer was listed in critical condition.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), CBC

Russia launches 36-missile barrage at Kyiv ahead of peace talks

Russia fired 36 ballistic missiles at Kyiv and the surrounding region overnight into November 7, striking a bridge over the Dnipro River and energy facilities that supply power to the capital, Reuters and Al Jazeera reported. Ukrainian officials said at least one person was killed and several wounded; President Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukraine intercepted only four of the missiles and accused Moscow of trying to pressure Kyiv ahead of three-way talks with Russian and US officials planned in Abu Dhabi. Russia's defense ministry said it had targeted military-industrial, energy and transport sites.

Sources: Reuters (via GV Wire), Al Jazeera

Middle East

Israel's president bypasses Netanyahu, taps rival Eisenkot to form government

President Isaac Herzog gave opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot, a former military chief, the first opportunity to form Israel's next government on November 8, a setback for Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu after the October 27 election left neither bloc a majority, the AP and Al Jazeera reported. Eisenkot received 67 recommendations from lawmakers, including Arab-led parties, against 46 for Netanyahu, and now has up to six weeks to build a coalition. Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister in the meantime and has called instead for a unity government.

Sources: AP (via WROC), Al Jazeera

Saudi-led coalition intercepts Houthi missiles fired toward Riyadh

The Saudi-led coalition said its air defenses intercepted ballistic and cruise missiles fired toward Riyadh and Saudi Arabia's Eastern Province on November 8, part of what a coalition spokesman described as a run of aerial attacks since the start of the month, Arab News and The National reported. A Houthi military spokesman said the group had carried out six separate operations against targets in Saudi Arabia and Yemen; Saudi officials said a building in Riyadh's financial district sustained minor damage. Houthi-reported casualty figures from separate Saudi airstrikes near Sanaa this week have not been independently confirmed.

Sources: Arab News, The National

Africa

Sudan and Ethiopia expel each other's diplomats amid drone-strike dispute

Ethiopia ordered six staff at Sudan's consulate in Asosa to leave within 48 hours and shut the mission, and Sudan retaliated by expelling diplomats from Ethiopia's consulate in Gedaref, officials told Reuters and AFP. The rupture follows Sudanese accusations that Ethiopia has allowed Sudan's paramilitary Rapid Support Forces to launch drone attacks on Sudanese power and fuel infrastructure from Ethiopian territory, which Addis Ababa denies. Sudan has lodged formal complaints with the UN Security Council and the African Union's Peace and Security Council.

Sources: Reuters/AFP (via The Washington Post), Al Jazeera

Congo's Ebola outbreak death toll passes 5,300, the worst on record

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo, caused by the Bundibugyo strain, has killed more than 5,300 people out of over 10,400 confirmed cases, according to the national health ministry's figures reported by Al Jazeera, with the case-fatality rate now above 50%. The World Health Organization has called it the largest Ebola outbreak ever recorded in the country and has kept it designated a Public Health Emergency of International Concern, though it has not recommended travel or trade restrictions. The outbreak has also spread into Uganda and Kenya.

Sources: World Health Organization, Al Jazeera

Tanzania opposition leader convicted of incitement, cleared of treason

A Tanzanian court convicted opposition leader Tundu Lissu on November 6 of inciting the public to disrupt the country's October 2025 general election and sentenced him to four years in prison, while acquitting him of treason, a charge that had carried a possible death sentence, the AP and the Washington Times reported. Lissu, held since his arrest in April 2025, plans to appeal, and rights groups including Amnesty International called the conviction politically motivated. The ruling came days after the death of Lissu's elder brother, whose funeral Lissu had sought permission to attend.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), Washington Times

Asia

New Zealand's National Party suffers historic losses as coalition clings to narrow lead

Final preliminary counts from Saturday's New Zealand general election show Prime Minister Christopher Luxon's National Party collapsing to about 29% of the vote, down from 38% in 2023, with senior figures including Finance Minister Nicola Willis and parliament's speaker Gerry Brownlee losing their seats, the AP reported. Luxon's National-ACT-New Zealand First bloc appears positioned for a bare majority of three seats, but Luxon himself called the race "too close to call," and final results are not expected for weeks. Neither Luxon nor the opposition Labour-led bloc has declared victory.

Sources: AP (via KSAT), RNZ
