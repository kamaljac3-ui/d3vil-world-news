Americas

Mexico files formal protest over US anti-cartel tip line

Mexico's government lodged a formal diplomatic complaint with Washington over a new DEA tip line soliciting anonymous reports on drug traffickers and corrupt officials in Sinaloa state, with President Claudia Sheinbaum calling the program interference in Mexico's internal affairs. DEA Administrator Terry Cole said the line targets members of designated terrorist organizations and money launderers, and that a similar line is planned for Jalisco state. Sheinbaum said the two countries' relationship is "one of coordination, not subordination."

Sources: AP (via ABC News), Washington Post

Lula names Bradesco chairman finance minister as markets rally

Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva named Luiz Carlos Trabuco, the 75-year-old chairman of Bradesco's board, to take over as finance minister starting in January, in what Reuters called one of the most consequential appointments of his term. Brazil's currency jumped as much as 4.7% against the dollar and the Bovespa stock index rose nearly 4% on the news, as investors welcomed a veteran banker into the post after months of market unease since Lula's October election win.

Sources: Reuters (via Investing.com), Reuters (via U.S. News)

Ecuador's El Niño floods kill 34, displace tens of thousands

Ecuador's government said flooding and landslides linked to this year's El Niño weather pattern have killed 34 people and affected more than 47,000 others, according to the National Secretariat for Risk Management's latest update. The worst-hit areas include Guayas province, where rivers overran Guayaquil and Milagro, and Los Ríos province, where a landslide in Quevedo killed two teenage sisters.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Rio Times

Europe

Ukraine strikes Russian oil hub and air base; Putin vows retaliation

Ukraine's military said drones struck a major Transneft oil-pumping station in Russia's Bashkortostan region and damaged aircraft at a Russian air base overnight, part of a weeks-long campaign that Kyiv says has cut Russia's refining capacity by roughly half. President Vladimir Putin called Russian retaliation for the strikes "inevitable," while Ukraine's foreign minister, Andrii Sybiha, told him to "stop complaining" and end the war instead. The extent of damage from the latest strikes has not been independently confirmed.

Sources: AP (via PBS NewsHour), Kyiv Post

Middle East

Israeli strike on Gaza clinic kills two Doctors Without Borders staff as ceasefire strain deepens

Two Doctors Without Borders employees were killed on November 27 when an Israeli strike hit one of the charity's clinics near Al-Shifa Hospital in Gaza City, MSF said, bringing to 17 the number of its staff killed in Gaza since October 2023. Israel's military said the two men, who worked as clinic security guards, were Hamas members; MSF disputed that account and called the strike a "direct hit" on a facility whose location had been shared with Israeli forces. The strike followed a round of strikes a day earlier that killed at least nine Palestinians, including a Hamas-appointed deputy minister, part of a campaign Israel says is targeting ceasefire violations, Reuters reported.

Sources: Reuters (via Al-Monitor), RTÉ

Saudi-Houthi fighting escalates with strikes on Riyadh, Yemeni cities

Saudi-led coalition air defenses intercepted a Houthi cruise missile aimed at the Riyadh region on Saturday, as the coalition reported destroying dozens of targets across Houthi-held Sanaa, Hodeidah and Saada. The exchange followed a Saudi strike a day earlier that Houthi-affiliated media said killed 11 civilians in Yemen's Taiz province; Houthi-run health officials say at least 19 civilians have been killed and 25 wounded by Saudi strikes over the past two days, figures that could not be independently verified and that the coalition did not immediately address.

Sources: Al Jazeera/AFP, AFP (via Yahoo News)

Africa

Gunmen kill at least 17 villagers in Nigeria's Plateau state

Armed men stormed the village of Tafigana in Plateau state's Bassa area late on November 25, opening fire on residents before fleeing as police approached; police said at least 17 people were killed and four wounded, while a local community association put the toll at 16, Reuters reported. Nigerian authorities urged calm and warned against reprisal attacks, as the state continues to see a string of similar assaults, including one that killed at least 25 people in August.

Sources: Reuters, allAfrica

DR Congo's Ebola outbreak passes 12,000 cases as fighting hampers response

The Democratic Republic of Congo's health ministry reported 12,021 confirmed Ebola cases and 5,904 deaths as of November 26, making it, per Reuters, the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak on record, with the Bundibugyo strain now confirmed in eight of the country's 26 provinces. The World Health Organization said fighting between Congolese forces and CRP militia fighters near Mongbwalu in Ituri province forced it to temporarily relocate its Ebola response team, and on November 27 it issued updated recommendations calling for improved security and accelerated research.

Sources: Al Jazeera/Reuters, allAfrica

Asia

China sends record warplanes near Taiwan as island votes in local elections

Taiwan's defense ministry said it tracked 30 Chinese military aircraft around the island on November 27, with 26 crossing the median line of the Taiwan Strait in what Taipei called a coordinated patrol involving Chinese naval vessels, a day before Taiwan's November 28 local elections. Taiwanese officials have warned of Beijing's interference in the vote; China's defense ministry did not immediately respond to requests for comment, Reuters reported.

Sources: Reuters (via U.S. News), Taipei Times

North Korea halts missile shipments to Russia amid rift over Seoul policy

Ukraine's military intelligence agency said North Korea has temporarily suspended deliveries of KN-23 ballistic missiles and artillery shells to Russia, along with transit rights for Russian military transport aircraft, after North Korea's foreign minister demanded Moscow clarify its approach toward South Korea. Pyongyang is also reportedly withholding approval of a Russian request to raise its troop deployment in Russia from roughly 8,500 to 50,000. Reuters said it could not independently confirm the claims, and neither Russia's defense ministry nor North Korean officials had responded to requests for comment.

Sources: Reuters, Japan Times
