Americas

Peru and Venezuela coordinate return flights for undocumented migrants

Peruvian President Keiko Fujimori and Venezuela's acting president, Delcy Rodríguez, spoke by phone Thursday to coordinate voluntary return flights for Venezuelans living in Peru without legal status, the first call between the two leaders since their countries restored relations in July after a two-year rupture. Fujimori said the two foreign ministries are drafting protocols for the flights, though no date or passenger count has been set; Rodríguez's office did not address separate expulsion plans Fujimori has pursued for Venezuelans with criminal records. Peru hosts roughly 1.6 million Venezuelans, the second-largest such population after Colombia's.

Sources: AP (via Local10), Rio Times

Brazil's presidential runoff tightens to within the margin of error

A Datafolha poll released Thursday showed Flávio Bolsonaro's lead over President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva narrowing to 51%-49% of valid votes ahead of the October 25 runoff, down from 52%-48% a week earlier, putting the race inside the survey's two-point margin of error. The Brazilian real lost 1.4% on Thursday, the sharpest move among the region's major currencies, as investors weighed the tightening contest. Prediction markets were still pricing Bolsonaro as the favorite, at roughly 80%, as of Friday.

Sources: Reuters (via Israel National News), Rio Times

Europe

Russia fires more than 850 drones at Ukraine in a single overnight barrage

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said Russia launched upward of 850 drones between Wednesday night and Thursday morning, striking 15 regions in what he called a deliberate escalation. Ukraine's emergency services reported three deaths and six injuries in the Kirovohrad region after a drone attack lasting more than ten hours, while Russia's defense ministry said it downed 166 Ukrainian drones over the same period. The barrage came as Kyiv resumed strikes on Russian oil refineries after a brief, informal halt to energy-infrastructure attacks collapsed.

Sources: NPR (AP), Al Jazeera

UK threatens a full visa ban on Eritreans over stalled deportations

Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood said Britain will impose a blanket visa ban on Eritrean nationals unless Asmara resumes accepting its deported citizens, after the Home Office said Eritrea abruptly halted cooperation on returns earlier this year. Eritrean diplomats in London are already barred from travelling more than 60 kilometers beyond the capital, and a fast-track visa service for prominent visitors has been suspended. Eritrea's embassy in London dismissed the threat as a "political gimmick," noting Britain has not issued a visa to an Eritrean official since 2023.

Sources: PA Media (via Yahoo), Arab News

Middle East

Houthi missiles strike Yemen's presidential palace complex in Aden

Yemen's Houthi movement fired missiles at the al-Maashiq presidential palace in Aden on Thursday, the first such attack on the internationally recognized government's own headquarters; officials said the prime minister and his cabinet were unharmed. Yemen's health minister put the toll at five killed and 16 wounded, while government military sources gave a lower count of four dead, and two officials separately told Reuters that two security guards were killed. A Houthi military spokesman said the group had targeted what it called "the Saudi enemy's military operations headquarters."

Sources: Al Jazeera, CBC News

Two commercial ships sink near the Strait of Hormuz within 24 hours

A Singapore-flagged container ship and a dry bulk carrier both sank near the Strait of Hormuz within a day of each other this week after being struck by projectiles, Reuters reported, as attacks on shipping through the waterway have surged over the past ten days. All crew aboard the container ship, the GFS Pride, were evacuated without injury after a fire broke out following the strike; Iran's Revolutionary Guard said a vessel had struck a naval mine, a claim a maritime security firm said it could not independently confirm. Crude exports through Hormuz have fallen nearly 40% below pre-war levels amid the rising attacks, according to shipping-tracking data cited by Reuters.

Sources: Reuters (via Investing.com), U.S. News

Africa

DRC's Ebola outbreak becomes the deadliest in the country's history

The Democratic Republic of Congo's health ministry reported more than 9,000 confirmed Ebola cases and over 4,300 deaths this week, making the current outbreak the worst in the country's history by case count, the AP reported. Officials said the true toll is likely higher, since a large share of deaths are recorded only after patients have died outside treatment centers, and the outbreak — caused by the rare Bundibugyo strain, for which there is no approved vaccine — now spans 65 of the country's 167 health zones. The World Health Organization has declared it a public health emergency of international concern after Kenya confirmed cases linked to travel from the DRC.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), Al Jazeera

Ethiopian forces reach the Eritrean border, raising fears of a wider war

Ethiopian government troops advanced to Zalambessa, a border town previously held by Tigrayan rebel forces, Reuters reported, days after Ethiopia and Eritrea severed diplomatic relations amid mutual accusations over the resumed Tigray conflict. AFP reported Thursday that Eritrean soldiers who had been stationed in the town withdrew, though accounts differed on whether any Ethiopian forces had crossed into Eritrean territory; Eritrea's information minister dismissed reports of an Ethiopian incursion as unreliable. Ethiopia has told the UN Security Council that Eritrean troops advanced up to 60 kilometers into Tigray before pulling back, a claim Eritrea disputes.

Sources: Reuters (via Martin Plaut), AFP (via Daily Beirut)

Asia

Indonesia's wildfire haze pushes Singapore's pollution to a 2026 high

Smoke from forest and peatland fires burning across Sumatra and Borneo pushed air quality in western Singapore into its "very high" pollution band this week, the worst reading of the year, while haze forced school closures in several Malaysian states and across Timor-Leste, Bernama reported. Indonesian President Prabowo Subianto approved accepting firefighting assistance from neighboring countries, and Singapore's prime minister said authorities are investigating which companies may be responsible for the fires, warning legal action is possible. Indonesia's forestry ministry has kept 61 aircraft on standby against the blazes, which have proven difficult to extinguish in peat terrain.

Sources: Bernama, The Jakarta Post

Marcos to summon China's ambassador over 'veiled threat' of force

Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. said he will summon Chinese Ambassador Jing Quan after Jing said Beijing "may easily win" a conflict if it used force, remarks the Philippines' foreign affairs department called a "thinly veiled threat," Reuters reported. Jing had added that China "will not use force" and "loves peace," but Marcos said the comment "sounds very much like a threat" and was not diplomatic language; China's embassy accused Manila of selectively quoting and overinterpreting the ambassador's words. It would be the second time in just over ten months that Marcos has summoned Jing, after a July incident in which a Filipino soldier was injured in a confrontation with the China Coast Guard.

Sources: Reuters (via Daily Maverick), Manila Times
