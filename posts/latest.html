Americas

UN reports nearly 4,600 killed in Haiti's gang violence this year

The UN human rights office said Tuesday that at least 4,595 people were killed and 2,050 injured in Haiti between January and October, as gangs adapt to a foreign-backed force deployed to push them back, spokesperson Marta Hurtado told reporters in Geneva. The office said gangs are also trying to influence Haiti's unscheduled upcoming elections; separately, Reuters reported that gang-driven displacement could leave more than 250,000 Haitian children acutely malnourished by next year.

Sources: UN News, Caribbean National Weekly

US defense secretary to deliver military aircraft to Colombia amid anti-cartel push

US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth will travel to Bogotá on Wednesday to hand over Colombia's first C-130 Hercules transport aircraft, with Colombian Defense Minister Jorge Eduardo Mora telling Congress two more are expected, Reuters reported. The visit deepens military cooperation under the US-led "Shield of the Americas" initiative against drug cartels, which Colombia joined in August; regional media reported Hegseth may also visit Venezuela afterward, though Reuters said it could not confirm those plans.

Sources: The Star Malaysia (Reuters), Free Malaysia Today (Reuters)

Europe

Ukraine collapses a span of Russia's Kerch Bridge for the second night running

Ukrainian drones, missiles and long-range strikes destroyed the central road span of the Kerch Bridge linking Russia to occupied Crimea overnight Monday into Tuesday, the second attack on the bridge in as many nights, Reuters reported. President Volodymyr Zelensky called the strike "unprecedented," and Crimea's Russian-installed governor, Sergei Aksyonov, said two people were killed in a separate drone strike near Kerch; rail traffic across the bridge resumed by Tuesday afternoon, though road traffic remained halted, according to the Associated Press.

Sources: CNBC (Reuters), PBS NewsHour (AP)

Dutch coalition survives collapse threat after days of crisis talks

Prime Minister Rob Jetten said Tuesday his three-party minority coalition will keep governing after four days of crisis talks triggered by a wealth-tax deal that junior partner VVD said breached the coalition agreement, Reuters reported. The parties agreed to stricter rules for striking deals with opposition lawmakers, particularly on financial matters, after VVD leader Dilan Yeşilgöz had called the government "a failed experiment"; a collapse would have forced the Netherlands' third general election since 2023.

Sources: U.S. News (Reuters), DutchNews.nl

Middle East

Houthi forces advance within 60km of Aden as Yemeni government pulls back

Yemeni government forces withdrew from Dhubab airport and the Bab el-Mandeb area on Sunday to avoid encirclement and concentrate on defending Aden, five military sources told Reuters, after Houthi fighters reached Khor Omairh roughly 100 kilometers from the city; AFP separately reported Houthi forces as close as 60 kilometers northwest of Aden after seizing the al-Maqatirah and al-Madaribah districts. A government employee in Aden told Reuters that residents fear the retreat could bring the fighting closer to the city, while territorial and casualty claims from both sides remain unconfirmed.

Sources: Al-Monitor (Reuters, AFP), The Arab Weekly

Britain sanctions eight Israeli firms over West Bank settlement construction

Britain imposed asset freezes Tuesday on eight Israeli construction and property firms, including Har Hevron Development Company and Hemdat Shomron, for building settlements in the occupied West Bank, acting alone this time rather than with international partners, Reuters reported. The measures, timed ahead of final tenders for the E1 settlement project that would split the West Bank, also sanctioned the Tzav 9 group over blocking Gaza aid convoys; Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband said Britain would "clamp down" on firms profiting from settlement expansion, while Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar called the move a "hostile act" and vowed retaliation, AFP reported.

Sources: Al-Monitor (Reuters, AFP), Al Jazeera

Africa

Africa CDC says Congo's Ebola outbreak remains "out of control" six months on

Africa CDC Director-General Jean Kaseya told Reuters the Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo is "still serious, and still not under control" six months after it was declared, warning it could become the worst on record if not stopped. The outbreak, driven by the Bundibugyo strain, has infected more than 11,000 people and killed over 5,400 as of mid-November, according to the US CDC and Europe's ECDC, with Africa CDC citing attacks on health workers, unpaid staff and funding shortfalls as hampering the response despite $2.9 billion in pledges.

Sources: Al Jazeera (Reuters), CNBC Africa

UN says drones caused nearly three-quarters of Sudan's war deaths this year

The UN human rights office said Tuesday that at least 2,230 civilians were killed in Sudan's civil war between January and October, with more than 1,630 of those deaths caused by drone strikes, Reuters reported. High Commissioner for Human Rights Volker Türk condemned a "brazen disregard for the laws of war" by combatants, who the office documented striking markets, fuel depots and healthcare facilities; Kordofan accounted for more than two-thirds of the documented deaths, followed by Darfur, and the UN said the real toll is likely higher.

Sources: U.S. News (Reuters), Arab News Pakistan (Reuters)

Asia

Xi breaks protocol to greet Trump in Shenzhen ahead of three-way summit with Putin

Chinese President Xi Jinping personally welcomed President Trump on the tarmac as he landed in Shenzhen Tuesday for the APEC leaders' summit, an unusual gesture that broke with normal diplomatic protocol, Al Jazeera and ABC News reported. Trump, who arrived after a stop in the Philippines for the ASEAN summit, is due to hold a first-ever three-way meeting with Xi and Russian President Vladimir Putin on Thursday expected to touch on nuclear arms reduction, trade and Iran, though officials on all sides have played down expectations of a concrete deal.

Sources: Al Jazeera, ABC News

Indonesia's Prabowo faces deadline to name a new vice president

Indonesian President Prabowo Subianto has yet to submit candidates to replace Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka, who resigned November 12 after courts found his academic credentials did not meet constitutional requirements, Coordinating Minister Yusril Ihza Mahendra told Antara News. Gerindra party figures Sufmi Dasco Ahmad and Sugiono have emerged as the leading contenders ahead of Prabowo's deadline to submit two names by November 25, with the People's Consultative Assembly expected to elect a successor in early December.

Sources: Antara News, The Star Malaysia
