Americas

Carney rallies for Canadian unity days before Alberta's independence referendum

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney traveled to Calgary on Thursday for a "Forever Canadian" rally, days before Albertans vote October 19 on whether to pursue a path toward separation from Canada. Carney has argued Canada is "worth fighting for," while a 1998 Supreme Court ruling means even a "yes" vote would only open negotiations rather than trigger immediate independence; critics have questioned how effectively Ottawa's unity campaign is reaching skeptical Albertan voters.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), The Washington Post

Tens of thousands march against Colombia's De la Espriella in nationwide protests

Opposition parties, unions, Indigenous groups and students marched across Colombia on Wednesday against President Abelardo de la Espriella's austerity cuts, with former President Gustavo Petro joining the Bogotá demonstration. De la Espriella's government said roughly 62,000 people took part and called the day largely peaceful, while rights groups reported riot police using tear gas and water cannon in some cities; organizers say the marches are building toward a larger "national strike" planned for November.

Sources: Al Jazeera (AFP, AP), ColombiaOne

Europe

Ukraine accuses U.S. of withholding missile-attack warning as toll from Russian strikes rises

Ukrainian officials said American counterparts told them the system used to detect Russian missile launches had failed due to bad weather, an explanation Kyiv does not believe, CBS News reported, deepening a rift over the arrangement meant to warn Ukraine of incoming attacks. The dispute follows a nationwide Russian barrage that has been tied to at least nine deaths and left more than half a million people without power, with President Volodymyr Zelensky saying the attack involved more than 39 ballistic missiles and 120 drones and loitering munitions. The U.S. has not publicly confirmed or disputed Kyiv's account.

Sources: CBS News, Al Jazeera (AP, Reuters, AFP)

Albanian protesters torch government building and banks as Tirana unrest escalates

Tens of thousands of students returned to Tirana's streets Wednesday, a day after briefly entering city hall, and this time set fire to a government building and two bank branches while throwing stones, Reuters reported; police again responded with tear gas and water cannon. The protests, demanding Prime Minister Edi Rama's resignation over education costs and corruption, have grown nightly since the weekend, and the European Union's delegation in Tirana said it was concerned about the escalation into violence. No deaths have been reported, though Albania's health ministry said Tuesday that 13 people had been treated for injuries.

Sources: Reuters (via Yahoo), France 24 (AFP)

Middle East

Saudi Arabia says it intercepted a cruise missile over Medina as Houthi war widens

The Saudi-led coalition said Thursday it intercepted and destroyed a cruise missile over the Medina region, Islam's second-holiest city, and separately downed a drone over the oil-rich Eastern Province; a coalition spokesman did not say who launched the missile or what it targeted. Yemen's Houthi movement denied responsibility, dismissing the Saudi claim as fabricated, while Riyadh's main airport briefly suspended service because of related attacks elsewhere in the kingdom. The claim could not be independently verified.

Sources: AP (via Local10), Seoul Economic Daily

Israel's Knesset election set for October 27 as early voting begins

Israel will vote for all 120 Knesset seats on October 27 — the first time since 1988 the election has been held on its originally scheduled date rather than early — with early voting for diplomats and other eligible voters abroad starting Thursday. Polls cited by the Times of Israel show Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc deadlocked with an opposition slate led by Gadi Eisenkot, Naftali Bennett and Yair Lapid, in a vote widely seen as a referendum on Netanyahu's handling of the wars in Gaza, Lebanon and Iran.

Sources: Al Jazeera (Reuters), The Times of Israel

Africa

DRC's Ebola outbreak passes 4,000 deaths as Kenya confirms a second case

The World Health Organization's latest figures put the Democratic Republic of Congo's Ebola outbreak — the country's worst on record — at more than 9,000 cases and over 4,000 deaths, with officials warning that roughly a third of new cases are only detected after the patient has died. Kenya confirmed a second case Wednesday, a 39-year-old man who is a close relative of the first patient and was already in quarantine when he fell ill; health officials said there is no evidence of spread in the wider community, and 108 of the first patient's contacts remain under quarantine.

Sources: The Washington Post (AP), Africanews

Mauritius imposes curfew after deadly unrest over water shortages, police shooting

Mauritius extended an overnight curfew across ten districts through Friday after protests over water shortages and the rising cost of living turned violent, AFP reported, with demonstrations escalating after police shot and wounded a woman at a roadblock. Authorities have made 94 arrests since the unrest began, and Prime Minister Navin Ramgoolam said some detainees could face prosecution under the Prevention of Terrorism Act; the officer involved in the shooting has himself been arrested. Some reports said order had returned by Wednesday, though the government's own statement extended the curfew through Friday.

Sources: Arab News (AFP), Africanews

Asia

At least 27 killed as a school roof collapses in Peshawar, Pakistan

The roof of a private school collapsed in Peshawar on Thursday, killing at least 27 students and a teacher, according to a hospital source cited by AFP, with the toll expected to rise as rescue crews finished clearing the rubble; more than 30 others were injured, several critically. Police have detained the school's owner on suspicion of negligence and illegal construction. Casualty figures varied by outlet through the day, with some local reports putting the toll as high as 34.

Sources: The National (AFP), Dawn

Japan protests Russia's arrest of a journalist as China visits disputed Kuril Islands

Japan lodged formal protests with both Russia and China on Tuesday after Russia's FSB arrested Hokkaido Shimbun reporter Yuki Imai on Sakhalin Island on espionage charges, and a Chinese delegation from Heilongjiang province visited the Russian-held, Japanese-claimed island of Iturup the same day. A Moscow court ordered Imai held for two months pending investigation, and Japan's chief cabinet secretary, Minoru Kihara, said Tokyo had been unable to reach him or confirm his condition; Tokyo called the Chinese delegation's visit to the island "totally unacceptable."

Sources: AP (via ABC News), Al Jazeera
