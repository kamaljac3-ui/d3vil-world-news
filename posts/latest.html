Americas

Brazil's Supreme Court convenes to decide whether to investigate one of its own justices

Ten of Brazil's Supreme Federal Tribunal's 11 justices met in an extraordinary session Tuesday to vote on whether to accept a Federal Police report implicating Justice Alexandre de Moraes over leaked messages tied to jailed banker Daniel Vorcaro, with Moraes himself barred from voting and filing a 44-page denial hours before the session opened. If the court accepts the report, Moraes becomes a formal suspect in an inquiry, though he would remain a sitting justice throughout — the latest flashpoint in a court crisis marked by canceled sessions and justices overruling each other, unfolding weeks ahead of Brazil's October presidential election. A vote result was not yet available at publication time.

Sources: ABC News (AP), The Rio Times

Europe

Denmark summons Russia's ambassador after a frigate fires flares at a military helicopter over the Baltic

A Danish air force Fennec helicopter photographing a Russian frigate in international waters off Gedser on Monday had two flares fired toward it, with one passing at close range. Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen called it "reckless action" from Russia and said it fit a pattern of Moscow's intensifying hybrid campaign against the EU and NATO; Foreign Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen summoned Russia's ambassador over the incident. It came the same day NATO jets shot down a drone over Lithuania, the latest in a run of incidents along the alliance's eastern flank.

Sources: Internazionale (Reuters), The Copenhagen Post

Suspected sabotage snarls the Dutch rail network on the day of the government's annual budget speech

Train services across the Netherlands were disrupted Tuesday morning after ProRail said security systems were tampered with in 24 locations; workers found pipes attached to the rails that trigger false section faults, and a train collided with one affected section at Steenwijk with no injuries reported. The disruption fell on Prinsjesdag, the day the government presents its budget to parliament in a ceremony that draws crowds to The Hague — a timing Dutch media called suspicious, though no authority has confirmed a motive. Intelligence agency AIVD said it was working with police to identify who was responsible.

Sources: ABC News (AP), Bloomberg

Middle East

Iran's security chief rejects US talks despite Trump signaling openness to a deal

Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, wrote on social media not to "get distracted by the U.S. president's mixed signals — from 'no negotiations' to 'we're ready to talk,'" adding "no talks until Iran's conditions are met, period," after Trump said Monday he was open to resuming talks on the Middle East war. Iran has set war reparations and the lifting of sanctions as preconditions, even as President Masoud Pezeshkian has separately said Tehran is willing to return to the table, including on its nuclear program, once Washington lifts sanctions and drops military pressure.

Sources: Business Recorder (AFP), Asharq Al-Awsat (AFP)

Africa

UN agencies warn Sudan's aid system could collapse within weeks without emergency funding

The International Organization for Migration said emergency shelter, sanitation, and household relief supplies for hundreds of thousands of Sudan's displaced could run out by the end of September without urgent donor funding, with IOM chief Amy Pope warning "the entire humanitarian system could collapse within weeks." The World Food Programme's acting Sudan head told Reuters its funding has fallen by roughly half this year, from $645 million to $206 million, leaving a nearly $300 million gap as war between Sudan's army and the Rapid Support Forces keeps driving displacement — 8.6 million people remain displaced inside Sudan alone.

Sources: TimesLIVE (Reuters), Al Jazeera

Asia

Search intensifies for 129 still missing after a ferry carrying 243 people capsizes in the Java Sea

The ferry Virgo Transport 8 lost contact early Sunday while sailing from Surabaya to Banjarmasin in rough weather, tilting and capsizing with 243 people and dozens of vehicles aboard. Six bodies have been recovered and 108 people rescued as of Monday, Indonesia's search-and-rescue agency said, with 655 personnel, 17 ships and aircraft, and navy underwater drones now searching for the roughly 129 still missing.

Sources: ABC News (AP), NPR
