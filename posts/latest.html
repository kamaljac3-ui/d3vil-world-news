Americas

Truck bomb near Colombian military base kills four, including an infant

A dump truck loaded with explosives detonated Sunday night near an army base in Planadas, in Colombia's Tolima department, killing four people — including a two-month-old baby — and damaging a hospital and nearby homes, regional officials said. Tolima's governor gave a preliminary toll of four dead, with local reports of more than a dozen wounded; authorities blamed a dissident faction of the former FARC guerrilla group, though no one had claimed responsibility as of Monday. The army's Fifth Division called the attack a "terrorist act" as President Abelardo de la Espriella pushes for intensified operations against armed groups in the area.

Sources: AFP (via Free Malaysia Today), AFP (via Tribune.net.ph)

Americans vote in midterm elections framed as referendum on Trump's second term

Polls opened across the United States Tuesday in midterm elections that will decide control of Congress, with 35 Senate seats, the full House and several governorships on the ballot. Forecasters give Democrats the edge in both chambers, and candidates from Michigan to Alaska made closing appeals over the weekend in contests widely framed as a test of President Trump's record; Trump has said he will not order new strikes on Iran before the vote. Results were not expected until polls close later Tuesday.

Sources: Reuters (via Yahoo), The Canadian Press (via Medicine Hat News)

Europe

Russian strike on Ukrainian aid center kills at least 26, dozens still missing

Russian aircraft dropped guided bombs on a school used as a humanitarian aid distribution point and shelter in Komyshuvakha, in Ukraine's Zaporizhzhia region, on Monday, killing at least 26 people including rescue workers and police officers, Ukrainian officials said. A second strike hit the site after the first, and officials said dozens more remained missing under the rubble, with the toll expected to rise. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called it "a vile terrorist strike," saying Russian forces knew residents gathered there weekly to collect aid; Russia did not immediately comment.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Korea Times

Belgium's prime minister offers resignation as budget talks collapse

Prime Minister Bart De Wever offered his resignation to King Philippe on Monday after overnight coalition talks over Belgium's 2027 budget broke down, pushing the government toward collapse. The coalition had been split for months over roughly €10 billion in proposed spending cuts and tax changes, with N-VA and MR pushing healthcare cuts while Vooruit and Les Engagés demanded higher taxes on capital gains. The king had not yet decided whether to accept the resignation and was due to begin talks with other coalition leaders Tuesday; without a deal, Belgium faces automatic spending caps De Wever has ruled out.

Sources: Reuters (via Internazionale), Brussels Signal

Middle East

Houthi forces advance to within 80km of Aden as Yemen's government reshuffles cabinet

Yemen's Houthi movement has advanced to within roughly 80 kilometers of Aden, the seat of the internationally recognized government, four military sources told Reuters — the closest the group has come to the city since the war's early months. The push threatens the main road linking Aden to the besieged city of Taiz, and Yemen's government responded by naming three new ministers in a cabinet reshuffle as fighting intensified. Aden's airport, repeatedly targeted in recent attacks, reopened after runway repairs, though two carriers have limited or suspended flights there into next week.

Sources: Reuters (via Investing.com), The National

US-Iran talks in Doha end without a breakthrough as contacts continue

Indirect talks between the United States and Iran mediated by Qatar concluded in Doha on Sunday without a deal to end their war, though Iran's foreign ministry said contact through Qatari mediators was continuing. Iran says it will not discuss its nuclear program until Washington lifts restrictions on its oil sales and port access, while the US has pressed for the nuclear issue to be addressed directly; Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi called the talks "very intense." President Trump has said no new US strikes would come before Tuesday's midterm elections, though Iranian officials reportedly see renewed strikes as more likely than a deal.

Sources: Reuters (via Business Recorder), Inkl

Africa

Congo's Ebola outbreak passes 5,300 deaths and spreads to four countries

The Ebola outbreak centered in the Democratic Republic of Congo has killed more than 5,300 people and infected over 10,400, according to Africa CDC figures reported by AFP, making it the fastest-growing outbreak of the disease on record. Nearly all deaths remain in the DRC, but confirmed cases have now reached Uganda, Kenya, Nigeria and France, prompting Uganda to order 21-day quarantines for travelers returning from the DRC. No vaccine is approved against this strain, Bundibugyo, though a trial began in the DRC in late October; health workers at one treatment center have stopped work over unpaid wages.

Sources: AFP (via Yahoo), Al Jazeera

US to cut South Africa and Rwanda from African trade program

The United States will suspend South Africa and Rwanda from duty-free trade benefits under the African Growth and Opportunity Act effective January 1, 2027, the US Trade Representative's office said Monday, citing practices inconsistent with the program's eligibility rules. USTR Jamieson Greer cited trade and investment barriers, corruption and threats to US national security and foreign policy interests; South Africa exported about $1.7 billion in goods to the US under AGOA last year. South Africa's trade department said it had expected a negative outcome and remained open to dialogue with Washington, while unions there warned of job losses in agriculture and manufacturing.

Sources: Reuters (via Al Jazeera), Daily Maverick

Asia

China's Communist Party confirms record expulsion of 33 officials, including former military No. 2

China's Communist Party formally confirmed the expulsion of 33 officials following this week's Fifth Plenum, including former Politburo member and ex-Central Military Commission vice chairman Zhang Youxia, Reuters reported, in the highest-profile purge of the post-Mao era. Only 279 of the 376 originally named Central Committee members attended the closed-door session, the lowest turnout in decades. The plenum also elevated General Zhang Shengmin to the Politburo and adopted a new party document tightening scrutiny of officials' assets, family finances and online activity.

Sources: Reuters (via The Standard), Free Malaysia Today

North Korea tests cluster warhead on a cruise missile for the first time

North Korea's Missile General Bureau test-fired a strategic cruise missile fitted with a cluster-munition warhead on Monday, state media KCNA said, claiming the weapon could destroy an area of five to six hectares. It follows an October 31 test of a similar cluster warhead on a tactical ballistic missile, part of what Pyongyang says is a push to field multiple cluster-warhead variants by year's end. South Korea's military did not confirm the cruise missile launch, and independent analysts noted KCNA provided no evidence to support the claimed destructive power.

Sources: AFP (via France 24), Korea Times
