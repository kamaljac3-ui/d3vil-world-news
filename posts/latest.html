Americas

Chile's president makes the first state visit to Bolivia in 71 years

Chilean President José Antonio Kast traveled to La Paz on Thursday to meet Bolivian President Rodrigo Paz, the first state visit by a Chilean leader to Bolivia in seven decades, and the two signed accords on trade, mining, energy and border security as their governments work toward normalizing relations severed since 1978. Bolivia's long-standing demand for a sovereign corridor to the Pacific, lost at the International Court of Justice in 2018, was left off the agenda, a point former president Evo Morales criticized; no embassies have yet reopened. Both leaders are recently elected conservatives whose countries have joined a US-backed anti-drug-trafficking coalition.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), MercoPress

Brazil's presidential runoff slips into a statistical dead heat

Two fresh polls released Friday, from CNT/MDA and Futura/100% Cidades, put Flávio Bolsonaro and President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva within a point and a half of each other ahead of the October 25 runoff, both gaps inside the surveys' margin of error and tighter than the leads Bolsonaro held just days earlier. Reuters had already reported the contest as a technical tie in its own Quaest poll published Wednesday. Prediction markets continue to price Bolsonaro as a heavy favorite, a notable divergence from what the traditional polling now shows.

Sources: Reuters (via Investing.com), Rio Times

Europe

France braces for a 'Yellow Vest' revival as cost-of-living protests sweep the country

Protesters staged more than 80 marches and hundreds of smaller actions across France on Saturday over rising living costs, with student groups that have organized since mid-September joined by a resurgent Yellow Vest movement and backed by the CGT, one of the country's largest unions. AFP reported French authorities detained dozens of people as the demonstrations began, and police closed Metro stations near government buildings and barred some gatherings in central Paris amid heightened security. The protests are being watched as an early gauge of public mood ahead of next spring's presidential election.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), Bloomberg

Italy and Spain agree to lift border checks imposed after the Ceuta migrant crisis

Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni and Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez agreed on the sidelines of Thursday's EU summit in Brussels to jointly end the border checks their countries imposed on each other after a record migrant crossing into Spain's North African enclave of Ceuta in August. Italy's checks lapse October 23 and Spain's a day earlier, timed to end together, and the two countries' interior ministers are expected to negotiate alternative security cooperation next week. The dispute began after Italy accused Spain of letting migrants who reached Ceuta move on into the EU's passport-free Schengen area.

Sources: AFP (via The Local), Euronews

Middle East

Trump signals the US may strike Iran's underground 'Pickaxe Mountain' nuclear site

President Trump said in a Thursday night interview that the United States "may have to" strike Pickaxe Mountain, a fortified underground facility tied to Iran's nuclear program, while ruling out nuclear weapons and signaling he does not foresee sending American ground troops. "Now we're looking at Pickaxe Mountain. We may have to go there," Trump said, following reports that US military planners have weighed using the GBU-43/B "mother of all bombs" against Iranian nuclear sites; he did not say whether any strike had been authorized. Iran's army said separately that its air defenses are now stronger than before the war began, a claim that could not be independently verified.

Sources: Reuters (via Business Recorder), Arab News

Houthi missiles strike Aden airport for the second time in a week

Two missiles struck Yemen's Aden airport on Friday, hitting an area used by a military unit aligned with the Saudi-backed government, a security official and an air traffic controller said; no casualties were reported, and a Yemenia Airways flight preparing for takeoff was delayed. Yemen's Aden Security Department blamed the Iran-aligned Houthis, who had not immediately claimed the attack, and police called it the second strike on the airport in under a week. The same day, a Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted a ballistic missile headed for Yanbu, a major Saudi oil-export hub.

Sources: AP (via CP24), Al Jazeera

Africa

Ethiopian forces take the historic city of Aksum as Eritrea denies heavy losses

Ethiopian federal forces and allied Tigray Peace Force fighters entered the historic city of Aksum and the town of Adigrat this week after Tigrayan rebel forces withdrew to surrounding mountains, following heavy drone strikes, The New Humanitarian reported. The pro-government Tigray Peace Force claimed Eritrean troops, whose presence inside Ethiopian territory AFP had confirmed earlier this month, suffered significant casualties and were pulling back toward the border, a claim Eritrea's information minister rejected; the losses could not be independently verified. Ethiopia and Eritrea severed diplomatic relations earlier this month amid the dispute over the fighting.

Sources: The New Humanitarian, AFP (via Al Jazeera)

DRC's Ebola outbreak spreads to an eighth province as toll nears 4,500 dead

Health officials confirmed two Ebola cases in North Ubangi province, making it the eighth province affected by the outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo, Reuters reported. The DRC's health ministry has now recorded roughly 9,277 cumulative confirmed cases and 4,478 deaths, keeping the outbreak on pace as the second-largest Ebola epidemic on record behind only the 2014-2016 West Africa epidemic, with Ituri province still the hardest-hit, accounting for about 73% of cases. The World Health Organization says a first vaccine trial is expected in the DRC by the end of October; no approved vaccine currently exists for this strain, Bundibugyo virus.

Sources: Reuters (via Africanews), CIDRAP

Asia

Delhi protest leader detained and reportedly arrested as India's voter-roll unrest continues

Police detained Abhijeet Dipke, founder of the "Cockroach" movement protesting India's electoral roll revisions, on Friday as he left a house in central Delhi, and Reuters reported Saturday that he was formally arrested to prevent a breach of the peace and was due to appear before a magistrate. The near-daily demonstrations demand the resignation of India's chief election commissioner over a revision that removed more than 130 million names from voter rolls, which the Election Commission says targeted duplicate and ineligible entries; a home ministry official told Reuters more than 2,000 people were detained in an earlier wave of the protests. Police had closed a central New Delhi park in an attempt to stop the near-daily rallies.

Sources: Reuters (via ABC Australia), Al Jazeera

Taiwan accuses China of election interference after sanctions on lawmaker's family firm

China's Taiwan Affairs Office barred VWAY Co., a company owned by the father of Taipei mayoral candidate Puma Shen, from doing business with Chinese entities, saying the firm kept trading in China despite an earlier sanction on a related family company. Taiwan's Mainland Affairs Council, citing CNA, called the move a "malicious attempt" to interfere in Taiwan's upcoming elections, while Shen said the underlying allegations were recycled claims from 2024 he has already addressed. The sanction is the second China has imposed on a company linked to Shen's family in little over a year.

Sources: CNA (Focus Taiwan), Taipei Times
