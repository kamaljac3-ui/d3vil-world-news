Americas

Haiti's anti-gang offensive enters a second day as forces hold key Port-au-Prince corridor

Haitian police backed by Salvadoran troops and private security contractors kept control Saturday of Route 2, the corridor linking Port-au-Prince to Haiti's south that gangs had held for roughly five years, a day after AFP reported police killed at least 34 suspected gang members in the Martissant neighborhood. Local outlets including RioTimes Online and Infobae reported the two-day toll climbing past 50, though that figure has not been confirmed by a wire service; police said the operation, which also involved the private security firm Vectus Global, was the first major push beyond gang-held territory in the area in five years.

Sources: AP (via WCAX), Infobae

Flávio Bolsonaro says he has no proof of fraud in Brazil's election, but blames gag order

Flávio Bolsonaro, who lost Brazil's October 25 presidential runoff to Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva by about 4.4 million votes, told a US television interview published Sunday that he has no evidence of fraud because he is "forbidden to investigate," rather than withdrawing his earlier claims the vote was "rigged." His father, former President Jair Bolsonaro, has not recognized the result and left for Miami; Brazil's electoral court is separately investigating alleged foreign interference in the race and fined Meta for failing to fully comply with judicial orders.

Sources: CBS News, Axios

Europe

COP31 climate talks collapse in Antalya without a deal, first such breakdown since 2009

UN climate talks in Antalya, Turkey ended Sunday without an agreement after running two days into overtime, with the European Union, the UK and climate-vulnerable nations rejecting a final draft that dropped any reference to moving away from fossil fuels, multiple outlets reported citing negotiators. Saudi Arabia and Russia had blocked language on transitioning away from oil, gas and coal, while Norway and Mexico pushed for it; Australia's climate negotiator Chris Bowen called the outcome "very disappointing," and it marks the first time since 2009 that a UN climate conference has closed with no deal.

Sources: NPR, Al Jazeera

Russian missile strikes kill six in Kyiv after repeated Ukrainian hits on Crimean bridge

Russian missiles struck Kyiv before dawn on November 20, killing six people including three water-utility workers and wounding at least 15, Ukrainian officials said, in strikes AP linked to Moscow's response to three Ukrainian attacks on the Kerch Bridge to Crimea in a single week. The barrage damaged Kyiv's Podilskyi Bridge and cut power to several districts, Mayor Vitali Klitschko said; Ukraine's air force said it downed 161 of 186 Russian drones launched in a single overnight wave days later, as both sides continued trading long-range strikes.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), Kyiv Post

Middle East

Israeli tanks pushed into central Gaza in first ground operation beyond the Yellow Line since ceasefire

Israeli forces sent tanks into central Gaza's Nuseirat area on November 18 to destroy what the military called a Hamas tunnel threatening its troops, Reuters reported, the first publicly announced Israeli ground incursion beyond the ceasefire's "Yellow Line" boundary since the October 2025 truce took hold. Hamas called the raid, which displaced thousands of Palestinians sheltering in the area, a "flagrant" ceasefire violation; the Israeli military said the operation concluded the next day and that Gaza's main north-south road had reopened, while the US-backed body overseeing the truce said both sides have fallen short of their commitments.

Sources: Reuters (via ThePrint), Reuters (via CTV News)

Houthis claim missile strike on Saudi Arabia's Rabigh oil refinery, drone intercepted over Riyadh

Yemen's Houthi movement said Sunday it scored a direct hit on Saudi Arabia's Rabigh oil refinery, a 400,000-barrel-per-day complex on the Red Sea coast, Reuters reported, while the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted and destroyed a separate drone aimed at the kingdom's western region. Saudi authorities have not confirmed any damage to the refinery, which the Houthis have claimed to have struck at least three times since late October without independent confirmation; the claims come amid the wider war that resumed in July alongside the US-Israeli conflict with Iran.

Sources: Reuters (via Archyde), Jerusalem Post

Africa

Drone strikes hit homes and a UN building in Sudan's capital as drone war becomes top killer of civilians

Drones struck residential areas of Khartoum before dawn on November 22, destroying at least one home and damaging a UN building, AFP reported; the UN's humanitarian office said the risk to its staff was "unacceptable" and that no casualties had yet been reported from that strike. A Sudanese army source blamed Ethiopia, which denied the accusation; the UN said last week that drone strikes, blamed mainly on the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces, have become the leading cause of civilian deaths in Sudan's civil war in 2026, killing more than 1,600 people between January and October.

Sources: AFP (via Japan Times), PBS NewsHour (AP)

Asia

Bangladesh vows to arrest Hasina on arrival as extradition pressure on India grows

A Bangladeshi junior foreign minister said Sunday that ousted Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina would be arrested the moment she tried to cross the border, as Dhaka pressed India to extradite her under their bilateral treaty after a tribunal sentenced her to death in absentia last November, PTI reported. Hasina, who has been in India since fleeing a student-led uprising in August 2024, has applied for fresh travel documents ahead of a planned December return that Bangladesh's interim government has rejected; India has said only that the matter is being examined and that courts will ultimately decide.

Sources: PTI (via ThePrint), Dawn

Myanmar rejects ICJ's landmark Rohingya genocide ruling; France seeks UN Security Council meeting

Myanmar's military-led government called "unjust" and "not fair" a November 19 International Court of Justice ruling that it committed genocide against the Rohingya minority in 2017, AFP reported, the first time in the court's 80-year history it has found a state responsible for genocide. France's foreign ministry said it would seek a UN Security Council meeting after Myanmar's rejection, noting ICJ rulings are final and binding even though the court has no means of enforcing them; the judges, in a 9-6 vote, ordered Myanmar to pay reparations to be determined later, while China voiced objections to the ruling.

Sources: Irrawaddy (AFP), Human Rights Watch
