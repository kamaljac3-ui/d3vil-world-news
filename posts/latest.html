Americas

Lula accuses Meta of suppressing his campaign as Bolsonaro's son holds a tight runoff lead

Brazil's top electoral court gave Meta 48 hours to explain why posts and videos from President Lula's re-election campaign saw reduced reach on Instagram and Facebook, after his team accused the company of election interference ahead of the October 25 runoff. Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, son of the former president, has led Lula in several post-first-round surveys, including an October 6-7 Datafolha poll that put him at 49% to Lula's 45%, within the poll's margin of error. Both campaigns are intensifying their social-media operations in the race's final two weeks.

Sources: France 24 (AFP), U.S. News

Europe

UK says Iran's Revolutionary Guard "almost certainly" behind foiled RAF Fairford plot

Prime Minister Andy Burnham told the House of Commons that a suspected plot against RAF Fairford was "almost certainly" directed by Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, hardening an earlier claim of only "strong indications" of Iranian involvement. The UK sanctioned two Iranian nationals it said were tied to plots against Britain and summoned Iran's ambassador; five men arrested near the base in late September were released on bail without charge. Iran's government rejected the accusation, with its ambassador saying Tehran has had no involvement in the matter.

Sources: UPI (Reuters), U.S. News

Drone attack sinks a ship off Bulgaria's Black Sea coast, second such strike in a week

Drones struck two merchant ships in Bulgaria's Black Sea exclusive economic zone on Tuesday, sinking one cargo vessel and damaging the Ukrainian-owned Chelsea 3, whose 15-member crew was rescued, Prime Minister Rumen Radev said. It was the second attack on shipping in Bulgarian waters in a week, after an October 6 strike sank the cargo vessel Alfa Watan; Radev said responsibility was unclear but that the outcome "benefits" Russia, and that he would raise the attacks with NATO. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen condemned the strikes, and Bulgaria said it would summon Russia's ambassador.

Sources: U.S. News (Reuters), Al Jazeera

Middle East

Riyadh's main airport to stay shut through October 17 as Saudis probe defense failures

Saudi aviation authorities extended the closure of Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport through October 17, as investigators examine why the kingdom's air defenses failed to stop last weekend's Houthi missile and drone barrage that killed at least 14 people and wounded more than 300. Officials say preliminary findings show the Houthis launched a wave of drones before following with cruise and ballistic missiles, and are also examining why no public warning was issued before impact. The airport, Saudi Arabia's main gateway, had already been struck twice in the preceding week.

Sources: ABC News Australia, PBS NewsHour (AP)

Africa

Congo's ex-president Kabila forms coalition with M23 rebels, raising risk of wider conflict

Former Democratic Republic of Congo president Joseph Kabila launched a political-military coalition in Goma on Monday that includes commanders from the Rwanda-backed M23 rebel group, which has held the city since early 2025. The new Front for the Refoundation of the Nation named M23 military commander Sultani Makenga and former electoral-commission chief Corneille Nangaa among its vice presidents; an adviser said the group aims to end "tyranny, by any means necessary including the use of force." Analysts cited by AFP said the move raises the odds of direct confrontation between Kabila, who was convicted in absentia of treason last year, and President Félix Tshisekedi's government.

Sources: Al Jazeera, AP (via WRAL)

UN chief "gravely concerned" as Ethiopia and Eritrea trade accusations of invasion over Tigray

UN Secretary-General António Guterres said he was "gravely concerned" by renewed fighting in northern Ethiopia and urged an immediate end to hostilities, as Ethiopia and Eritrea accused each other of launching a war. Ethiopian and allied forces recaptured Tigray's capital, Mekelle, after Tigrayan rebel forces that had seized the city in late September withdrew, and Ethiopia's government told the UN Security Council it was defending itself against an Eritrean "all-out invasion" of the region, a claim Eritrea denies. Ethiopia severed diplomatic relations with Eritrea and expelled its diplomats this month, and Guterres called on both sides to return to the 2022 Pretoria peace agreement.

Sources: UN News, Al Jazeera

Asia

China launches 9-day live-fire drills spanning its coast as Taiwan tracks closely

China's maritime authorities began live-fire exercises on Monday that run through October 20 across the Bohai Sea, Yellow Sea, East China Sea and South China Sea, drills Taiwan's defense ministry said it was monitoring closely, including one zone off Zhejiang province near Taiwan. Beijing's foreign ministry defended the exercises as routine activity in its own waters, while Taipei urged China to end what it called hostile rhetoric; the drills followed President Lai Ching-te's National Day address, which Beijing had condemned. No no-sail zone was designated around Taiwan itself.

Sources: The Japan Times, Hong Kong Free Press

Pakistan's PTI moves to challenge Khyber Pakhtunkhwa emergency rule in court

Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf said it would petition the Peshawar High Court to overturn the federal government's imposition of emergency and governor's rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, two days after President Asif Ali Zardari approved the measure and the province's PTI chief minister was detained. Lawyers in Karachi announced a "black day" protest against the move, which the federal cabinet had justified by citing the provincial government's "deliberate disengagement" from fighting terrorism; PTI calls the action unconstitutional. Because the emergency was declared without a resolution from the provincial assembly, both houses of parliament must still approve it to keep it in force.
