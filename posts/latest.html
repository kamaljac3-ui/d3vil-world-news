Americas

Ecuador arrests two-time presidential candidate Luisa González in money-laundering probe

Ecuadorian police arrested Luisa González, a two-time presidential candidate and leader of the Citizen Revolution party, at her home in Manabí province on Thursday as part of a money-laundering investigation into her 2023 campaign financing. A judge ordered González and two co-defendants held in pretrial detention the next day in a case prosecutors call "Laberinto," which examines roughly $500,000 in campaign advertising funds. González, who lost a 2025 runoff to President Daniel Noboa, has denied wrongdoing and called the case "absurd" political persecution.

Sources: Reuters (via Investing.com), EFE (via Infobae)

Salvadoran forces battle Haitian gangs as search for four missing soldiers continues

Salvadoran special forces deployed to Haiti clashed repeatedly with gang members this week while searching for four aviation technicians who went missing after their Gang Suppression Force vehicle was ambushed near Croix-des-Bouquets on October 18. The GSF said the fighting has killed at least seven gang members and wounded four of its own personnel, and officials believe the four Salvadorans are alive but have not located them. President Nayib Bukele has warned of a "stronger response" if the men are not released. The men's location and condition have not been independently confirmed.

Sources: AP (via Washington Post), Al Jazeera

Europe

France deploys 70,000 police as nationwide protests resume under partial bans

French authorities deployed roughly 70,000 police officers nationwide on Saturday as a fresh wave of demonstrations began, a week after rioting and looting that followed cost-of-living protests left more than 2,700 people detained. Officials banned marches outright in Lyon and Marseille and restricted routes elsewhere, and police fired tear gas to disperse protesters in a banned area near Lyon's university, according to an AFP journalist. President Emmanuel Macron said any march not organized by a recognized union would be blocked "wherever we can," a stance unions and some court challenges are contesting.

Sources: France 24 (AFP), Euronews

Middle East

Netanyahu orders major West Bank operation after Hebron checkpoint attack kills two soldiers

Two Palestinian gunmen attacked an Israeli military checkpoint near Hebron on Friday, killing two Israeli soldiers and wounding three others before both attackers were killed, the army said. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu ordered a full lockdown of the occupied West Bank and a broader military operation he said was meant to "destroy" militant infrastructure, with troops carrying out house-to-house searches in Hebron. Hamas, which stopped short of formally claiming the attack, praised it as a "heroic operation"; the assault came four days before Israel's first national election since the October 2023 Hamas attack.

Sources: AP (via Washington Post), Al Jazeera

Gunmen kill seven at a Jordanian church during a funeral

Gunmen opened fire inside a Greek Orthodox church in the Jordanian city of Karak during a funeral on Friday, killing at least seven people and wounding roughly 34 more, authorities said. Jordanian officials called it a terrorist attack; King Abdullah II vowed a "harsh" response and declared three days of national mourning. No group had claimed responsibility, and the Associated Press said it could not independently verify how many gunmen took part. The attackers' identities and motive have not been confirmed.

Sources: AP (via PBS NewsHour), Al Jazeera

Africa

Mob beats foreign national to death in Durban amid anti-migrant unrest

A crowd of roughly 50 people beat a foreign national to death in central Durban on Thursday, South African police said, after two men broke away from an anti-immigration march and the larger group then assaulted him. Police opened a murder investigation but had not established the victim's nationality or a motive; the killing followed a motorcade by the "March and March" anti-immigration campaign and came two weeks after soldiers were deployed to Durban during two days of rioting and looting targeting foreign nationals. The victim's identity and the attackers' motive have not been confirmed.

Sources: Reuters (via US News), Africanews

Drone strikes kill schoolchildren in Ethiopia's Amhara region, four more in Tigray

A drone strike hit Sana Elementary School in Ethiopia's Amhara region on October 20, killing at least six schoolchildren and injuring more than 20 others, a teacher and residents said. A separate drone strike the same day killed four people, including two children, in Tigray's Finarwa village, AFP reported, as fighting between federal forces and armed opposition groups escalates in both regions following the breakdown of a 2022 ceasefire agreement. Full casualty figures for the Amhara school strike have not been independently confirmed.

Sources: AFP (via The Peninsula), allAfrica (Addis Standard)

Asia

Kim Jong Un makes first China trip in a year, reportedly meets Premier Li Qiang

North Korean leader Kim Jong Un crossed into China at Dandong on Saturday over a newly opened bridge, his first visit to the country in about a year, South Korea's Unification Ministry said. A lawmaker briefed by South Korean intelligence said Kim met Chinese Premier Li Qiang in the border city, though it remained unclear how long Kim would stay or whether he would travel on to Beijing. The visit falls a day before the anniversary of China's entry into the Korean War. Neither Beijing nor Pyongyang has officially confirmed the visit or the meeting with Li.

Sources: AP (via US News), Reuters (via Investing.com)

Typhoon Champi kills one, injures 500 in Taiwan before heading toward China

Typhoon Champi swept past Taiwan this week, killing a man swept away by waves off Liuqiu island and injuring more than 500 people, Taiwan's fire department said. The storm knocked out power to roughly 288,000 households at its peak and damaged roads across the island before weakening and tracking toward China's eastern coast.

Sources: CNA (Focus Taiwan), AP (via US News)
