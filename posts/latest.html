Americas

UN report finds nearly 1,200 killed in Haiti's gang violence over the summer

The UN's integrated office in Haiti, BINUH, said in a quarterly human-rights report released this week that at least 1,167 people were killed and 459 injured nationwide between July and September, with kidnappings in the capital also rising over the same period, according to Reuters. Gang members were blamed for more than 45% of the casualties, while over half occurred during government or allied operations against armed groups; the report also documented 344 cases of sexual violence by gangs and a tripling of alleged extrajudicial killings by police, to 44 cases. Gang coalitions still control an estimated 85-90% of Port-au-Prince even as El Salvador's deployment of more than 2,000 troops and intensified joint raids against gang strongholds continue.

Sources: Reuters (via Internazionale), UPI

Europe

France deploys 70,000 police for a fifth straight Saturday of nationwide protests

France's Interior Ministry mobilized roughly 70,000 police and gendarmes nationwide on Saturday for a fifth consecutive weekend of demonstrations against the cost of living and a proposed police bill, with Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez saying he expected 50 to 60 separate marches, according to AFP. The previous Saturday's protests drew about 146,000 people nationwide, including 46,000 in Paris, French authorities said. The contested bill would grant officers a presumption of legitimate self-defense when using their weapons, a provision that has become a focal point for Yellow Vest activists, students and unions joining the movement.

Sources: AFP (via BSS News), France 24

Russian strikes kill nine and hit a Kyiv hospital, as a Ukrainian strike kills four in Russia

Russian drone and missile attacks killed at least nine people and injured 61 across Ukraine over 24 hours into Friday, Ukrainian officials said, including a strike whose debris sparked a fire at a trauma hospital in central Kyiv, according to Reuters. Russia's military said it downed nearly 1,200 Ukrainian drones in the same period, while Russian authorities said a Ukrainian drone strike separately killed four workers and sparked a fire at an oil refinery in Russia's Volgograd region. President Volodymyr Zelensky said speed restrictions on SpaceX's Starshield satellite system were making it harder for Ukraine to intercept incoming drones, as the strikes capped a week of US-mediated diplomacy on ending the war.

Sources: Reuters (via Asharq Al-Awsat), RTÉ

Middle East

UN Security Council renews Yemen sanctions for another year, condemns Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia

The UN Security Council adopted Resolution 2837 this week, extending the Yemen sanctions regime — asset freezes and travel bans on designated individuals and groups — through November 2027 and renewing the mandate of its Panel of Experts, according to the Council's own meetings record. The resolution condemns the Houthis' renewed military escalation inside Yemen and their continued cross-border and maritime attacks on Saudi Arabia, and reaffirms an existing arms embargo against the group. The vote came as Saudi-led coalition forces reported intercepting fresh Houthi missiles and drones aimed at Riyadh and the kingdom's Eastern Province.

Sources: UN Security Council (press.un.org), Al Jazeera

Africa

Congo's Tshisekedi signs law opening a path to a possible third term

Democratic Republic of Congo President Félix Tshisekedi signed a law this week setting out how a new constitution could be drafted and put to a referendum, a measure critics fear could be used to scrap the two-term presidential limit that currently bars him from seeking re-election in 2028, according to Reuters. Parliament passed the bill in June, but the Constitutional Court sent it back for revision over provisions letting the president call a referendum on any issue deemed "of fundamental importance"; the revised version was signed and announced on state television this week. Tshisekedi has denied planning to seek a third term but said in May, "If people want me to have a third term, I will accept," while opposition coalition C64 has called the law an attempt to "grant an unlimited number of terms."

Sources: Reuters (via Arab News), Africanews

Congo's renamed M23 rebels push deeper into South Kivu, displacing 323,000

Fighters from the Front for the Refoundation of the Nation, the rebranded M23 movement, crossed Kahuzi-Biega National Park and seized the town of Bunyakiri and surrounding areas in eastern Congo's South Kivu province in an offensive that began around November 6, according to AFP. The UN's humanitarian agency OCHA estimates more than 323,000 people, about 54,000 households, were displaced within just a few days as the rebels advanced roughly 50 kilometers toward the strategic town of Walikale, while Congo's army says it has mounted a counteroffensive and disputes how much ground was lost. The fighting marks a sharp escalation of a conflict that had eased in recent months despite a US-brokered Congo-Rwanda peace deal reached in December.

Sources: AFP (via News24), The New Humanitarian

Asia

Taiwan accuses China of pressuring Papua New Guinea into expelling its diplomats

Taiwan's foreign ministry said this week it has begun withdrawing diplomatic and technical staff from Papua New Guinea after the Pacific nation ordered Taipei's representative office closed, and said it would halt aid programs and spot-market LNG purchases from PNG in response, according to Focus Taiwan, the Central News Agency's English service. Taiwan's deputy foreign minister said Beijing may have pressured or offered economic incentives to PNG's government, which recognizes China and has no formal ties with Taipei; China's foreign ministry welcomed the closure and said it would look to expand its own LNG imports from PNG instead. Taiwan noted that PNG's prime minister was expected to visit China around the APEC summit, a factor it says weighed on the decision.

Sources: Focus Taiwan / CNA, ANI

Malaysia presses ASEAN to finish South China Sea code of conduct as summit opens in Manila

Malaysia's foreign minister told counterparts at the ASEAN Foreign Ministers' Meeting in Manila on Saturday that negotiations on a South China Sea code of conduct with China have reached a critical stage, and urged renewed political will to conclude them, according to Bernama. He acknowledged that disputes over the code's geographical scope and legal force remain unresolved, even as ASEAN and China are still targeting a conclusion by the end of 2026; the Philippines' foreign secretary said the bloc remains focused on closing out the talks. The ministerial sessions mark the start of the 49th ASEAN Summit, with a planned Trump appearance at a related ASEAN-US summit expected later in the week.

Sources: Bernama, The Star
