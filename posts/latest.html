Americas

Chile's government strikes deal to cut diesel prices, winding down nationwide truckers' strike

Chile's government reached an agreement with the Fuerza del Norte and Centro-Sur trucking confederations on Thursday to cut diesel prices by 142 pesos a liter and freeze fuel costs through March 2027, aiming to end blockades that have disrupted highways since the strike began November 10, according to EFE. The deal follows two days of talks and comes at an estimated cost of roughly $500 million to the treasury of President José Antonio Kast's government, which had earlier offered a narrower 90-day price cap that unions rejected. Some trucking groups in the Araucánia, O'Higgins and Antofagasta regions rejected the new terms and kept blockading roads, so whether the strike has fully ended remains unclear.

Sources: EFE (via Infobae), Rio Times

Brazil's Senate advances Lula's fiscal adjustment package in push to calm markets

Brazil's Senate approved the core text of a government fiscal-adjustment package by 45 votes to 31 on Thursday, a measure President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva's administration says will save at least 45 billion reais (about $8 billion) heading into the 2027 budget, according to EFE. The bill would cap real spending growth at 2% rather than 2.5% in years when fiscal-containment measures are triggered and cut tax benefits across the board by 10%, with opposition leader Rogério Marinho calling it an accounting trick that shifts costs onto states and businesses. Four remaining amendments go to a vote on November 17 before the bill moves to the lower house.

Sources: EFE (via Infobae), Rio Times

Europe

US envoys brief Zelensky in Dublin after Moscow talks with Putin on ending the war

US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Russian President Vladimir Putin in the Kremlin for about two hours on Thursday, then flew to Dublin to brief Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky on the sidelines of the European Political Community summit, according to the AP. Zelensky said the envoys told him Europe would be included as a single negotiating voice in future talks with Moscow and that the next prisoner exchange would be the largest of the war so far, while Putin warned that any European troops deployed to Ukraine after a ceasefire would be treated as legitimate military targets. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said he had no objection to a five-country European format — Germany, France, Britain, Italy and Poland — representing the continent in future negotiations with Russia.

Sources: AP (via PBS NewsHour), AFP (via France 24)

UN chief calls for a "climate justice compact" as COP31 leaders summit meets in Antalya

UN Secretary-General António Guterres urged governments at COP31's leaders summit in Antalya, Turkey, on Wednesday to adopt a new "Climate Justice Compact" that would tax fossil-fuel companies' windfall profits to help fund developing countries' climate adaptation, declaring "polluters must pay," according to UN News. Roughly 40 heads of state and government, including Brazil's Lula and Australia's Anthony Albanese, addressed the summit, which organizers framed as a push to turn years of pledges into concrete action amid warnings that current national climate plans would deliver only about a third of the emissions cuts needed. The United States is not attending the conference, which runs through November 20.

Sources: UN News, RTÉ

Middle East

IAEA says it still cannot verify Iran's uranium stockpile as inspection standoff drags on

The International Atomic Energy Agency said this week it is "increasingly concerned" that Iran has still not allowed inspectors into the Isfahan Fuel Enrichment Plant, a site Tehran declared in June 2025 but has never opened for inspection, and that it cannot verify the location or status of Iran's roughly 440.9 kilograms of uranium enriched to 60%, according to the AP. Iran told the agency that normal monitoring cannot resume while it considers itself under threat, though it says it remains committed to resuming full cooperation once "legal and practical conditions" allow; a confidential report seen by AFP urges Tehran to take incremental steps that would let inspectors return. The verification gap dates to US and Israeli strikes on Iranian nuclear sites in June 2025, and Iran denies seeking nuclear weapons.

Sources: AP (via WNCT), Al Jazeera

Africa

Ethiopia-Kenya border crisis deepens as Moyale clashes displace tens of thousands

Fighting between Ethiopia's Oromia and Somali regional forces for control of the border town of Moyale has driven an estimated 50,000 people into Kenya since the violence erupted on October 18, according to Reuters and the International Organization for Migration. The clashes, triggered by a dispute over a Somali regional administration office and flag in the jointly administered town, have killed at least nine people and spread to nearby districts, while a new IOM assessment found most displaced families lack enough food, water or shelter. Kenyan officials say they are in direct contact with Ethiopia's federal government to try to end the fighting, and the UN has appealed for $8.9 million to support the displaced over the next three months.

Sources: Reuters (via Hiiraan Online), UN News

MSF warns Ebola responders in Congo are going unpaid as outbreak funding gap tops $700 million

Médecins Sans Frontières said this week that health workers fighting Congo's Ebola outbreak remain unpaid, poorly equipped and short of fuel for ambulances, even as the epidemic has infected more than 11,000 people and killed over 5,400, according to the AP. Africa CDC Director-General Jean Kaseya said more than $700 million of the $962 million disbursed to response organizations has not yet been accounted for by Congolese authorities, though he cautioned it was too early to call the gap embezzlement rather than a reporting failure. MSF called on Congo's government and UN agencies to strengthen financial oversight so money reaches the outbreak's front line.

Sources: AP (via KXNET), PM News Nigeria

Asia

Taiwan says China staged unannounced "silent intimidation" naval drills across the western Pacific

Taiwanese security officials said this week that China ran a large, unannounced military exercise across the western Pacific and around Taiwan in early November, at one point massing more than 100 navy and coast guard vessels with simulated strikes on other militaries and cities, according to Reuters. One official called the deployment larger than China's last formally named Taiwan drills, "Justice Mission 2025," though officials said the Chinese presence had since shrunk to around 70 vessels within the first island chain. The exercise comes ahead of next week's APEC summit in Shenzhen, where Chinese President Xi Jinping and US President Donald Trump are expected to meet, and as Beijing's state-run Global Times warned Taiwan's government would face a "harsh reckoning" over any move toward independence.

Sources: Reuters (via The Japan Times), Taipei Times

Malaysia's king and attorney-general clash over former PM Najib's conditional pardon

Malaysia's Attorney-General's Chambers sought an audience with King Sultan Ibrahim this week after deferring implementation of former Prime Minister Najib Razak's house arrest, saying it needed to clarify how to interpret his September conditional pardon, according to Bernama. The king called the pardon "clear, evident and without any ambiguity" and warned that disputing it defies his decree, after Najib paid the 50-million-ringgit fine required under the pardon's terms on November 6. Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim defended his 2023 decision to bring Najib's pardon application before the Pardons Board, saying it was meant to protect any prisoner's constitutional right to seek clemency, as coalition partner DAP pressed for a fuller explanation.

Sources: Bernama, Malay Mail
