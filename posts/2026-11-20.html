Americas

US and Venezuela agree to restore military ties on surprise Hegseth visit to Caracas

US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth made an unannounced trip to Caracas on Wednesday and signed a deal reestablishing military cooperation with Venezuela, including joint training and intelligence sharing, the Associated Press reported. The agreement follows the US operation that captured former President Nicolás Maduro earlier this year; Hegseth framed the pact as part of a regional counter-narcotics push, while Venezuela's interim government, led by Delcy Rodríguez, said the training would help the countries face shared security threats.

Sources: AP (via Yahoo), Al Jazeera

Thousands march across Colombia in second nationwide protest against de la Espriella's economic agenda

Trade unions and opposition parties led a second nationwide demonstration on Thursday against President Abelardo de la Espriella's proposals to lower the income-tax threshold and raise health-care contributions, AFP reported. De la Espriella said he would tolerate peaceful marches but not road blockades, after an October decree loosening rules on police use of force at protests drew criticism from rights groups; no major violence was reported.

Sources: Arab News (AFP), The Sun (AFP)

Europe

Putin rules out limited Ukraine ceasefire after repeated strikes cripple Kerch Bridge

Russian President Vladimir Putin said Thursday he will not negotiate a limited ceasefire with Ukraine, citing a string of Ukrainian strikes that have hit the Kerch Bridge linking Russia to occupied Crimea for a third time in a week, according to wire reports. Reuters reported the attacks have further strained Russia's supply lines to the peninsula, with road traffic across the bridge still suspended as crews work to restore rail service on a single track.

Sources: U.S. News (Reuters), CBS News

EU proposes new €45 billion loan for Ukraine, to be repaid from Russian reparations

The European Commission proposed lending Ukraine up to €45 billion more next year, on top of an existing €90 billion facility, with Commission President Ursula von der Leyen saying Kyiv would only need to repay it once Russia pays war reparations, Reuters reported. The loan, split between military aid and budget support, is meant to cover roughly two-thirds of a €70 billion hole in Ukraine's 2027 budget and still needs approval from all 27 EU member states.

Sources: The Star Malaysia (Reuters), Euronews

Middle East

Iraq reaches last-minute compromise with Iran-backed militia to avert clash over Baghdad-area stronghold

Iraqi security forces began deploying Friday into Jurf al-Sakhar, a stronghold of the Iran-backed Kata'ib Hezbollah militia south of Baghdad, under a compromise deal reached after Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi had readied forces to move against the group, Reuters reported. Security officials said the arrangement calls for the militia to withdraw in stages over a month, following accusations that Kata'ib Hezbollah ran an oil-smuggling network siphoning hundreds of millions of dollars from state pipelines, which the group denies.

Sources: ThePrint (Reuters), Al-Monitor (Reuters)

Saudi-led coalition orders civilians out of Yemen's main port after Houthi missile strike on Riyadh

The Saudi-led coalition told civilians to evacuate Yemen's Houthi-controlled Hodeidah port on Friday, posting a map of the designated evacuation zone, Reuters reported. The warning follows Thursday's Houthi missile strike that damaged a tower in Riyadh's financial district housing Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund, and comes amid the heaviest fighting between Saudi-backed forces and the Houthis since a rough calm took hold in 2022.

Sources: Arab News Pakistan (Reuters), Gulf News

Africa

Rights group says Sudanese army strikes killed at least 13 civilians in West Kordofan, with a deadlier strike days later

Sudan's Emergency Lawyers rights group said an air strike by the Sudanese Armed Forces killed at least 13 civilians, including women and children, in the Abu Qalb area of West Kordofan state, and the Associated Press reported it could not independently verify the toll. Rights monitors separately reported a drone strike days later on a school sheltering displaced people in the same state, killing at least 16 more; that figure has not been corroborated by a wire service. Sudan's army has continued pushing toward Al-Nuhud, West Kordofan's second-largest city.

Sources: Al Jazeera, The Washington Post (AP)

Mozambique's top opposition leader goes on trial over deadly 2024 election unrest

Vena&ncirc;ncio Mondlane, who challenged Mozambique's disputed 2024 election result, went on trial at the Supreme Court in Maputo this week on charges of instigating terrorism and inciting civil disobedience, the Associated Press reported. He denies all charges, saying his messages calling for "electoral truth" were political rather than incitement; the protests that followed the election were the deadliest against the ruling Frelimo party since independence, killing more than 300 people.

Sources: AP (via Yahoo), Al Jazeera

Asia

UN's top court finds Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, a first for the ICJ

The International Court of Justice ruled Thursday, by a 9-6 vote, that Myanmar's military committed genocide against the Rohingya during 2017 "clearance operations" that killed roughly 10,000 people, the first time in the court's history it has held a state responsible for genocide, the Associated Press reported. The binding ruling, which cannot be appealed, orders Myanmar to investigate and prosecute those responsible and to allow displaced Rohingya to return safely; Myanmar's military-backed government called the judgment unfair.

Sources: PBS NewsHour (AP), Al Jazeera

Bangladesh deploys paramilitary troops nationwide as unrest grows over Hasina's planned return

Bangladesh deployed hundreds of paramilitary troops across the country this week and intensified a crackdown on supporters of ousted former Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina, Reuters and AFP reported, after a crude bomb exploded outside a cabinet minister's Dhaka residence and arsonists targeted a lawmaker's house. Police said at least 71 members of banned Awami League-linked organizations were arrested in 24 hours, as Hasina says she plans to return to Bangladesh in December; Prime Minister Tarique Rahman blamed India for the unrest.

Sources: The Star Malaysia (Reuters), New Straits Times (AFP)

Cyclone Akvan makes landfall in Odisha after more than 370,000 evacuated

Cyclonic storm Akvan came ashore near Gopalpur in Odisha's Ganjam district on Friday morning, India's Press Trust of India reported, after the state evacuated roughly 372,000 people to shelters ahead of the landfall. Odisha's disaster management minister said no major damage or deaths had been reported as of Friday, with authorities crediting preparations that included hundreds of rescue teams and the cancellation of dozens of trains.

Sources: OrissaPost (PTI), Down To Earth
