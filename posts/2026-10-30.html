Americas

Flávio Bolsonaro flees to Miami in defiance of a Brazilian passport order

Brazilian Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, defeated in the Oct. 25 presidential runoff by Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, left the country through Argentina and Ecuador rather than surrender his passports under a Supreme Court order tied to a money-laundering investigation, landing in Miami on Wednesday with his family. AFP reported his Brazilian and diplomatic passports were canceled, though he also holds Italian citizenship; he has denied fleeing, saying he left to protect his family, while Justice Alexandre de Moraes's order cited flight risk.

Sources: AP (via WECT), NPR

Carney presses "associate membership" for Canada at EU summit in Montreal

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney hosted European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and other EU leaders in Montreal this week, pushing to deepen a trade and security partnership and floating a new "associate member" status for Canada within the bloc. The move comes as Ottawa seeks to diversify away from the United States amid President Trump's trade pressure on allies, though EU officials say the title and its details remain unsettled, with France favoring strict "Made in Europe" defense-spending rules that could limit Canadian firms' access.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), CBC

Colombia's transitional tribunal charges nine ex-FARC commanders with war crimes

Colombia's Special Jurisdiction for Peace charged nine former commanders of the FARC's Comando Conjunto Central, including ex-senator Pablo Catatumbo, with war crimes and crimes against humanity over 94 armed attacks, massacres, disappearances and displacements in Tolima, Huila and Quindío between 1991 and 2016. The tribunal said it is the first time these defendants have had to answer for this documented pattern of violence, and the nine now have 20 working days to say whether they accept the charges.

Sources: EFE (via Vanguardia), Infobae

Europe

Spain declares mourning after high-speed rail crash kills 68

A high-speed train slammed into another that had stopped on the tracks near Urda, in Toledo province south of Madrid, on Wednesday, killing at least 68 people and injuring about 80, officials said. Spain's transport minister called the crash the result of an "inexplicable" order from a central control center after a safety system had failed, and Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez declared three days of national mourning for the country's second fatal high-speed crash this year.

Sources: AP (via NPR), Al Jazeera

European gas prices top €100 for first time since the 2022 energy crisis

Benchmark European natural gas prices briefly crossed €100 per megawatt-hour on Wednesday, the first time since the 2022 energy crisis, as disruption to Middle East LNG shipping tied to the Iran war kept supply tight. EU Energy Commissioner Dan Jørgensen warned the bloc faces "another energy crisis" heading into winter, with gas storage at just 75% in October against the usual 90% target, and analysts forecasting at least another year of elevated prices even if the war ends.

Sources: Reuters (via Euronews), RTÉ

Zelensky says Ukraine is still waiting on US sign-off for Patriot missiles

President Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukraine has waited nearly a month for Washington to approve shipments of Patriot and NASAMS interceptors funded by NATO allies through the PURL program, with deliveries running at roughly a third of the 35 interceptors a month Ukraine says it needs. US officials denied any political motive for the delay, though it comes amid friction between Washington and Kyiv over a separate US-Russia diesel deal and Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries.

Sources: Reuters (via US News), Kyiv Post

Middle East

Houthis strike Yemen's Aden airport for a sixth time in weeks

Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi rebels fired ballistic missiles at Aden International Airport on Wednesday after warning residents to evacuate, Yemeni officials told Reuters, the sixth such strike on the facility in recent weeks. A Houthi spokesperson said the attack targeted a Saudi cargo plane allegedly delivering weapons to a Yemeni government military base co-located at the airport; separately, the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted a ballistic missile fired toward the kingdom's Eastern Province.

Sources: Reuters, Arab News

Qatar presses new draft deal on Tehran as US-Iran mediation widens

Qatari mediators returned to Tehran to press a new draft agreement aimed at ending the US-Iran conflict, after presenting the proposal on Oct. 25 with a response expected by week's end, a Qatari official told Reuters. Washington wants nuclear issues addressed from the outset, while Tehran is demanding the US first lift its naval blockade and oil-export sanctions; Reuters separately reported that Pakistan's army chief has presented Tehran with a competing framework, with Pakistan, Turkey, Saudi Arabia and Qatar potentially acting as guarantors.

Sources: Reuters, Al-Monitor

Africa

Dozens to over 100 killed in disputed strike on a Darfur market

A Sudanese rights group, Emergency Lawyers, said an army drone strike on a crowded market in Nyala, South Darfur, killed more than 100 people and wounded 113 on Monday, blaming Sudan's military; a separate monitor, the Sudan Doctors Network, put the toll at 21 dead from shelling, and AP said it could not independently verify casualty figures. Médecins Sans Frontières said its Nyala hospital received 36 bodies and treated more than 150 wounded, the latest violence in a market controlled by the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces, which has fought Sudan's army since 2023.

Sources: AP (via ClickOnDetroit), Al Jazeera

DRC's Ebola outbreak passes 10,000 cases, nears 5,000 deaths

The Democratic Republic of Congo's Bundibugyo-strain Ebola outbreak has surpassed 10,000 confirmed cases and is approaching 5,000 deaths according to government data released this week, making it the second-largest and second-deadliest Ebola outbreak on record after the 2014-2016 West African epidemic. Health authorities are reporting 400 to 500 new infections weekly, concentrated in Ituri province, and no approved vaccine or treatment exists for this particular viral strain.

Sources: Al Jazeera, allAfrica

Nearly 50,000 flee into Kenya as Ethiopia's Moyale border clashes continue

Fighting around the town of Moyale on the Ethiopia-Kenya border, which began Oct. 21 for reasons that remain unclear, has pushed an estimated 50,000 people across the border into northern Kenya, up from about 36,600 just three days earlier, the UN's migration agency said. The IOM warned that the fast-rising numbers of new arrivals risk overwhelming local services in a region already strained by displacement.

Sources: Addis Standard, UN News

Asia

China sends only a low-level envoy to ASEAN defense summit after a Philippine snub

China downgraded its presence at the 20th ASEAN Defence Ministers' Meeting-Plus in Cebu on Thursday to a single defense attaché, its lowest-level representation at the forum since 2010, with Beijing blaming a Philippine official for blocking fuller participation. Australian Defence Minister Richard Marles told reporters on the sidelines that China's military buildup — which he called the largest conventional build-up since World War Two — has created "pressure" across Southeast Asia and urged partners to deepen cooperation; US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth also attended.

Sources: The Diplomat, The Philippine Star

Cyclone Arnab kills at least three as India evacuates over 180,000

Cyclone Arnab, a very severe cyclonic storm, made landfall near Narsapur in Andhra Pradesh on Wednesday, killing at least three people and damaging hundreds of homes, India's National Disaster Response Force said, even as the state government claimed no casualties. More than 180,000 people were evacuated across Andhra Pradesh and Odisha, with authorities reporting roughly 70,900 hectares of crop damage as the storm weakened after moving inland.

Sources: AP (via ABC News Australia), PTI (via Daily Pioneer)

Indonesian students protest Prabowo's handling of worst fires in a decade

Hundreds of students and civil-society activists protested in Jakarta and other cities on Wednesday against President Prabowo Subianto's handling of forest fires that have produced Indonesia's worst haze crisis in 11 years, demanding the fires be declared a national disaster and that responsible companies face prosecution. Indonesian authorities have named 244 people and 14 companies as suspects in fire-related cases, and Prabowo's approval rating has fallen to roughly 44.5% from over 81% a year earlier amid the mounting anger.

Sources: Reuters (via The Print), The Star
