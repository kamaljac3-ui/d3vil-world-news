Americas

Explosive drones hit a Colombian police base in Cúcuta, damaging dozens of homes

Drones carrying explosives struck a police base in the border city of Cúcuta on November 23, injuring civilians and damaging about 60 homes, AP reported, while a separate drone attack hit a police substation in nearby Tibú. No group had claimed responsibility as of Monday, though Colombia Reports said the ELN guerrilla group has carried out a string of drone attacks in the surrounding Catatumbo region; the strikes come as Colombia's government presses an offensive against rebel groups and drug gangs in the area.

Sources: AP (via Washington Post), Colombia Reports

Brazil's lower house passes Lula's fiscal bill as the real slides past a key level

Brazil's Chamber of Deputies approved President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva's roughly $10 billion fiscal adjustment package on November 23, sending it to Lula to sign, after the Senate had already cleared it, Bloomberg reported. The vote came hours after the real weakened past six per dollar, a level investors have watched closely, reflecting doubts that the measures — which the government projects will save R$46-56 billion in 2027 — will be enough to stabilize Brazil's public finances.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), Bloomberg

Europe

Ukraine says naval drones sank a Russian warship at a Libyan port, extending the war's reach

Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukrainian forces used seven domestically made sea drones to sink the Russian corvette Merkury at Benghazi's naval base on November 22, also destroying a helicopter aboard and damaging a sanctioned Russian cargo ship, AP reported. The Kremlin said only that it had "taken note" of the claim, while satellite imagery reviewed by open-source analysts showed the vessel capsized; the UN's Libya mission warned against drawing the country into the wider war.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), Al Jazeera

Russian drones and missiles kill at least 15 across the Kyiv region as toll in one town climbs

An overnight Russian missile and drone barrage on November 22-23 killed at least 15 people across the Kyiv region, officials said, with the toll in the town of Dymer alone climbing to 11 as rescuers pulled bodies from a collapsed apartment building, Reuters reported. A separate strike on a grocery store in Vyshneve killed another person, while Ukraine's air force said Russia launched 186 attack drones in a single wave as both sides continue trading long-range strikes.

Sources: Reuters (via Sight Magazine), Kyiv Independent

Middle East

Israeli strikes kill UNRWA worker and others in Gaza despite ceasefire

An Israeli airstrike killed a Gaza man working for the UN's Palestinian refugee agency near a water well in the Jabalia refugee camp on November 23, wounding three others, Reuters reported. A separate strike on a vehicle in western Gaza City killed two more people the same day, and AP reported at least eight killed in three strikes overnight into Tuesday, including one on a police station in Khan Younis; Israel's military did not immediately respond to questions about the incidents.

Sources: Arab News (Reuters), Al Jazeera

Iran says tankers hit mines in Strait of Hormuz despite US naval escort

Iran's Revolutionary Guard said three oil tankers struck naval mines and caught fire while transiting the Strait of Hormuz under US military escort on November 24, calling the escort a failure, AP reported as part of its daily roundup of the ongoing Iran war. Al Jazeera's live coverage said Iran's army put the figure at two tankers; US Central Command has not responded to the claims, which follow a pattern of similar Iranian mine-strike claims since the war resumed that have gone independently unverified.

Sources: AP (via Local 10), Al Jazeera

Africa

Amnesty says Ethiopian-allied shelling of Tigray's Alamata city may be a war crime

Amnesty International said it had independently verified 14 civilian deaths from four artillery barrages Ethiopia's military and the allied Tigray Peace Forces fired into residential areas of Alamata city in late September as they advanced, and called for an international investigation, Reuters reported. Amnesty's weapons analysts matched shell fragments at the scene to munitions used by the ENDF and its allies; neither the ENDF nor Tigray's TPLF forces responded to Reuters' requests for comment, though the TPLF called the allegations unfounded in a written reply to Amnesty.

Sources: Reuters (via Yahoo News), Amnesty International

Fighting near DR Congo hospital traps Ebola responders as outbreak's toll keeps climbing

Heavy fighting near a hospital in DR Congo's Ituri province forced Ebola response teams to shelter in place for more than 24 hours, the World Health Organization said, disrupting containment work in the area where the Bundibugyo-virus outbreak first emerged. WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said the outbreak had reached more than 11,600 confirmed cases and roughly 5,700 deaths as of November 17 — making it one of the deadliest Ebola outbreaks on record — and that insecurity is now one of the biggest obstacles to the response.

Sources: UN News (WHO), Al Jazeera

Asia

Taliban accuses Pakistan of arming anti-government fighters as border war grinds on

Afghanistan's Taliban-led government accused Pakistan of backing armed groups seeking to destabilize its rule, with Foreign Minister Amir Khan Muttaqi calling it a "project to destabilise Afghanistan," IANS reported. Pakistan rejected the allegation as propaganda; the accusation comes amid border clashes that have continued since late October, with Pakistan's army saying it killed 74 fighters in raids along the frontier and Islamabad separately accusing Kabul of sheltering the Pakistani Taliban militants who attack its security forces.

Sources: IANS (via The Hawk), Al Jazeera

Fiji declares nationwide disaster as El Niño drought reaches crisis level

Fiji's government declared a nationwide state of disaster on November 18 as an El Niño-driven drought left communities facing severe water shortages, Reuters reported, with the forecast calling for below-normal rainfall into at least April. Prime Minister Sitiveni Rabuka's cabinet reallocated FJ$20 million for water deliveries and relief, and the government has since ordered schools across the Pacific nation to close for five days from November 30 to protect children as the crisis continues.

Sources: Reuters (via NTD), Pacific Islands News Association
