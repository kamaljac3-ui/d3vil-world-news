Americas

Argentina won't say whether it helped defeated Brazilian candidate Bolsonaro leave the country

Brazil's foreign ministry summoned Argentina's top diplomat in Brasília last week to explain Buenos Aires' role in Senator Flávio Bolsonaro's departure through Argentina after he lost the October 25 presidential runoff to Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, Reuters and AP reported, with Bolsonaro later reaching the US on humanitarian parole. Argentine presidential spokesman Adrián Ravier declined Tuesday to say whether President Javier Milei's government assisted the exit, calling the question "irrelevant" and insisting Bolsonaro crossed the border with valid documents. Relations between the two governments were already strained after Brazil downgraded ties with Argentina in August over remarks Milei made about Lula.

Sources: Reuters/AP (via The Spokesman-Review), Buenos Aires Times

Colombia's drone war escalates again, with two more attacks on security forces in 48 hours

Colombia's army said an ELN-attributed drone attack killed two soldiers and wounded five in San José del Palmar, Chocó, over the weekend, and explosive-laden drones struck a police station in Chimichagua, Cesar, early Tuesday without causing injuries. The incidents are the latest in a drone campaign that AFP reported has more than doubled in the first five months of this year under President Abelardo de la Espriella, who broke off peace talks with armed groups after taking office in August; he has vowed to "bring the full weight and legitimate force of the State down upon the criminals."

Sources: AFP (via Al Jazeera), El Heraldo

Europe

Spain's conservatives extend their lead as campaigning intensifies before Nov. 29 snap vote

Polling released this week shows Alberto Núñez Feijóo's conservative Partido Popular widening its lead over Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez's Socialists ahead of Spain's November 29 snap election, which Sánchez called after parliament rejected his housing-relief measures. One poll put the PSOE below 100 seats for the first time in its series, while PP and the far-right Vox would together clear the 176-seat majority threshold, according to Reuters; the official campaign opens November 13 ahead of a Nov. 16 televised debate between the two leaders.

Sources: Reuters (via Al Jazeera), Rio Times

Cyberattacks on supermarket refrigeration systems spread to a dozen European countries

A wave of cyberattacks hit refrigeration and cooling systems at supermarkets in Germany, Romania, the UK and elsewhere this week, spoiling food and prompting the EU to call the attacks a "deliberate attempt to disrupt our food supply chain," agriculture commissioner Christophe Hansen said, according to AFP and Reuters reporting carried by Al Jazeera. A Romanian cybersecurity official told AFP the intrusions "seem semi-amateurish" and that authorities do not suspect a state actor; no one has claimed responsibility, and EU agencies met Tuesday with national authorities to coordinate a response. It is the second such wave to hit European supermarkets in a month, after an earlier attack swept hundreds of French stores in October.

Sources: AFP/Reuters (via Al Jazeera), CNBC

Middle East

US forces strike Iranian targets in the Strait of Hormuz hours before American midterms

A US official told the Associated Press that American forces carried out defensive strikes on Iranian targets in the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, including forces that had used surface-to-air missiles against US assets, after Iranian state television reported explosions on Qeshm Island. The strikes were not publicly announced and appeared limited rather than a return to full-scale hostilities, though reporting indicates Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is pressing President Trump for a further round after this week's elections. Mediators are reportedly still working on a framework in which Iran would halt further attacks in the strait in exchange for US concessions.

Sources: AP (via PBS NewsHour), The Washington Post

Trump says US will end $3.8 billion in annual security assistance to Israel

President Trump said Tuesday that Washington is ending a 10-year, $3.8-billion-a-year security assistance deal with Israel brokered under the Obama administration, telling Fox and Friends he had agreed to the move with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu because "they no longer need it." Netanyahu has previously said he wants to reduce Israel's reliance on US support, telling CBS in May he hoped it could reach "zero" within a decade. The announcement, made as Americans voted in midterm elections where support for Israel has been contentious, does not affect existing weapons already committed under the memorandum.

Sources: AFP (via SCMP), Gulf News

Israeli strikes kill more Palestinians in Gaza despite yearlong ceasefire

Israeli strikes killed at least five Palestinians across the Gaza Strip on Monday, including a 17-year-old girl struck by a drone in Gaza City and a security officer killed in al-Mawasi, local health officials told Reuters, as Israel continues to intensify strikes it says target Hamas members rearming in violation of a year-old ceasefire; Hamas denies the accusation and says Israel is undermining the deal. Gaza's Health Ministry says more than 1,500 Palestinians have been killed in Israeli strikes since the ceasefire began, a figure Israel has not independently verified.

Sources: Reuters (via Dawn), Al Jazeera

Africa

Congo rebel coalition threatens to quit Doha peace talks over Tshisekedi's referendum push

A rebel coalition that includes M23 and is now led by former president Joseph Kabila threatened this week to abandon Qatar-mediated peace talks with Kinshasa if the government proceeds with a constitutional referendum that critics say could let President Félix Tshisekedi seek a third term, after Congo's Senate unanimously approved a referendum law last week. Coalition spokesman Bertrand Bisimwa said the Doha agreements were built on the current constitution and that changing it "could undermine the entire peace process," a storyline Reuters has tracked since talks resumed earlier this year. The dispute comes as the US sanctioned Rwanda's defense ministry and moved to end Rwanda's duty-free US trade access over alleged support for M23.

Sources: Reuters (via Yahoo News), Al Jazeera

Sudan accuses Ethiopia of hosting RSF drone bases in UN complaint; Guterres "appalled"

Sudan's government filed complaints with the UN Security Council and the African Union this week accusing Ethiopia of letting the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces launch drone strikes from Ethiopian territory, naming Asosa Airport and a Bahir Dar air base as launch sites; Ethiopia denies the accusation and counters that Sudan's army backs Tigrayan rebels on its own soil. UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres' spokesman said he was "appalled" by the escalating drone attacks and that strikes on Sudanese civilian infrastructure violate international humanitarian law; AFP has reported the broader drone campaign killed at least 30 people in a single October strike in Sudan's Blue Nile region. Sudan's figures on drone strikes originating from Ethiopia have not been independently verified.

Sources: Al Jazeera (citing AFP), AFP (via Al Jazeera)

Asia

Indonesia's police chief of staff removed as suspect in wiretapping of President Prabowo

Indonesian police said President Prabowo Subianto, several ministers, political figures and journalists were illegally wiretapped by three senior police officers in a surveillance operation running from 2025 into this year, Antara reported. Prabowo dismissed former police chief Listyo Sigit Prabowo as his presidential chief of staff after Listyo was named a suspect, and ordered the national police chief to handle the investigation "objectively and transparently." Officials are divided on the legal severity: a coordinating minister said the charges amount to unlawful surveillance with no indication of treason, while the state secretary said the alleged wiretapping of the head of state could potentially constitute treason.

Sources: Antara, Antara

Thailand summons Myanmar's military attaché after cross-border airstrike damages Thai buildings

Thailand's armed forces chief summoned Myanmar's military attaché to Bangkok on November 2 and warned him to "exercise caution" after a Myanmar airstrike on a Karen National Union position opposite Mae Sam Laep village, in Thailand's Mae Hong Son province, damaged about 17 Thai homes and public buildings — including a health centre and a church — on October 31. No injuries were reported on the Thai side, but the incident is the latest spillover from an escalating Myanmar military campaign against resistance groups that killed at least 15 people, including a newborn, in separate strikes on a clinic and a school reported earlier this week.

Sources: Reuters (via The Star), The Irrawaddy
