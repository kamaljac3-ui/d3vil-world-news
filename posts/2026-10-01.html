Americas

Hurricane Polo slams Mexico's Pacific coast, flooding Baja California and Sonora

Hurricane Polo made its first landfall near Las Barrancas in Baja California Sur as a Category 2 storm early Tuesday, then crossed the Gulf of California and came ashore a second time near Guaymas, Sonora, as a Category 1 hurricane. The storm flooded streets, toppled trees and utility poles and cut power across both states; Mexican officials reported no deaths but warned that storm surge and heavy rain could cause "very severe" damage.

Sources: ABC News (AP), CBC News

US sanctions nearly 50 people tied to a Sinaloa Cartel faction, including a Baja California governor's ex-husband

The US Treasury sanctioned 21 people and 25 companies linked to the Sinaloa Cartel's Los Mayos faction on Tuesday, including faction leader Ismael Zambada Sicairos ("Mayito Flaco") and Carlos Alberto Torres Torres, the former husband of Baja California Governor Marina del Pilar Ávila. Treasury accused Torres of using his political influence to shield cartel operations in the Mexicali and Tijuana areas; Torres denied any criminal ties, and the governor said neither she nor her government would answer for the allegations.

Sources: Diario Libre (EFE), Rio Times Online

Europe

Drones violate Moldova's airspace three times in one day as Russia pounds Ukraine's grid

Three drones crossed into Moldovan airspace on Tuesday as Russia carried out a day-long attack on Ukraine's energy system, prompting authorities to twice close the country's northern and central airspace. One drone crashed and exploded near the village of Hîrbovăț; Romania and Poland scrambled fighter jets in response, and officials in Chișinău said fragments from one device matched the Russian-designated Geran-2, the Iranian-designed Shahed drone.

Sources: Yahoo News (AP), Kyiv Independent

Russia says European factories arming Ukraine are legitimate military targets

Russian foreign ministry spokesperson Maria Zakharova said Tuesday that weapons factories in European countries producing arms for Ukraine are "potential military targets for Russia," responding to a question about a proposed Patriot missile plant in Poland. Zakharova said European countries hosting such factories were becoming "fully involved" in the war; NATO and allied governments have said they will continue supporting Ukraine's air defenses regardless.

Sources: AAP (Reuters), Kyiv Independent

Middle East

FlyDubai co-pilot stabs captain, tries to crash Tel Aviv-bound jet before being subdued

A flydubai co-pilot stabbed the plane's captain and attempted to crash the Dubai-to-Tel Aviv flight carrying 174 people on Wednesday, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said, before the captain, crew and passengers forced their way into the cockpit and overpowered the attacker. The flight diverted to Tabuk, Saudi Arabia, where both pilots were hospitalized and the co-pilot was arrested; UAE prosecutors have opened an investigation into whether the incident involved "any terrorist activity or prior planning."

Sources: ABC News (AP), Al Jazeera

US completes military withdrawal from Iraq, ending Operation Inherent Resolve

US Central Command announced Wednesday that American forces had completed their withdrawal from Erbil Air Base, formally ending Operation Inherent Resolve, the 12-year coalition mission against the Islamic State group in Iraq. President Trump called the exit "a victory for the United States" and "a victory for Iraq"; the counter-ISIS task force will now be headquartered in Jordan overseeing operations in Syria, while US-Iraq military cooperation shifts onto a bilateral footing.

Sources: Yahoo News (AP), The Hill

Africa

Ethiopian government forces capture Tigray town as fighting kills at least 52 civilians

At least 52 civilians were killed over three days of fighting around the town of Alamata in Ethiopia's Tigray region, a doctor at the local hospital told the BBC, as government forces and allied militia captured the strategic town from Tigrayan rebels. The dead reportedly included children and cattle herders caught in artillery and explosive fire that struck residential areas; more than 700 wounded people have been admitted to hospitals supported by Médecins Sans Frontières, and fighting has since spread into the neighboring Amhara and Afar regions.

Sources: Yahoo News (Reuters), The Star (BBC)

Renewed fighting for control of Baidoa kills children, damages health facility, UNICEF says

Fighting between Somali government forces, backed by Turkish fighter jets and drones, and militia loyal to an ousted regional president has killed at least 23 people in and around the city of Baidoa since clashes erupted in mid-August. UNICEF said Tuesday that children were among those killed and wounded in the latest outbreak and that a health facility was damaged, calling on all parties to protect civilians and allow humanitarian access.

Sources: Yahoo News (AP), Al Jazeera

Asia

UN Command finds North Korea violated armistice in DMZ mine blast; Pyongyang denies it

The UN Command said a joint investigation found an active North Korean anti-personnel mine on the South Korean side of the Demilitarized Zone, determining that a Sept. 21 blast that injured three South Korean soldiers violated the Korean War armistice. South Korea's military demanded an apology and an end to North Korean border fortification work, while Kim Yo Jong, sister of North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, rejected the findings as a "farce" and warned of "merciless" retaliation if South Korean troops open fire.

Sources: The Korea Herald (Yonhap), Al Jazeera

Pakistani airstrikes on Afghanistan kill nine civilians, Kabul says; Islamabad says it hit militants

Pakistan carried out airstrikes on homes in Afghanistan's Helmand and Kunar provinces overnight Wednesday, which Afghanistan's Taliban government said killed nine women and children and wounded 11 others. Pakistan's information ministry said the strikes targeted militant hideouts and killed 22 fighters as part of its ongoing cross-border counterterrorism campaign; Kabul called the strikes "an act of aggression," and the competing casualty claims could not be independently verified.

Sources: ABC News (AP), Al Jazeera
