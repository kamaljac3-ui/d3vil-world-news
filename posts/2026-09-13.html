Americas

Brazil's Chief Justice removes Alexandre de Moraes from the "fake news" probe he led for years

Chief Justice Edson Fachin stripped Justice Alexandre de Moraes of control over the Supreme Court's long-running disinformation investigation, after a colleague unsealed records showing jailed banker Daniel Vorcaro — at the center of the collapsed Banco Master — had sought Moraes' legal advice before his arrest. Fachin upheld Moraes' past rulings but scheduled a full-court session for Tuesday to decide whether to formally investigate him, a rupture inside Brazil's top court that lands weeks ahead of a presidential race where Lula could face Flávio Bolsonaro.

Sources: Bloomberg, Northwest Arkansas Democrat-Gazette (AP)

Chile arrests 284 people in protests marking the Pinochet coup anniversary

Chilean police detained 284 people — including 31 minors and 16 foreign nationals — during protests Thursday and Friday marking 53 years since the 1973 coup, an 80% jump in arrests from last year's anniversary. It was the first year since Chile's return to democracy in 1990 with no official government commemoration, after President José Antonio Kast, the country's first far-right head of state since Pinochet, declined to hold one.

Sources: Arab News, Buenos Aires Times

Europe

Sabotage suspected after a French passenger train derails, injuring 44

A regional train carrying 186 passengers from Rouen to Caen derailed Friday evening near Cléon in Normandy, overturning one carriage and derailing four others; 44 people were injured, including an 18-year-old airlifted to a Rouen hospital. French police told The Associated Press a piece of rail found on the track was "probably" linked to the derailment and that a sabotage investigation is underway.

Sources: ABC News (AP), South China Morning Post

Middle East

AFP tallies roughly 500 dead in the Houthi offensive that seized the Bab el-Mandeb Strait

Following this week's Houthi capture of Yemen's Mocha port and Mayyun Island, giving the Iran-aligned group full control of the Bab el-Mandeb shipping strait, an AFP count of military and medical sources puts deaths since the offensive began at roughly 500, mostly combatants, with the UN estimating about 46,000 people displaced. Casualty figures are still being reconciled across reports and should be treated as developing rather than final.

Sources: Al Jazeera (AFP), Euronews

Africa

Congo's Ebola outbreak — already the fastest-growing on record — spreads to a 7th province

Health officials confirmed the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola has reached Sud-Ubangi in northwestern Congo, the first western province hit in an outbreak that began in the east and has now infected 6,942 people and killed 3,349, officials said. The case traced to a man who traveled from the outbreak's epicenter in Ituri province through Rwanda, Uganda, and three other Congolese provinces before falling ill and dying in Sud-Ubangi.

Sources: PBS News (AP), U.S. News (AP)

Algeria severs diplomatic ties with the UAE

Algeria cut diplomatic relations with the United Arab Emirates on Thursday, giving its ambassador 48 hours to leave the country after accusing Abu Dhabi of "provocative or hostile" actions. The rupture caps years of rising tension over the UAE's support for Morocco's claim to Western Sahara, its normalization with Israel, and its growing influence in the Sahel, which Algeria treats as its own security perimeter; the UAE's foreign ministry called the break regrettable and said it hopes it is temporary.

Sources: allAfrica.com, Al Jazeera

Asia

Nepal's flood death toll climbs to 1,386, with over 5,000 still missing

The confirmed death toll from the August 26 flash floods along the Nepal-Tibet border rose to 1,386 as of Saturday, with 5,130 people still listed missing, Nepali officials said. China separately reports 43 dead and 519 missing in the Tibet Autonomous Region; floodwaters from the glacier-collapse disaster carried some victims' remains as far as 240 kilometers into India.

Sources: ABC News, Al Jazeera

North Korea fires ballistic missiles a day after US-South Korea-Japan drills wrapped up

North Korea launched multiple short-range ballistic missiles from the Wonsan area Saturday, which flew roughly 250 kilometers before South Korea's military said it was analyzing their exact specifications. The launch came a day after the three allied militaries concluded joint exercises Pyongyang has called a security threat; U.S. Pacific Command said the launches pose no immediate threat to the U.S. or its allies.

Sources: ABC News (AP), Washington Times
