Americas

Maduro's wife asks New York judge for bail, citing heart condition

Cilia Flores, the wife of deposed Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro, appeared before a federal judge in Manhattan on Thursday to seek bail so she can undergo cardiac treatment outside the Brooklyn jail where she has been held since her and Maduro's capture in January. Prosecutors opposed her release, arguing she has received adequate care in detention and would pose an "extreme" flight risk if freed; both she and Maduro have pleaded not guilty to narco-terrorism and drug-trafficking charges, with trial set for June 2027.

Sources: Internazionale (Reuters), El Tiempo

Europe

Death of evicted Madrid pensioner sets off call for November general strike

Maricarmen Abascal, the 87-year-old pensioner whose eviction from the Madrid apartment she had lived in for roughly 70 years triggered nationwide housing protests, died Wednesday, the capital's tenants' union said. Thousands marched through Madrid hours after her death was announced, and trade unions have called a nationwide general strike over housing for November 11, adding to the pressure on Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez ahead of the snap election he called for November 29.

Sources: France 24 (AFP), The Irish Times

Belgium's Liège rocked by a week of violent student protests

Riot police used tear gas and water cannon in the Belgian city of Liège this week as student protests over overcrowded classrooms and education budget cuts turned violent, with more than 100 people detained since the unrest began on October 1. Protesters set fire to bins and scooters and smashed windows at a local school on Tuesday; Liège's mayor, Willy Demeyer, said he hoped for dialogue, while authorities have ordered early business closures and train station shutdowns to avoid further clashes in the city center.

Sources: Al Jazeera, The Brussels Times

Middle East

Houthi attacks hit two Saudi airports as Yemen's war escalates

Saudi Arabia's civil aviation authority said Houthi missile and drone attacks struck airports in Jazan and Najran this week, injuring three people and causing material damage, as Iran-aligned Houthi forces step up strikes on the kingdom. The attacks came as Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces press a major offensive to retake Houthi-held territory, including parts of the Red Sea coast, a campaign both sides describe as intensifying since late September.

Sources: TimesLive (Reuters), News4Jax (AP)

Africa

Eritrean troops confirmed inside Ethiopia's Tigray region

Witnesses told Reuters and AFP that Eritrean soldiers have been seen driving through the northern Ethiopian town of Adigrat, with some headed toward the Tigrayan capital, Mekelle, days after Ethiopia and Eritrea severed diplomatic ties. A local official said the troops had been there for days and residents "are very concerned"; Eritrea has dismissed the reports as an Ethiopian pretext for more war, while fighting between the Tigray People's Liberation Front and federal forces, which broke out on September 23, has raised fears of a return to the 2020-2022 civil war.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Arab News

UNICEF: nearly 2,000 Congolese children orphaned or separated by Ebola

UNICEF said more than 1,900 children in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo have been orphaned or separated from their caregivers in the country's Ebola outbreak, the deadliest on record, with most cases identified in Ituri and North Kivu provinces. The agency said children account for more than a quarter of confirmed cases but roughly a third of deaths, and it is seeking funding for emergency nurseries near treatment centers as the DRC response remains less than half-funded.

Sources: UNICEF, The Gazette (Nigeria)

Asia

Thailand's flood death toll reaches 60 as millions remain affected

Thailand's government said Wednesday that flooding since mid-September has killed 60 people nationwide, with river levels still rising in parts of the country and flooding ongoing in 27 of the kingdom's 77 provinces. Reuters and AP reported the toll had climbed steadily through the week from an initial count of 31 on October 5, with Prachinburi province hit hardest and more than 3 million people affected as Bangkok works to clear floodwater ahead of IMF-World Bank meetings next week.

Sources: Malay Mail (Reuters), ABC News (AP)
