Americas

Quebec's separatist PQ wins power but falls short of a majority

The separatist Parti Québécois swept Monday's provincial election, surging from three seats in 2022 to roughly 59 and winning a minority government as Premier-designate Paul St-Pierre Plamondon's party routed the governing Coalition Avenir Québec, projected to win zero seats. St-Pierre Plamondon has pledged to hold a third independence referendum, though not before Donald Trump leaves the White House in 2029, and his short-of-majority result means he will need other parties' support to govern. Opinion polling has consistently shown a majority of Quebecers oppose independence.

Sources: CBC News, CTV News (The Canadian Press)

Bolivia activates regional manhunt for fugitive ex-president Morales

Bolivia's government said Monday it has begun seeking international cooperation to capture former president Evo Morales, who has evaded two outstanding arrest warrants — one accusing him of terrorism and armed uprising over road blockades that paralyzed the country in May and June, the other from a 2024 human-trafficking case. Presidency Minister Fernando Aramayo said a "regional collaboration" was being activated but did not name which countries or agencies would take part, or set a timeline. Morales, who has been sheltering in his coca-growing stronghold of Chapare since late 2024, has threatened new protests starting October 13.

Sources: The Rio Times, La Razón (Bolivia)

Europe

Russia vows more strikes on Kyiv as US pushes new peace talks

Russia said it would intensify strikes on Kyiv and other Ukrainian cities after a weekend drone attack damaged a key bridge in the capital, framing the escalation as retaliation for Ukrainian strikes on Russian oil refineries. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Washington has proposed fresh trilateral peace talks before the end of October and that Ukraine responded positively, even as strikes on Kharkiv and Zaporizhzhia this week left casualties and damaged apartment buildings.

Sources: Al Jazeera, CNBC

France's student protests swell to 86,000 as unrest enters a third week

An estimated 86,000 people demonstrated across France on Tuesday, the Interior Ministry said, as a three-week-old student movement over crumbling schools and teacher shortages merged with a public-sector strike against the government's budget plan. Hundreds of schools shut for the day, and more than 5,000 people — mostly minors with no prior record — have been taken into police custody since protests began in late September, with upwards of 600 police officers injured in clashes nationwide.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Euronews

Germany arrests former spy chief on suspicion of treason

German federal prosecutors arrested August Hanning, 80, who led the BND foreign intelligence service from 1998 to 2005, on suspicion he continued passing classified material to a foreign power for years after leaving the post. A former chief of staff who served under Hanning and later under successive BND presidents until March was also arrested; prosecutors did not say which country the two men are suspected of spying for.

Sources: NPR, ABC News (AP)

Middle East

Iran keeps Strait of Hormuz shut as US weighs new strikes

Iran's blockade of the Strait of Hormuz remained in place this week, with Tehran insisting it will not reopen the waterway until Washington meets seven conditions tied to a June memorandum, even as Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi received US feedback on a seven-day trust-building plan through Qatari mediators, Reuters reported. President Trump has said Iran will be "choked out or bombed" if it does not abandon its nuclear program, and the US is sending a third aircraft-carrier strike group to the region as both sides remain deadlocked over how any deal would be sequenced.

Sources: Reuters (via Investing.com), Al Jazeera

Yemeni government forces claim key Red Sea gains against Houthis

Yemen's internationally recognized government said its forces, backed by intensified Saudi-led coalition airstrikes, have seized the Red Sea port of Mokha and areas near the Bab al-Mandeb strait in a widening offensive aimed at retaking all Houthi-held territory. The territorial claims have not been independently verified. The push follows last week's launch of the government campaign and comes after the Iran-aligned Houthis' own rapid advance in September along roughly 150 kilometers of Red Sea coastline.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Yahoo News (AP)

Africa

Nigerian Air Force crash kills all 32 aboard, including air chief

A Nigerian Air Force ATR-42 crashed into a swamp near a naval base in Igbokoda, Ondo State, on Monday while flying a routine route from Benin City to Lagos, killing all 32 people aboard, President Bola Tinubu said. Among the dead was Air Marshal Sunday Kelvin Aneke, the air force's most senior officer; Tinubu ordered an immediate investigation and declared three days of national mourning with flags flown at half-mast.

Sources: France 24 (AFP), Vanguard

Ethiopia's Abiy sworn in for second term as Tigray war reignites

Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed took the oath of office for a second five-year term Monday, opening a new parliamentary term dominated by his Prosperity Party after June's general election, as his government faces renewed fighting in Tigray and a ruptured relationship with Eritrea. The inauguration came as federal-aligned forces consolidated control of the Tigrayan capital Mekelle after rebel forces withdrew; Abiy called on Ethiopians to "put the interests of the nation first."

Sources: Al Jazeera, The East African

Asia

China tops medal table as Asian Games close in Japan

The 2026 Asian Games closed Sunday in Nagoya and Aichi, Japan, with China topping the medal table for a twelfth consecutive edition of the continental Games, winning 169 of the 341 medals it collected. Host Japan finished second and South Korea third, as organizers acknowledged logistical problems with accommodation and transport that drew complaints through the 16-day event.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Arab News

Koreas' DMZ dispute deepens as Kim Yo Jong rejects apology demand

South Korean and UN Command investigators concluded this week that North Korean troops crossed the Military Demarcation Line and buried the land mines that wounded three South Korean soldiers in a September 21 blast, prompting Seoul to demand an apology and weigh further steps. Kim Yo Jong, sister of North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, rejected the findings as a "self-staged farce" and the apology demand as "utterly pathetic," ruling out any talks on the matter.

Sources: Al Jazeera, France 24 (AFP)
