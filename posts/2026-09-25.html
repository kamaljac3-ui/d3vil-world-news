Americas

Colombia severs diplomatic relations with Iran, citing terror links and nuclear standoff

Colombia's Foreign Ministry announced it has cut diplomatic relations with Iran, a rupture that took effect September 19 and ends more than five decades of formal ties. President Abelardo de la Espriella's government, in office since August, cited Iran's alleged links to militant groups, the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz and Tehran's refusal to let UN inspectors access its nuclear sites, and it designated Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps a terrorist organization while keeping consular relations open. The move follows Bogotá's restoration of ties with Israel as the new government aligns more closely with Washington.

Sources: Infobae (wire), Rio Times

Europe

Russian drone kills 14-year-old boy in Kyiv apartment strike

A Russian drone slammed into a 16-story residential building in Kyiv's central Pechersk district before dawn Friday, killing a 14-year-old boy and wounding at least seven other people. President Volodymyr Zelensky said rescue crews worked through the wreckage for hours after the strike, one of the deadliest to hit the Ukrainian capital's downtown core in recent weeks.

Sources: ABC (Australia), US News (AP)

Russian helicopter briefly crosses into Polish airspace, NATO jets scrambled

A Russian military Mi-8 helicopter flying out of the Kaliningrad exclave entered Polish airspace for about 42 seconds this week, prompting Poland to scramble fighter jets, its military said. Polish officials called it the latest in a string of Russian incursions testing NATO's eastern air defenses, a day after a separate intercept of a Russian reconnaissance plane over the Baltic Sea.

Sources: US News (AP), Euronews

Middle East

Yemen fighting kills more than 150 in a day as Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan plan defense talks

Clashes across multiple Yemeni front lines killed at least 150 Houthi and government-aligned fighters in the 24 hours before Friday, according to an AFP tally, as the Houthi offensive toward the Bab al-Mandeb strait continues. Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan announced plans for urgent talks between their military chiefs under a mutual defense pact, and France said it would send troops, radar and air-defense systems to help protect the Saudi port of Yanbu from Houthi attack.

Sources: Al Jazeera (AFP), Euronews

Turkey agrees to gradually hand over military base to Iraq

Turkey and Iraq agreed to a gradual, timetable-free handover of Turkey's Bashiqa-Zelikan camp in northern Iraq to the Iraqi government, following talks between Presidents Recep Tayyip Erdogan and Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly. Iraq separately pledged to clear armed groups Turkey considers security threats from the Sinjar area, after a PKK-linked Yazidi militia there agreed earlier this month to disarm.

Sources: ABC News (AP), US News (AP)

Africa

Ethiopian army battles Tigray rebels near Afar border as war fears grow

Ethiopian federal forces fought overnight to repel a Tigrayan rebel assault near the town of Abala in the Afar region, days after Tigray forces seized three airports and declared a return to full-scale war. Ethiopia's army said it killed 272 Tigrayan fighters in fighting in neighboring Amhara, a figure that could not be independently verified, as an internet blackout was reported across parts of the country's north.

Sources: Al Jazeera, NPR

Nigerian communities dispute kidnapping toll as governor downplays hundreds missing

Communities in Nigeria's Niger state said this week that 547 people were abducted in last month's mass kidnapping from a mosque in Dikera and nearby villages, directly contradicting Governor Mohammed Umaru Bago's claim that only a "few dozen" were taken. The dispute has fueled protests in Borgu district, where residents accuse the state government of minimizing an attack that has left hundreds still unaccounted for a month later.

Sources: US News (AP), Gulf News

Asia

Typhoon Dujuan leaves at least nine dead in Japan after record rainfall

Typhoon Dujuan dumped record rainfall on Japan's Kanto region this week, triggering floods and landslides that killed at least nine people and left four missing, mostly in Chiba and Kanagawa prefectures. The island of Oshima recorded 832 millimeters of rain in 48 hours, more than double its typical rainfall for all of September, as the storm damaged roughly 500 homes before moving out to sea.

Sources: The Japan Times, Al Jazeera
