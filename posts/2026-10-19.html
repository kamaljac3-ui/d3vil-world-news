Americas

Albertans vote on first step toward independence referendum

Albertans cast ballots Monday in a non-binding referendum asking whether the province should begin the constitutionally required legal process toward a future binding vote on leaving Canada, with results expected in the early hours of Tuesday. Premier Danielle Smith, who has said she personally wants Alberta to remain in Canada, cast her own vote to stay and urged support on nine other ballot questions covering immigration and voter identification rules. Pre-vote polling suggested roughly 70% of Albertans favored remaining in Canada, a margin separatist groups have vowed to keep contesting regardless of Monday's outcome.

Sources: CTV News (Canadian Press), CP24 (Canadian Press)

Maduro pleads not guilty to new US torture charges

Nicolás Maduro and his wife, Cilia Flores, pleaded not guilty Thursday in a Manhattan federal court to new charges accusing the former Venezuelan leader of directing the torture of opposition figures, military personnel and American citizens. A judge cut off Maduro's statement rejecting the indictment "in its entirety" and entered the not-guilty plea on his behalf; both defendants face a mandatory minimum of 50 years in prison if convicted and have argued for the case's dismissal on sovereign-immunity grounds. The judge postponed the trial's start to June 2027 to accommodate the new charges.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), Reuters (via U.S. News)

Europe

Macron decries "shame" as French unrest toll rises to nearly 2,400 arrests

President Emmanuel Macron said "we all feel anger and shame" in a national address Sunday after a weekend of nationwide protests over the cost of living and school conditions turned violent, with authorities reporting nearly 2,400 people arrested and close to 1,000 police, gendarmes and firefighters injured. Authorities counted 550,000 marchers nationwide, while unions put the figure near 800,000; fringe groups set fire to the town hall in Limoges and clashed with police in several cities. Macron stopped short of declaring a state of emergency, instead leaving local officials free to ban specific gatherings.

Sources: Reuters (via Investing.com), Al Jazeera

Albania's capital enters sixth day of anti-government unrest

Protests against Prime Minister Edi Rama entered a sixth consecutive day in Tirana on Sunday, with police firing tear gas, water cannon and rubber bullets at demonstrators demanding his resignation over university conditions, alleged corruption and a coastal resort deal. Albania's health ministry said 28 people, including 19 police officers, were injured in Sunday's clashes alone, bringing the total hurt since the unrest began to 272. Rama, in power since 2013, told Reuters he would not resign and cast the protests as an attempt to undermine Albania's path toward the European Union.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Euronews

Middle East

Houthi missile strike on Yemeni army camp kills at least 18 soldiers

Iran-aligned Houthi rebels fired ballistic missiles at a government military training camp in Yemen's southern Shabwa province on Monday, wounding roughly two dozen troops and killing at least 18, according to Yemeni government and medical sources cited by Reuters; two Yemeni military officials told AP the toll had reached 20. Officials described it as one of the deadliest attacks in a renewed surge of fighting between the Houthis and the Saudi-backed government, and cautioned the final death toll was still being confirmed. A Saudi-led coalition separately said it intercepted another missile fired by the rebels.

Sources: Reuters (via The National), AP (via The Hill)

Lebanon to press Israel-attack grievances at US-hosted military talks

A Lebanese military delegation is due in Tampa, Florida, on October 20 for trilateral talks with American and Israeli officials, where it plans to demand an end to continued Israeli strikes in southern Lebanon, the Lebanese presidency said. The meeting follows a US-brokered arrangement under which Beirut agreed to disarm Hezbollah, even as Israel has kept striking targets in the south; Lebanon also wants the mandate of international peacekeepers extended past its scheduled 2027 end date. The Arab League said in a statement that diplomatic efforts around Lebanon are expected to intensify in the coming days.

Sources: Reuters (via Internazionale), Al-Monitor

Africa

Congo's Ebola outbreak, already the deadliest on record there, keeps spreading

The Democratic Republic of Congo's Ebola outbreak has grown to roughly 9,400 confirmed cases and more than 4,500 deaths since it was declared in May, making it the largest and deadliest Ebola epidemic in the country's history, health ministry figures show. The outbreak, caused by the rare Bundibugyo strain, has now spread into an eighth province, while neighboring Kenya has confirmed two cases linked to a Kenyan citizen who had been living in the DRC and later died there.

Sources: Reuters (via Kuwait Times), CDC situation update (via Contagion Live)

Suspected Islamic State-linked militants kill 20 at Nigerian military base

Militants believed to be linked to the Islamic State group attacked a Nigerian military base in Borno State, killing at least 20 people, including 15 soldiers and five members of a local civilian defense force, a military official told AFP. The base was reportedly destroyed in the assault, forcing surviving troops to withdraw toward the nearby town of Monguno. Neither the death toll nor the attackers' precise affiliation has yet been independently confirmed by a second source.

Sources: AFP (via AzerNews)

Asia

India's youth-led protest movement defies crackdown over voter-roll purge

Indian police detained the founder of the youth-led "Cockroach" protest movement and dozens of supporters in New Delhi last week amid continuing demonstrations against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar over a disputed revision of voter rolls that removed roughly 130 million names. The Election Commission denies favoring any party, but the crackdown, which has included mass detentions and mobile internet suspensions in parts of the capital, has drawn criticism from the United Nations human rights office. India's Supreme Court has said restrictions on assembly and movement must comply with constitutional limits.

Sources: Al Jazeera, NPR

US holds second round of secret talks with Myanmar's military rulers

The United States and Myanmar's military-backed government held a second round of talks within a month, this time in Tokyo, Reuters reported, marking a notable shift after years of Washington shunning the generals who seized power in 2021. US Assistant Secretary of State Michael Vance again led the American delegation; Reuters could not establish who represented Myanmar, and the State Department would not comment on what was discussed. An earlier round in Naypyitaw in September focused on freeing an American businessman and shutting down scam centers, and could open the door to broader talks on Myanmar's rare-earth mineral deposits.

Sources: Reuters (via Internazionale), The Irrawaddy
