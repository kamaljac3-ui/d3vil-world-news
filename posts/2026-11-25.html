Americas

Colombian special forces free all hostages after day-long siege at a Cartagena supermarket

Armed men who botched a robbery of a cash-in-transit van took dozens of hostages inside an Éxito supermarket in Cartagena on November 24, triggering a standoff that briefly suspended flights at the city's airport, AFP reported. Colombian police and soldiers stormed the store early on November 25, freeing all remaining hostages, killing one captor and detaining four others, President Abelardo de la Espriella said; the siege came a day after a car bomb wounded 23 people outside the Meta governor's office in Villavicencio, part of a wider wave of violence Colombia's government blames on FARC dissident factions.

Sources: AFP (via Yahoo News), Al Jazeera

Deadly clashes continue in Sinaloa weeks after capture of cartel boss "Chapo Isidro"

A Mexican federal judge ordered Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, the Sinaloa Cartel figure known as "Chapo Isidro," held in pretrial detention on November 23 and transferred him to the Altiplano maximum-security prison, three days after a raid captured him in Guasave, CBS News reported. Mexico's government, which coordinated the operation with US intelligence, says clashes between security forces and cartel gunmen since the raid have killed roughly 20 people, including four soldiers; Meza Flores' formal arraignment hearing resumes November 28, and Mexico's attorney general says he will face trial domestically before any US extradition request is considered.

Sources: AP (via CBS News), Mexico News Daily

Europe

EU unveils 22nd sanctions package targeting Russian banks and shadow-fleet tankers

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen proposed a 22nd package of sanctions against Russia on November 23, which would cut roughly 60 more Russian banks off the SWIFT payment system, list more than 70 additional shadow-fleet tankers and bar Russian athletes from competing under their flag at EU sporting events, wire reporting said. The package, which still needs unanimous approval from all 27 member states, would also extend an energy-services ban to Russian refineries and electricity infrastructure and create a task force targeting crypto and third-country sanctions evasion.

Sources: Reuters (via Yahoo News), European External Action Service

Russian strike on Zaporizhzhia kills two, wounds dozens including children

A Russian air strike hit residential buildings in Zaporizhzhia on November 24, killing two people and wounding around 40 others, including an infant and several children, regional military administration head Mykhailo Semikin said, according to wire reporting. The strike destroyed a private house and partially collapsed a five-story apartment building, and came a day after Ukraine said its naval drones sank a Russian warship at a Libyan port, an operation Kyiv has publicly defended amid questions from Libya's UN mission about drawing the country into the wider war.

Sources: AP (via Washington Post), Al Jazeera

Middle East

Houthi fighters advance within 60km of Aden as Yemen's government fortifies defenses

Yemen's army said Houthi fighters had massed roughly 60 kilometers from Aden, the seat of the internationally recognized government, and were "now ready to attack" as of November 24, AP reported. Government forces that withdrew from the western coast near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait have dug in on a defensive line north and west of the city; presidential council head Rashad al-Alimi said the government would remain in Aden and trusted its forces to repel the advance, which follows an October 15 Houthi assault on the presidential palace that killed four people.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), France 24

Houthi drone strike wounds 11 at Riyadh's tallest tower, second attack on it in a week

A Houthi drone attack wounded 11 people, including Saudi, Indian, Nepali and Egyptian nationals, at the Riyadh tower housing Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund on November 24, Reuters reported, the second strike on the building in less than a week after a November 19 missile attack caused damage but no injuries. The Saudi-led coalition, which backs Yemen's government against the Houthis, called it a "cowardly terrorist attack" and separately struck Houthi targets in the Red Sea port of Hodeidah; most international flights to Riyadh were cancelled and the AFC Asian Cup was postponed as a result of the escalation.

Sources: Reuters (via Haaretz), Al Jazeera

Africa

Zambia's runner-up presidential candidate committed to trial on treason charges

Zambian opposition leader Brian Mundubile and his running mate Makebi Zulu were formally indicted and committed to the High Court to stand trial on treason charges on November 23, alongside about a dozen other co-accused, Reuters-sourced reporting said. Prosecutors allege the group, which finished second in the August 13 election won by President Hakainde Hichilema, recruited current and retired security personnel between April and August and funded military training abroad as part of a plot to overthrow the government; Mundubile denies wrongdoing and calls the case politically motivated, and his lawyer says no trial date has been set.

Sources: Africanews (Reuters), allAfrica.com

Dozens killed in reprisal attacks on Fulani communities in Nigeria's Niger State

Vigilante groups killed dozens of people in reprisal attacks on Fulani communities around Wawa and Gada-Oli in Nigeria's Niger State over the November 21-22 weekend, Reuters and AP reported, after gunmen killed the head of nearby Lumman-Ba'are village and four others. Fulani community groups put the death toll as high as 64, while Amnesty International said it had confirmed seven deaths and an unknown number of abductions; police have not named any suspects or given an official casualty figure, and Reuters said it could not independently verify either count.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), Reuters (via SRN News)

Asia

Indonesia charges former police chief with treason over wiretapping of the president

Indonesian prosecutors filed treason charges on November 23 against former national police chief Listyo Sigit Prabowo and five others, including a civilian and current and former officers, over an alleged illegal wiretap of President Prabowo Subianto's phone, AFP reported. Police say the group conspired to weaken public trust in the government, allegedly to keep Listyo in his post; he was dismissed as presidential chief of staff the day after his arrest, and investigators say the wiretapping operation also targeted officials, journalists and businesspeople. Treason against the president carries a maximum penalty of death by firing squad under Indonesian law.

Sources: AFP (via New Straits Times), Japan Times

South Korea scrambles jets as Russian bombers again skirt its air defense zone

South Korea briefly issued an air-raid alert on Ulleung Island on November 24 after two Russian Tu-160 nuclear-capable bombers entered its air defense identification zone and came within about one kilometer of the island, Yonhap reported. Seoul's military scrambled F-15K fighters and lodged a protest, calling it the third unauthorized entry by Russian aircraft in about a week; Russia's defense ministry said the flight was "planned" and did not violate South Korean airspace or any international rule, while Seoul's government called the repeated incursions unacceptable.

Sources: Yonhap (via UPI), Korea JoongAng Daily
