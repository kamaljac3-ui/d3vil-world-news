Americas

Paraguay's Senate impeaches Peña instead of accepting his resignation, and police detain him

A day after President Santiago Peña announced his resignation over the Ueno Bank collapse, Paraguay's Senate voted 44-0 to impeach and remove him from office for misconduct rather than accept the resignation, and prosecutors then had police detain him at his Asunción residence on suspicion of breach of trust tied to the bank's failure, EFE reported. Vice President Pedro Alliana was sworn in as president, and prosecutors also ordered the detention of the central bank's former president, Carlos Carvallo; Peña denies wrongdoing and was due to give his first statement to prosecutors. The Senate route mattered constitutionally: a resignation would have let Peña become a senator for life, while impeachment does not.

Sources: AP (via NPR), EFE (via Infobae)

US sanctions target Cuba's oil-shipping network and Chinese suppliers to its military

Secretary of State Marco Rubio announced sanctions Wednesday on ten targets tied to Cuba, including a crude-oil shipping network, state-owned import and technical-support entities, and China-based suppliers to the Cuban military, and identified five vessels as blocked property, Reuters reported. The Treasury Department separately issued a determination authorizing sanctions on foreign persons operating in Cuba's electronics, manufacturing, marine and technology sectors; the State Department said existing humanitarian trade authorizations for food and medicine remain in place. China's government rejected the sanctions, accusing Washington of trying to undermine China-Cuba economic ties.

Sources: Reuters (via U.S. News), U.S. State Department

Europe

Death toll from Russia's strike on a Ukrainian aid site climbs past 50

Ukrainian officials said the death toll from a November 2 Russian bombing of a former school used as a humanitarian aid distribution point in Komyshuvakha, Zaporizhzhia region, has risen to roughly 55 as rescuers kept recovering bodies five days into the search, up from the 31-37 figures reported earlier in the week. Reuters reported at least 37 dead and dozens missing as of November 4, including two rescue workers and four police officers killed when a second wave of bombs struck first responders; President Zelenskyy called it one of the deadliest attacks of the war. The final toll has not stabilized, and Russia's defense ministry says it targeted Ukrainian military units there, a claim that has not been independently confirmed.

Sources: AP/Reuters (via Yahoo News), France 24

Middle East

Dozens killed in disputed strikes on a market in Yemen's Taiz province

At least 30 people were killed and eight wounded Friday when strikes hit a crowded market in the al-Waziyah district of Yemen's Taiz province, with Houthi officials blaming Saudi-led coalition warplanes and Yemen's internationally recognized government denying any air raid occurred and instead blaming indiscriminate Houthi missile fire, the AP reported. Neither side's account has been independently verified, and the incident adds to a string of disputed market strikes in the area, including one reported on November 2. Responsibility for the strike remains disputed and has not been independently confirmed.

Sources: AP (via Yahoo News), France 24

Africa

Somalia's top court rules the president's term has expired, orders elections within 90 days

Somalia's Supreme Court ruled Thursday that President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud's four-year mandate expired on May 15 and that both houses of parliament's terms expired in April, rejecting a March constitutional amendment that had extended the timeline toward elections planned for 2027, the AP reported. The court ordered political stakeholders to agree on an electoral framework within 30 days and hold elections within 90, and separately voided Mohamud's dismissal of the chief justice a day earlier as "null and void"; the presidency had not responded to the ruling as of Friday, and the government has defended the extended timetable as needed to shift to universal suffrage.

Sources: AP (via The Washington Post), allAfrica

Nigerian court voids Atiku Abubakar's 2027 presidential nomination

A Federal High Court in Abuja ruled Thursday that opposition leader Atiku Abubakar's nomination as the ADC's 2027 presidential candidate was invalid, also setting aside the party leadership that produced his nomination, after a suit from a former ADC deputy national chairman who argued that leadership was improperly installed, AFP reported. The ADC has filed an appeal and asked the electoral commission not to act while it is pending, and Atiku said he remains committed to the race; the party has accused the governing APC of involvement in the lawsuit, which APC-aligned figures deny.

Sources: AFP (via Africanews), Premium Times

Asia

Indonesia deploys over 20,000 troops as former president Jokowi appears in court

Indonesian authorities brought more than 20,000 troops and police into Jakarta, with nearly 6,800 deployed around the East Jakarta District Court Thursday for a defamation hearing involving former President Joko Widodo over allegations he used a fake university diploma before his 2014 run for office, which he denies, the AP reported. The heavy security reflects broader tension from a separate wiretapping scandal in which three senior generals, including a former national police chief under Widodo, have been detained; Widodo has not been implicated in that case, and most of the deployed troops were held in reserve rather than visibly patrolling.

Sources: AP (via ABC News), The Diplomat

New Zealand's governing coalition holds a razor-thin lead in a knife-edge election

New Zealand's governing National-led coalition held a narrow lead over the Labour-led opposition as vote counting continued Saturday, with National and Labour effectively tied in party-vote share and roughly half a million special votes still to be counted, Reuters reported. The ruling three-party coalition appeared positioned for a majority of just one seat on preliminary counting, but neither Prime Minister Christopher Luxon nor Labour leader Chris Hipkins declared the result settled; final results are not expected until November 27.

Sources: Reuters (via Al Jazeera), RNZ
